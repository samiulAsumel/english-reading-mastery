## Welcome

If you are reading this, you may know very little English — or none at
all. That is completely fine. This lesson does not teach English yet. It
teaches you how this course works, so that when Lesson 1 begins, nothing
about the *page itself* is confusing. Only the English will be new.

Read this lesson once, slowly. There is nothing to memorise and no
vocabulary to learn yet — just five minutes of looking around before you
start.

## What This Course Actually Teaches

::: concept title="Direct understanding, not translation"
Most courses teach English like this:

**English word → your language → meaning**

Every sentence has to travel through a translation step before it means
anything to you. That step is slow, and it never fully disappears — you
stay dependent on it forever.

This course trains a different, faster path:

**English words → English structure → meaning, directly**

You will still be given word meanings when a word is genuinely new (see
"Vocabulary Cards" below), but the *goal* of every lesson is to shrink
that translation step a little more, until reading English feels like
reading, not decoding.
:::

That is the whole idea behind the name of this course. Everything else —
levels, lessons, practice questions — exists only to build that skill in
small, ordered steps.

## How the Course Is Organised

The course is divided into levels, and each level is divided into
modules, and each module contains lessons. You do not need to think about
this structure while you study — just know that it exists, in case you
ever want to see the whole map.

::: framework title="From zero to advanced reading"
- Level 0 — Orientation (this lesson)
- Level 1 — Foundation: words, phrases, and the shape of a sentence
- Level 2 — Elementary: sentences joined into paragraphs
- Level 3 — Intermediate: following an idea across several sentences
- Level 4 — Upper Intermediate: arguments, evidence, academic English
- Level 5 to Level 8 — advanced, technical, and critical reading
:::

You are at the very start of that path. Lesson 1 begins Level 1.

## How a Lesson Page Is Built

Every lesson page — including this one — is built from the same set of
parts, always in the same places. Learn these once, and you will
recognise them instantly for the rest of the course.

::: example title="At the top of every lesson"
**Learning Objectives box** — a short list, right under the title,
telling you exactly what you should be able to do by the end. Read it
first; it tells you what to pay attention to.

**Difficulty and time badges** — next to the title, showing roughly how
long the lesson takes and how difficult it is, so you can plan when to
study it.
:::

::: example title="Coloured boxes inside the lesson"
As you read, you will meet several kinds of highlighted boxes, each with
its own colour and its own job:

**Core Concept** — the main idea of the section, explained plainly before
any detail.

**Example** — a real sentence showing the idea in action.

**Important** — something you must not skim past; it usually prevents a
common, serious mistake.

**Common Mistake** — a specific error learners often make, and how to
avoid it.

**Note** — a smaller strategic tip you can apply while reading.

**Golden Rule** — one short, memorable sentence near the end of the
lesson that captures its most important rule. If you remember nothing
else from a lesson, remember its Golden Rule.

Each box also has a small icon next to its title. The icon and the colour
always match the box's job, so after a lesson or two you will recognise
the type before you even read the heading.
:::

::: example title="Vocabulary Cards"
When a lesson introduces a genuinely new word, it appears in its own
card: the word, its part of speech, a plain-English meaning, and one
short gloss in your language for that single word only — never a full
translated sentence. This course only uses your language to confirm the
meaning of one word at a time; every explanation around it stays in
English, because that is the skill you are here to build.
:::

::: example title="At the bottom of every lesson"
**Practice section** — questions to test what you just learned, always
before you move on.

**Mark as complete button** — click it once you have genuinely finished
the lesson and its practice. This updates your personal progress, stored
only on your own device.

**Previous / Next navigation** — two large links for moving to the
lesson before or after this one, so you never have to search for where
you left off.
:::

## Finding Your Way Around the Site

::: note title="Five pages worth knowing"
**Lessons** — every lesson, in order, with your completion marked.

**Course** — the full curriculum map: every level and every module.

**Vocabulary** — every word taught so far across every lesson, collected
in one searchable list.

**Skills** — a list of specific reading skills (like "Sentence
Structure" or "Inference"), showing which lessons build each one.

**Progress** — your personal dashboard: lessons completed, your current
streak, and a shortcut back to wherever you left off.

You can reach any of these from the menu at the top of the page. On a
narrow screen, tap the menu icon in the top corner to open the same list.
:::

::: note title="Two more useful controls"
**Search** — click the search box (or press Ctrl/Cmd+K) at any time to
jump straight to a lesson by its title, a concept, or a vocabulary word,
instead of scrolling through the lesson list.

**Light / dark mode** — the small icon in the top-right corner switches
between a light and a dark version of every page. Use whichever is more
comfortable for your eyes.
:::

## Your Study Routine, Every Single Lesson

::: framework title="The four-step process for every lesson"
- Read the whole lesson once, without stopping to look anything up
- Read it a second time, slowly, and notice anything that felt unclear
- Work through every practice question yourself, honestly, before checking anything
- Explain the lesson's main idea back to yourself, in your own words
:::

Follow this same routine on Lesson 1, and on every lesson after it. The
structure never changes — only the English inside it gets more advanced.

::: warning title="Do not translate whole sentences with a tool"
It is fine to look up the meaning of a single unfamiliar word. It is not
fine to run a whole sentence or paragraph through a translation tool and
read the result instead of the English. That habit feels helpful today
and quietly keeps you dependent on it forever — exactly the trap this
course is built to help you escape. Struggling briefly with real English
is the point, not a problem to avoid.
:::

::: important title="Mistakes are expected, and useful"
You will get practice questions wrong, especially early on. That is not
a failure — it is information. A wrong answer shows exactly which part
of the structure did not click yet, which is far more useful to know now
than to discover later. Answer every question yourself first, even when
you are unsure, rather than skipping it.
:::

## Lesson Summary

This course trains direct understanding of English, not translation, one
small step at a time. Every lesson page uses the same building blocks —
an objectives box, coloured concept boxes, vocabulary cards, a golden
rule, and a practice section — so once you recognise them here, you will
recognise them everywhere. Your job on every lesson is the same four-step
routine: read once, read again slowly, do the practice honestly, then
explain the idea back to yourself.

::: golden
The page never changes shape — only the English inside it grows. Learn the shape once, here, and every lesson after this becomes one thing lighter to think about.
:::
