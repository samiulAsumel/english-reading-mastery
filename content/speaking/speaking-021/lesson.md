## Introduction

[Lesson 21](/lessons/21/) taught you to hedge an inference correctly on
the page, where you have time to choose the word. Saying a hedged
inference out loud, live, under a timer, is a different skill — the
hedge word has to come out naturally, in the flow of the sentence, with
no pause to search for it.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The lights are off and the gate is locked. The office is probably
closed for the day.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice how *probably*
gets almost the same stress as *closed* — in fluent English, the hedge
word is never thrown away or mumbled, because it's doing real
grammatical work.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="8s"
Scenario: the delivery truck is an hour late, and the driver isn't
answering calls. When the timer starts, say one sentence giving a
reasonable, hedged inference about why — out loud, immediately, no
planning pause.
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "The truck may be stuck in
traffic" and "There's probably been some kind of delay on the road" are
both natural. What matters is a real hedge word landing naturally in
the sentence, spoken immediately, without stating the cause as a
certain fact.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="probably" pos="adverb" meaning="likely, though not certain — সম্ভবত" example="The truck is probably stuck in traffic; it's rarely this late without a reason."
:::

::: vocabulary word="might" pos="modal verb" meaning="a weaker possibility than may or could — হয়তো (দুর্বল সম্ভাবনা)" example="The driver might have taken a different route."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your sentence include a genuine hedge word, spoken with normal stress?
- Did you speak immediately, with no pause to plan the sentence first?
- Did you avoid stating the cause as a confirmed fact?
:::
