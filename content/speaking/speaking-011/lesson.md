## Introduction

[Lesson 11](/lessons/11/) taught you to find a sentence's backbone and
attach every clause to the noun it belongs to. Saying a relative clause
out loud is harder than writing one — there's no time to plan where the
clause goes, you just have to keep talking and drop it in as the
sentence unfolds.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The driver who called this morning said the shipment would be late.
:::

Listen once without speaking. Notice that "who called this morning"
doesn't interrupt the flow — a fluent speaker says it as one continuous
breath group, not as a separate, bolted-on afterthought.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="10s"
You'll see a person or thing, plus one fact about them. When the timer
starts, say one full sentence describing them using who, which, or that
— e.g. "a colleague / sent the report late" → "My colleague who sent the
report late apologized this morning." Pick your own colleague, place, or
object and fact, and speak immediately.
:::

::: timer seconds="10" label="Describe someone or something now — one sentence, no planning"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say three different sentences, each with a relative clause, then record and compare your pacing."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — what matters is that you produced
one continuous sentence with a relative clause attached to the right
noun, spoken immediately, without a pause before or during the clause.
Example: "The email that arrived this morning explained the delay."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="colleague" pos="noun" meaning="a person you work with — সহকর্মী" example="My colleague who joined last month is already fluent."
:::

::: vocabulary word="apologize" pos="verb" meaning="to say sorry for something — ক্ষমা চাওয়া" example="She apologized for the delay as soon as she called."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak immediately, with no pause to plan the sentence?
- Did the relative clause attach to the right noun, spoken as one continuous sentence?
- Did you avoid repeating the noun inside the clause?
:::
