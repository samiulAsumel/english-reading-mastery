## Introduction

[Lesson 52](/lessons/52/) taught you to check a definition against its
boundary — what it excludes as much as what it includes. Explaining a
concept this precisely out loud, with no time to draft it first, is a
genuine test of whether you actually understand it or just recognize the
word.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
A budget isn't just a list of expenses — it's a plan for how money will be
spent before it's spent. That's different from a report, which just records
what already happened.
:::

Shadow this once, paying attention to how "isn't just" and "that's
different from" get slightly more stress — they're the words doing the
actual defining work.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick a concept from your own field and explain it out loud in three parts:
what it is, what it is not (or what it's often confused with), and one quick
example. Speak as soon as the timer starts — no planning the sentence first.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — is / is not / example, in that order"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your definition, then check whether all three parts — is, is not, example — are actually there."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct concept to choose — what matters is the shape of
the answer. A strong response sounds like: "Caching isn't the same as
storage. Storage is where data lives permanently; caching is keeping a
temporary, faster-to-reach copy of data you'll need again soon. A website
that remembers your login for an hour without asking again is using a
cache, not just storage." Notice the definition, the contrast, and the
example all show up, live, in under thirty seconds.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="anchor" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to make an abstract idea concrete by attaching it to a specific example (সুনির্দিষ্ট উদাহরণ দিয়ে বাঁধা)" collocation="anchor an idea, anchor to, anchor with an example" example="I anchor the abstract idea of 'delay' with the example of last Tuesday's berth clash."
:::

::: vocabulary word="confused with" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="mistaken for something else because the two seem similar (বিভ্রান্ত হওয়া)" collocation="be confused with, easily confused with, often confused with" example="'Delay' is often confused with 'cancellation', but they are different."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state what the concept is, clearly and first?
- Did you also say what it is not, or what it's often confused with?
- Did you give one concrete example without being asked?
- Did you speak immediately, with no rehearsed script?
:::
