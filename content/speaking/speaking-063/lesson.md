## Introduction

[Lesson 63](/lessons/63/) taught you to reconstruct a full
problem-solution chain when reading. This drill asks you to build one
out loud, live, the moment a scenario is given — symptom, root cause,
solution, and an honest trade-off, in one continuous explanation.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Late deliveries weren't caused by traffic. The real root cause was an
outdated routing system. A new algorithm reduced delays by thirty
percent, though it also raised fuel costs.
:::

Notice the pace doesn't slow down for "root cause" or "reduced" — these
are said with the same natural rhythm as the rest of the sentence, not
carefully, word by word, the way a translated sentence often sounds.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given a short problem scenario. When the timer starts, speak:
(1) the root cause, not just the symptom, (2) the solution and a
solution-strength word that matches its real effect, and (3) one honest
trade-off — all in one connected explanation, no planning pause.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Explain the chain now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer names the root
cause explicitly (not the symptom), picks a solution-strength word that
matches the real outcome rather than defaulting to "solved," and states
a trade-off without hesitating or needing a prompt.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="symptom" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a visible sign of a problem, not the problem's underlying cause (লক্ষণ)" collocation="a symptom of, obvious symptom, treat the symptom" example="Long queues are a symptom of the underlying scheduling problem."
:::

::: vocabulary word="trade-off" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a downside accepted in exchange for a benefit (বিনিময়ে ত্যাগ)" collocation="trade-off between, difficult trade-off, accept a trade-off" example="There is a trade-off between speed and thoroughness."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the root cause separately from the symptom, without being asked?
- Did your solution-strength word match the real outcome, not just the intention?
- Did you state a trade-off in the same breath, not as an afterthought?
:::
