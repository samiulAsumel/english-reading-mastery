## Introduction

[Lesson 30](/lessons/30/) trained you to pull data apart from
interpretation on the page. Doing it live is harder — under time
pressure, speakers naturally fuse "what happened" and "what it means"
into one breathless sentence. This drill trains you to give each layer
its own spoken sentence, deliberately.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Sales rose twelve percent after the new layout went in. That's
consistent with the layout helping, but it only covers this one store.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice the full stop after "went in" —
the speaker actually pauses there, marking the boundary between the
fact and the interpretation that follows it.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Think of a real or plausible result you know something about (a change
at work, a personal habit, a small test you tried). When the timer
starts, say the observation as one complete sentence, pause briefly, then
say your interpretation as a separate sentence using "suggests" or "is
consistent with," and name the claim's actual scope.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — observation, pause, interpretation, scope"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your observation and interpretation, then check whether you actually paused between the two instead of running them together."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Container dwell time fell eighteen percent after we started
using the new yard-mapping app. That's consistent with the app helping
drivers find containers faster, but we also added two yard staff that
same month, so it only really covers this one terminal." Check that you
paused after the observation instead of blending it straight into your
interpretation.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="consistent with" pos="phrase" meaning="a cautious spoken phrase for saying evidence fits an interpretation without overclaiming it proves it — সামঞ্জস্যপূর্ণ" example="Saying 'consistent with' instead of 'proves' kept the spoken claim honest."
:::

::: vocabulary word="scope" pos="noun" meaning="who or what a spoken claim actually covers, stated out loud rather than left vague — পরিধি" example="Naming 'this one terminal' out loud kept the claim's scope honest."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you pause between your observation and your interpretation, rather than fusing them?
- Did your interpretation use a support-language phrase matched to how strong your evidence actually is?
- Did you state the claim's real scope out loud, instead of letting it silently widen?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
