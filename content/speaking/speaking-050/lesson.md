## Introduction

[Lesson 50](/lessons/50/) taught you to recognize "Although X, Y does
not necessarily…" instantly while reading. This drill is the spoken
capstone of the module: produce that same three-part structure live, out
loud, straight in English — no Bangla sentence composed first and then
translated.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Although the new terminal reduced average dwell time, this doesn't
necessarily mean overall efficiency improved, since congestion at the
older terminals stayed the same.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice how "although" signals right away
that a claim is coming that will be limited somehow — a fluent speaker
commits to the whole three-part shape before finishing the first clause,
rather than building it piece by piece.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Pick a topic you know well. In 20 seconds, say one "Although…" sentence
out loud with all three parts — concession, claim, qualification — live
and straight in English.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — one full 'although' sentence, all three parts"

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
"Although the team hired ten new engineers this year, this doesn't
necessarily mean the product will ship faster, since they're still being
onboarded." There's no single correct topic — what matters is that all
three parts are present, the sentence is spoken fluently rather than
built word by word, and it was composed directly in English.
:::
