## Introduction

[Lesson 5](/lessons/5/) taught you to read a verb's form and know
instantly where it sits on the timeline. This drill asks for the
opposite direction, live: hear a time question, and produce the correct
verb form immediately, with no pause to work out the rule first.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
I finished it yesterday. I have already finished it. I will finish it
tomorrow.
:::

The three sentences answer three different questions about the exact
same task. Shadow all three, and notice how little the sentence changes
— almost everything stays the same except the verb form itself.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="6s"
Pick one question type at random before the timer starts: "What did you
do yesterday?", "What have you already done today?", or "What will you
do tomorrow?" When the timer starts, answer immediately with the
matching tense. Do this five times, mixing all three question types.
:::

::: timer seconds="6" label="Answer now — match the tense to the question, no planning pause"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Answer five mixed time questions out loud, then compare your answers to the shadowed model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "I checked the report yesterday,"
"I have already checked the report," and "I will check the report
tomorrow" are all correct answers to their matching question. What
matters is that the verb form matched the question's timeframe
immediately, without a pause to think it through.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="timeframe" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="the period of time a statement refers to (সময়কাল)" collocation="within the timeframe, realistic timeframe, set a timeframe" example="The team agreed on a realistic timeframe of six weeks for the upgrade."

::: rubric
- Did you use Past Simple, Present Perfect, and Future correctly at least once each across your five answers?
- Did the correct tense come out immediately, without a pause to work out the rule?
- Did your shadowed pace stay close to the model on your second attempt?
:::
