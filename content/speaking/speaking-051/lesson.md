## Introduction

[Lesson 51](/lessons/51/) taught you to trace how an author's ideas connect,
link by link, into a conclusion. Doing this out loud is a different skill
from doing it on paper — there's no backspace key, so you have to keep the
chain straight in your head while your mouth is still finishing the sentence
before it.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The delivery was late because the truck broke down. That meant the customer
missed their morning slot, so they had to reschedule the whole installation.
As a result, the technician lost an entire day's work over one broken part.
:::

Listen straight through once, then shadow it — notice how each "because,"
"so," and "as a result" gets a small beat of extra stress, almost like a
signpost telling the listener a new link is starting.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick any everyday chain of cause and effect — a plan that fell apart, a
small mistake that grew, a good decision that paid off — and explain it out
loud in three or four connected steps. Use spoken connectors (because, so,
which meant, that's why) to make every link audible, and finish with a
conclusion that actually follows.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — trace the whole chain before the timer ends"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your chain, then play it back and check whether a listener could follow every link."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct chain — what matters is that every link is audible
and connected. A strong answer sounds like: "I forgot to charge my phone, so
it died before my alarm went off. That meant I woke up an hour late, which
meant I missed the first bus. So I ended up walking into the meeting fifteen
minutes late — one uncharged phone, four links, one late arrival." Notice
every link is signposted; nothing is left for the listener to guess.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="signpost" pos="verb" register="technical" meaning="to clearly mark a transition so a listener can follow it (স্পষ্টভাবে ইঙ্গিত দেওয়া)" collocation="signpost the transition, signpost clearly, signpost such as" example="I signpost each step with 'first', 'as a result', and 'finally'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="thread" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the connected line of reasoning running through what you're saying (যুক্তির ধারা)" collocation="follow the thread, lose the thread, thread of the argument" example="Repeat the key term so the listener never loses the thread."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak at least three connected links, not a single flat statement?
- Did you signal each link out loud with a spoken connector?
- Did your conclusion genuinely follow from the chain rather than trail off into something unrelated?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long planning pause before starting?
:::
