## Introduction

[Lesson 36](/lessons/36/) taught you to resolve a word's meaning from
context when reading. This drill flips it around — say a sentence, live,
that resolves a word's meaning for someone *listening* to you, with no
time to plan the perfect phrasing first.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The company issued a statement about the billing issue. Every issue was
resolved within a week.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice how "billing issue" and "resolved"
make the first "issue" mean *problem*, while the second "issue" (issued
a statement) means *officially provided* — the sentence carries both
meanings without ever stopping to explain either one.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
You'll be given a word with more than one meaning: **model**. In 15
seconds, say one sentence using "model" to mean a physical or scientific
model (like a small-scale version of something), and make the meaning
obvious from the words around it — no stopping to plan first.
:::

::: timer seconds="15" label="Speak now — make the meaning obvious from context"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your sentence, then record it and check whether the meaning is clear without extra explanation."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — *"The engineers built a scale
model of the new terminal before construction began"* works because
"engineers," "scale," and "before construction" all point to the
physical-model sense. What matters is that you spoke immediately and
that a listener wouldn't need to ask "which model do you mean?"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="ambiguous" pos="adjective" meaning="capable of being understood in more than one way — দ্ব্যর্থবোধক" example="Said alone, 'model' is ambiguous until the sentence around it narrows it down."
:::

::: vocabulary word="context clue" pos="noun phrase" meaning="a word or phrase nearby that signals a term's intended meaning — প্রসঙ্গ-সংকেত" example="'Scale' and 'construction' are context clues pointing to the physical sense of 'model.'"
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your sentence make the intended meaning of the word clear without stopping to explain it separately?
- Did you speak within the time limit, with no planning pause?
- Could a listener repeat back which meaning you intended, based only on your sentence?
:::
