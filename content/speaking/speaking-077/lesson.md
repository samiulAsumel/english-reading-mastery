## Introduction

[Lesson 77](/lessons/77/) taught you to tell a fact apart from an
interpretation and an opinion. This drill makes you build all three
live, about a real situation, and label them the instant you say them.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The warehouse processed two hundred fewer orders this week. That's a
fact. This drop is likely connected to two staff members being on
leave. That's an interpretation. Management should have arranged
temporary cover. That's an opinion.
:::

Shadow this once, then again faster. Notice each label comes right
after its sentence, as a short, fixed tag, not a long explanation.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="40s"
Think of a real situation from your work or studies. In 40 seconds, say
one fact, one interpretation, and one opinion about it, labelling each
one right after you say it.
:::

::: timer seconds="40" label="Speak now — fact, interpretation, opinion, each labelled"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your three sentences and labels, then check the fact sentence carried no hidden judgment."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is the three-way split.
One natural version: "The delivery app's rating dropped from 4.6 to 4.1
over three months. That's a fact. The drop likely reflects slower
delivery after the courier partner changed. That's an interpretation.
The company shouldn't have switched providers without testing
reliability first. That's an opinion." Check your own fact sentence for
any hidden judgment word.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="tag" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to attach a short label to something right after stating it (চিহ্নিত করা)" collocation="tag a statement, tag as, tag with" example="After each statement I tag it: 'That is a fact', or 'That is my opinion'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="reasonable" pos="adjective" register="neutral" meaning="fair and sensible, in a way most people would accept (যুক্তিসঙ্গত)" collocation="reasonable assumption, reasonable doubt, reasonable price" example="It is reasonable to assume the delay was caused by the storm."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you say all three sentences and their labels without a long planning pause?
- Is your fact sentence genuinely free of any inference or judgment word?
- Would another reasonable person be able to disagree with your opinion sentence specifically, and not the fact?
:::
