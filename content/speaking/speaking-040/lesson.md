## Introduction

[Lesson 40](/lessons/40/) showed how moving "only" two words changes an
entire sentence's meaning. In speech there's no chance to go back and
fix the placement — you get one shot, live, so this drill trains exact
placement under time pressure.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Only the supervisor approved the new schedule. The supervisor approved
only the new schedule.
:::

Listen once, then shadow both sentences, noticing how the stress falls
on "supervisor" in the first and on "schedule" in the second — the
stress itself signals where "only" is attaching.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="12s"
You'll hear a fact: a company tested one new process, and nothing else.
In 12 seconds, say a sentence using "only" so it's unmistakable that
just that one process was tested — nothing about who did the testing.
:::

::: timer seconds="12" label="Speak your scoped sentence now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your sentence, then record it and check where the stress actually fell."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
*"The company tested only the new scheduling process this quarter."*
Stress falls on "scheduling process," signaling that's what "only"
restricts — not who did the testing, and not when. A listener should be
able to repeat back exactly what was and wasn't tested.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: rubric

- Did you avoid accidentally restricting the wrong part of the sentence?
- Did you speak within the time limit, without restarting the sentence?
