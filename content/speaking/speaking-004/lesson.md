## Introduction

[Lesson 4](/lessons/4/) taught you that a reason clause answers *why?*
and normally follows the main clause it explains. In real conversation,
someone asks you *why*, and you don't get time to plan a full sentence
first — the main clause and the reason clause have to come out attached
to each other, live.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
I was late because the bus didn't come. I checked twice because I wanted
to be sure. We changed the plan because the weather got worse.
:::

Notice *because* is said quickly and without stress — it's a connector,
not the main point of the sentence. Shadow it until the whole sentence
comes out in one breath, not two separate chunks.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="7s"
Someone asks you: "Why?" — about something true in your own life (why
you were tired today, why you chose your job, why you were late once).
When the timer starts, answer with one full sentence using *because*,
immediately.
:::

::: timer seconds="7" label="Answer now — main clause, then because, no planning pause"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Answer three different 'why?' questions out loud with because, then play yourself back."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — "I was tired because I worked a long
shift" and "I chose this job because it pays well" are both natural.
What matters is that the reason came immediately, attached to the main
clause, without a long pause or a Bangla sentence formed first.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="attached" pos="adjective" meaning="joined closely to something else, with no gap — সংযুক্ত" example="The reason clause should sound attached to the main clause, not separate."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your answer come out as one connected sentence, not two separate pieces with a long pause between them?
- Did you use because correctly to answer a real "why"?
- Did you recover and keep talking if your first attempt came out wrong, instead of stopping?
:::
