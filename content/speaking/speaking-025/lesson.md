## Introduction

[Lesson 25](/lessons/25/) taught you that a sentence's emphasis can
shift depending on what comes first. A fluent speaker does this
constantly, live — stressing "It was the manager who..." differently
from a plain statement of the same fact. This drill asks you to control
that emphasis with your own voice, on the spot.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
It was a software bug that delayed the shipment, not the shipping
company's fault at all.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it — notice the heavier stress on *software
bug*, right where the cleft structure puts the spotlight. That stress
isn't optional; it's what tells the listener where to focus.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="12s"
Fact: *heavy rain caused the flight delay.* When the timer starts, say
this fact twice, out loud, back to back — first as a cleft sentence
spotlighting the rain ("It was..."), then as a plain sentence
spotlighting the delay itself.
:::

::: timer seconds="12" label="Speak both versions now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Speak both versions, then record and listen for the stress difference."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
"It was heavy rain that caused the flight delay." Then: "The flight was
delayed for several hours because of heavy rain." What matters is
audible stress on *heavy rain* in the first version, and a plainer,
more even rhythm in the second.
:::

::: vocabulary word="stress" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="extra emphasis given to certain words in speech (শব্দে জোর)" collocation="stress a word, place the stress, word stress" example="Put the stress on 'safety', not on 'speed', if that is your main point."

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your first sentence use "It was X that..." with clear stress on X?
- Did your second sentence rephrase the same fact plainly, without the cleft structure?
- Did you speak both versions back to back, without a long pause between them?
:::
