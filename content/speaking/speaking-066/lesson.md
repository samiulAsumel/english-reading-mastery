## Introduction

[Lesson 66](/lessons/66/) taught you to preserve an author's exact
level of uncertainty. Under time pressure, speakers often round "may"
up to "will" without noticing — this drill trains you to resist that,
live.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Delays are unlikely under normal conditions. But if a key supplier
misses the deadline, the impact on the schedule could be severe.
:::

Notice "unlikely" and "could be severe" are both said with confidence
in the delivery, even though the content itself is hedged — calibration
is about word choice, not about sounding unsure.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given a short risk scenario. When the timer starts, state:
(1) how likely the risk is, using an accurate hedge word, and (2) how
severe the impact would be if it happened — as two separate, clearly
distinguished statements.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Give your risk statement now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Give your risk statement, then record it and compare your hedge words against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer clearly
separates "how likely" from "how bad if it happens" as two distinct
sentences, and picks a hedge word (unlikely, likely, possible) that
matches the actual scenario rather than defaulting to "definitely" or
"probably" out of habit.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="probability" pos="noun" meaning="how likely something is to happen — সম্ভাবনা" example="The probability of a delay is low, but not zero."
:::

::: vocabulary word="severe" pos="adjective" meaning="serious in effect or consequence — গুরুতর" example="A single supplier failure could have a severe impact on the schedule."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state probability and impact as two separate, clearly distinguished statements?
- Did your hedge word match the real strength of the scenario, not an automatic default?
- Did you respond immediately, with no planning pause?
:::
