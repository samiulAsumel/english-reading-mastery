## Introduction

[Lesson 45](/lessons/45/) taught you to recognise fixed academic word
groups on sight when reading. Saying them live is the harder half of the
skill: a fluent speaker retrieves *contributed to* or *consistent with*
as one ready-made unit, mid-sentence, without pausing to assemble it word
by word.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Better training contributed to the improvement, and the results are
consistent with earlier studies.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it at full speed. Notice
that "contributed to" and "consistent with" each carry one smooth stress
pattern, the same way a single word would — not four separate words said
one at a time.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
You'll be given a short workplace topic. In 20 seconds, speak one or two
sentences that use, correctly, one causal-family verb (contribute to,
result in, lead to, or give rise to) and one stance bundle (be
consistent with, be attributed to, or be associated with).
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak your sentence now"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Given the topic *a port added extra crane capacity*: *"The extra crane
capacity contributed to faster vessel turnaround, and the results are
consistent with what other ports reported after similar upgrades."*
There is no single correct sentence here — what matters is that
"contributed to" signals partial, not total, causation, and "consistent
with" signals compatibility rather than proof, both spoken as single
fluent units.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="contribute to" pos="phrasal verb" meaning="to be one of several factors producing an outcome, without being the sole cause — অবদান রাখা" example="Saying 'contributed to' out loud signals that other factors were also involved."
:::

::: vocabulary word="be consistent with" pos="phrase" meaning="to be compatible with a finding, without thereby proving it — সামঞ্জস্যপূর্ণ হওয়া" example="Spoken live, 'consistent with' should never be swapped for the much stronger 'proves.'"
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your causal-family verb match the strength of causation you actually meant?
- Did your stance bundle avoid overstating what the evidence shows?
- Did both chunks come out as single fluent units, not word-by-word constructions?
- Did you speak within the time limit, without a long pause to retrieve either chunk?
:::
