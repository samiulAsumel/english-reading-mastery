## Introduction

[Lesson 70](/lessons/70/) taught you to find what an author is actually
placing in focus. This drill asks you to build that same emphasis out
loud, live — using "not only... but also" and "what matters is" to make
sure your listener hears your real point, not just your first sentence.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The new system not only processes orders faster but also reduces
errors. What matters most is the error reduction, not the speed.
:::

Notice the stress lands on "errors" and "matters most" — a fluent
speaker doesn't say every word with equal weight, they lean into the
word carrying the real point.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
You'll be given a short scenario with two true facts about it. When the
timer starts, combine them into one "not only... but also" sentence,
then say "what matters most is..." to name which one actually matters
more, and why.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Give your answer now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Give your emphasis sentence, then record it and compare your stress and structure against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer keeps the "not
only... but also" sentence genuinely parallel on both sides, and
follows it with a clear "what matters most is..." that names one
specific point rather than just repeating both facts again.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="stress" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="extra emphasis given to certain words in speech (জোর)" collocation="stress a word, place the stress, word stress" example="Put the stress on 'safety', not on 'speed', if that is your main point."
:::

::: vocabulary word="what matters is" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="a spoken phrase that directly names the most important point (যা গুরুত্বপূর্ণ তা হলো)" collocation="what matters is, but what matters is, what really matters is" example="What matters is reliability, not price."
:::
