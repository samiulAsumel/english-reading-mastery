## Introduction

[Lesson 42](/lessons/42/) taught you to find a sentence's main clause
first, then see how a subordinate clause hangs off it. Saying a sentence
like that out loud, live, is a harder skill than reading one — you have
to decide, in real time, which clause carries your main point and which
one is only supporting it, before the sentence is even finished.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The terminal delayed the shipment because the crane broke down during
loading.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice the small pause
right before "because" — that pause is doing real work, signalling to
the listener that the main point ("the terminal delayed the shipment")
is finished and a supporting reason is coming next.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
You'll be given a short scenario about a workplace change. In 20
seconds, say one sentence with a clear main clause and a subordinate
clause attached to it using because, although, when, or since. When the
timer starts, speak immediately — no planning pause.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Say your sentence now"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Given the scenario *a warehouse switched to barcode scanning*: *"The
warehouse reduced picking errors because the new scanners caught
mistakes before items left the shelf."* There is no single correct
sentence here — what matters is that the main clause stands as a
complete idea on its own, the subordinate clause is correctly attached
with a real subordinator, and a listener can hear, from your pacing
alone, which clause is the main point.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="subordinate clause" pos="noun phrase" meaning="a dependent clause that cannot stand alone as a sentence — অধীনস্থ বাক্যাংশ" example="'Because the crane broke down' is a subordinate clause, not a complete sentence on its own."
:::

::: vocabulary word="main clause" pos="noun phrase" meaning="a clause that can stand alone as a complete sentence and carries a sentence's main point — মুখ্য বাক্যাংশ" example="Even with the subordinate clause removed, the main clause still makes a complete sentence."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your sentence have one clear main clause that could stand alone?
- Was the subordinate clause correctly attached with because/although/when/since?
- Did you speak immediately, with no planning pause before starting?
- Did your pacing (a brief pause before the subordinator) help signal the clause boundary?
:::
