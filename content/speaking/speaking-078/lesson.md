## Introduction

[Lesson 78](/lessons/78/) taught you to read a sentence for the job it
does, not just what it says. This drill makes you build that live —
support a claim, concede a real cost, and name each move as you make it.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The new scheduling software cut missed shifts by eighteen percent. That
supports the claim. It did require two weeks of staff retraining
first. That concedes a limitation.
:::

Shadow this once, then again at a natural pace. Notice the concession
isn't said apologetically — it's stated plainly, the way a confident
author admits a real cost without undercutting the whole argument.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="35s"
Think of a claim from your own work or studies. In 35 seconds, say a
sentence that supports it with evidence, then a sentence that concedes a
real limitation — naming each sentence's job right after you say it.
:::

::: timer seconds="35" label="Speak now — support the claim, concede a limitation, name each job"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your two sentences and their labels, then check the concession was a real cost, not a fake one."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is a genuine concession.
One natural version: "The new inventory system cut stock discrepancies
by twenty-two percent. That supports the claim. It did take a month of
parallel manual tracking while staff learned it. That concedes a
limitation." Check that your own concession names a real cost, not
something trivial that doesn't actually weaken the case at all.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="undercut" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to weaken an argument or claim, often unintentionally (দুর্বল করা)" collocation="undercut the argument, undercut a claim, be undercut by" example="A single counterexample can undercut a universal claim."
:::

::: vocabulary word="credible" pos="adjective" register="formal" meaning="believable, trustworthy (বিশ্বাসযোগ্য)" collocation="credible source, highly credible, credible claim" example="The regulator treated the whistleblower's account as credible."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak both sentences and their labels without a long planning pause?
- Was your concession a real, specific limitation, not a vague or fake one?
- Did the concession sound like it strengthened your credibility rather than undermining the claim?
:::
