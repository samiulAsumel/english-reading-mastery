## Introduction

[Lesson 2](/lessons/2/) taught you to spot a sentence's core underneath
its decoration. Speaking works the opposite way: you say the core first,
and the decoration has to arrive while you're already talking, with no
pause to plan it. That's exactly what this drill practises.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The manager reviewed the report. The manager carefully reviewed the
report. The manager carefully reviewed the report this morning.
:::

Notice the model doesn't restart the sentence to add a detail — it keeps
the same words and simply extends them forward. Shadow it twice: once at
the first length, once at the full length.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="7s"
Say a short sentence about something you did today — three or four
words, Subject + Verb + Object. As soon as you finish it, without
pausing, add one more detail: how, or where, or when. Do this five
times.
:::

::: timer seconds="7" label="Say the sentence, then keep going — add the detail without stopping"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say five short sentences, each with one added detail tacked on, then compare your fluency to the shadowed model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There is no single correct sentence — what matters is that the addition
came without a restart. For example: "I checked the schedule. I checked
the schedule quickly." or "I checked the schedule this morning." Either
extension is correct as long as it kept moving forward instead of
starting over.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="extend" pos="verb" meaning="to make something longer by adding to it — সম্প্রসারিত করা" example="Extend the sentence by adding one detail, without restarting it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="restart" pos="verb" meaning="to begin again from the start — পুনরায় শুরু করা" example="Try not to restart the sentence just to add one word."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you add the detail by continuing forward, rather than stopping and restarting the whole sentence?
- Was the detail you added a genuine adjective, adverb, or short "where"/"when" phrase?
- Did at least 3 of your 5 attempts finish before the timer ended?
:::
