## Introduction

Objects are the easiest instant-naming drill because a noun is just one
word. Actions are harder — you need a small sentence shape, "**is/are +
verb-ing**," and building even a small sentence under time pressure is
exactly where the old habit (form it in Bangla, then translate) tries to
come back. This lesson pushes past that.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="8s"
Picture someone doing something ordinary — walking, eating, reading,
driving, talking on the phone. When you start the timer, say a full
sentence: "He is walking" / "She is eating." Do this five times with
five different actions.
:::

::: timer seconds="8" label="Say the sentence — don't build it in Bangla first"
:::

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.85"
He is walking. She is eating. They are talking. I am reading. We are
waiting.
:::

Notice how naturally "is/are" links to the verb-ing in a native rhythm —
almost run together, not said as two separate, effortful words. That
rhythm is exactly what shadowing trains your mouth to copy.

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your five action sentences out loud, then compare your rhythm to the shadowed model above."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Any grammatically correct "subject + is/are + verb-ing" sentence counts
— for example: "He is driving." "She is cooking." "They are running."
"I am writing." "We are waiting for the bus." What matters is speed and
correct verb form, not a specific sentence.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you correctly use is/are with the -ing form every time, without stopping to think?
- Did at least 3 of your 5 sentences come out before the timer ended?
- Did your shadowed rhythm sound closer to natural speech on your second attempt than your first?
:::
