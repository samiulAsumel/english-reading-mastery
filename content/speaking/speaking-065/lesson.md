## Introduction

[Lesson 65](/lessons/65/) taught you that "better" is an incomplete
sentence until you supply the missing criterion. This drill asks you to
evaluate two options out loud, live, naming your criterion first — not
after someone asks "better at what?"

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
In terms of speed, the new system is better — it processes transactions
in half the time. In terms of setup cost, though, it's worse.
:::

Notice "in terms of" is spoken quickly, almost as one word — it's a
fixed phrase that signals the criterion is coming next, and a fluent
speaker doesn't pause before or after it.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given two options to compare. When the timer starts, name
your criterion first ("in terms of..."), state which is better and why,
then name one criterion where the judgment flips — all in one
continuous answer.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Evaluate the options now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer opens with "in
terms of [criterion]" before making any judgment at all, backs the
judgment with a specific detail, and then names a second criterion
where the answer changes — without needing to be prompted for it.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="in terms of" pos="phrase" register="formal" meaning="used to name the specific dimension a claim applies to (যে দিক থেকে)" collocation="in terms of cost, in terms of speed, in terms of safety" example="In terms of cost, Option A wins; in terms of reliability, Option B does."
:::

::: vocabulary word="justify" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to give a good enough reason for something (যুক্তিসঙ্গত করা)" collocation="justify a decision, fully justify, justify the cost" example="The savings justify the higher upfront cost."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the criterion before making the comparative claim, not after?
- Did your evidence genuinely match the stated criterion?
- Did you name a second criterion where the judgment flips, without being asked?
:::
