## Introduction

[Lesson 26](/lessons/26/) taught you to read a claim, a counterargument,
and a rebuttal on the page. Arguing out loud is harder — there's no time
to draft the sentence first, and a real conversation partner won't wait
for you to plan your rebuttal. This drill trains the shape of an argument
as something you can produce live.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Companies should let employees choose their own working hours. Some
managers argue that flexible hours make teams harder to coordinate.
However, most teams already coordinate around a few shared meeting
times, not every single hour of the day.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice how "however" carries a small
drop and pause before it — that pause is doing real work, signaling
"here comes my answer," before the rebuttal even starts.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Pick any claim you actually believe (about work, technology, or daily
life). When the timer starts, say your claim, one reason for it, and a
counterargument someone might raise against it — followed immediately by
your rebuttal. Don't script it first; build it sentence by sentence as
you speak.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — claim, reason, counterargument, rebuttal"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct claim — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Small businesses should use social media for marketing. It
reaches customers without the cost of traditional advertising. Some
owners argue it takes too much time to manage well. However, even ten
minutes a day is often enough to keep a small, loyal audience engaged."
Check that your version had all four pieces, spoken without a long pause
before the rebuttal.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="counterargument" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="the opposing view you raise against your own claim before answering it (প্রতিতর্ক)" collocation="raise a counterargument, answer a counterargument, strong counterargument" example="Someone will say it is too costly; my answer to that counterargument is the long-term saving."
:::

::: vocabulary word="pivot" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the turning point in speech where you move from one side of an argument to your response (মোড়)" collocation="pivot to, the pivot in, pivot from" example="The pivot word 'however' tells the listener my real point is coming."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state the claim within the first few seconds, without a long planning pause?
- Did your counterargument sound like a real objection, not a token one?
- Did "however" (or a similar pivot word) lead naturally into your rebuttal?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
