## Introduction

[Lesson 58](/lessons/58/) taught you to reconstruct a mechanism — the
intermediate steps that turn a cause into an effect — rather than accepting
a bare "this caused that." Explaining a mechanism out loud, live, is the
real test: you have to keep the cause, the mechanism, and sometimes the
trigger-versus-root-cause distinction all in your head at once, with no
paragraph in front of you to check.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The update cut loading time by compressing images before they're sent to
the browser. The immediate trigger was a slow homepage; the real root cause
was that images had never been resized in the first place.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice how "by" and "root cause" both get a small lift —
they're the spoken signposts marking exactly where the mechanism and the
deeper explanation sit.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Explain a mechanism you understand well, out loud. State the cause and
effect, then say how it works using "by," "through," or "via." If a trigger
and a root cause are both relevant, name both, live, without pausing to plan
it out first.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — cause and effect, then the mechanism, then trigger vs. root cause if relevant"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your explanation, then check whether you actually named the mechanism, not just the result."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct mechanism to pick — what matters is the shape of
the explanation. A strong answer sounds like: "Our support team cut response
time by routing tickets automatically to whichever agent already handled
that customer before, instead of a random queue. The trigger for most slow
responses used to be a ticket landing with someone unfamiliar with the
account; the root cause was that the old system had no memory of past
conversations at all." Cause, effect, mechanism, trigger, root cause — all
spoken live, in order.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="trigger" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="the immediate event that sets a process or problem in motion, distinct from its deeper cause (তাৎক্ষণিক সূচনাকারী ঘটনা)" collocation="trigger an alert, immediate trigger, trigger for" example="The trigger was a midnight software update, not the underlying fault."
:::

::: vocabulary word="by means of" pos="phrase" register="formal" meaning="through the use of a specific method or tool (মাধ্যমে)" collocation="by means of, achieved by means of, by means of a system" example="Cargo is tracked by means of a barcode system."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state the cause and effect clearly before explaining the mechanism?
- Did you use "by," "through," or "via" to name the actual pathway, live?
- If relevant, did you separate the trigger from the root cause without losing the thread?
- Did you speak immediately, with no planning pause before starting?
:::
