## Introduction

[Lesson 47](/lessons/47/) taught you to ask what job each sentence is
doing in a paragraph's argument. This drill asks you to build that
argument out loud, live, and narrate the job of each sentence as you say
it — the same skill readers use, run in reverse.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Sales at the shop have dropped this quarter. That's the context. The real
problem is that regular customers are visiting less often. That's the
claim. Repeat visits fell by a third compared to last year. That's the
evidence.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice the short, flat tag after each
sentence — "that's the context," "that's the claim" — and how naming the
job doesn't slow the speaker down much; it becomes a reflex with
practice.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="40s"
Pick a topic you know well. In 40 seconds, say a context sentence, a
claim sentence, and an evidence sentence about it, out loud — naming each
one's job right after you say it, the way the model above does. Don't
write anything down first.
:::

::: timer seconds="40" label="Speak now — context, claim, evidence, each one named as you go"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your three-sentence explanation with the job of each one named, then play it back."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic. What matters is that all three
sentences are present, each one plausibly does the job you named for it
(the claim sentence should be arguable, not just another fact), and you
kept speaking continuously instead of stopping to plan between
sentences.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you produce a genuine context, claim, and evidence sentence, each doing a different job?
- Did you name each job immediately, without a long pause to think?
- Did you speak the whole thing without writing it down first?
:::
