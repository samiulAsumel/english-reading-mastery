## Introduction

[Lesson 75](/lessons/75/) taught you that not all sources for the same
claim deserve equal trust. This drill makes you compare two sources out
loud and reach a live verdict, with a reason, under time pressure.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The vendor's website claims a 30% reduction in errors. An independent
audit measured 14% over six months of real use. The audit is more
trustworthy — the vendor benefits from a bigger number, the audit
doesn't.
:::

Shadow this once slowly, once at full speed. Notice the judgment comes
with its reason attached in the same breath, not as an afterthought.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="35s"
Think of a claim you've heard reported by two different kinds of
sources — a company and an independent reviewer, an official and a
witness. In 35 seconds, say both versions, then judge out loud which is
more trustworthy and why.
:::

::: timer seconds="35" label="Speak now — version one, version two, judgment with a reason"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record both versions and your judgment, then check your reason was about independence or measurement, not just a gut feeling."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is a real, specific
judgment. One natural version: "The airline says flight delays dropped
20% this year. An independent aviation tracker measured only an 8% drop
using flight-log data. The tracker is more trustworthy — the airline
has a reputational incentive to report a bigger improvement." Check
that your own reason named something concrete about the source, not
just "it sounds more accurate."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="stake" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a personal or financial interest in a particular outcome (স্বার্থ)" collocation="have a stake in, high stakes, financial stake" example="The vendor has a stake in the result, so treat its claim with care."
:::

::: vocabulary word="tracker" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="an independent system or organization that monitors and measures something over time (পর্যবেক্ষক সংস্থা)" collocation="independent tracker, cargo tracker, performance tracker" example="An independent tracker records vessel delays at every port."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak both versions of the claim without a long planning pause?
- Did your judgment give a specific reason, not just "one seems more reliable"?
- Did the reason involve independence, measurement method, or stake in the outcome?
:::
