## Introduction

[Lesson 7](/lessons/7/) taught you that a preposition builds a
relationship between two things. In speech, that relationship has to
arrive instantly when someone asks *where*, *when*, or *how* — there's
no time to search for the "correct" preposition the way you might while
writing.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
I work in a busy office. It depends on the weather. The report was
written by my colleague.
:::

Notice how naturally the preposition attaches to the verb before it —
*depends on* is said as one unit, not two words with a gap. Shadow it
until that attachment feels automatic.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="6s"
Someone asks you "where?", "when?", or "how?" about something true in
your life. When the timer starts, answer with one full sentence
containing a prepositional phrase, immediately. Do this five times.
:::

::: timer seconds="6" label="Answer now — the preposition attaches to the sentence, no separate pause"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Answer five where/when/how questions with a prepositional phrase, then play yourself back."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "I work in a small office," "It
depends on the traffic," and "The decision was made by my manager" are
all natural answers. What matters is that the prepositional phrase
arrived attached to the sentence, without a long searching pause.
:::
