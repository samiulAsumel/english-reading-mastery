## Introduction

[Lesson 6](/lessons/6/) taught you that *a* and *an* are chosen by
sound, not spelling — *a university*, *an hour*. In speech there's no
time to check a rule; your mouth has to make the choice by ear, on the
spot, which is exactly why this drill is pure rhythm and sound
training, not grammar.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.85"
A ship. An hour. A university. An idea. A report. An important report.
:::

Listen once without speaking. Then shadow it, paying attention to how
*an* glides smoothly into the vowel sound that follows it, while *a*
stays short and separate before a consonant sound.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="6s"
Look around you and pick a noun. When the timer starts, say it with the
correct article — *a* or *an* — immediately, by ear. Do this six times,
mixing nouns that start with consonant sounds and vowel sounds.
:::

::: timer seconds="6" label="Say it now — let your ear choose a or an, don't spell it out in your head"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say six nouns with a or an, then play yourself back and check each choice was correct."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Any noun paired with the correct article by sound counts — "a chair,"
"an office," "a schedule," "an hour." What matters is that the choice
came from listening to the first sound, not from thinking through the
spelling rule.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="glide" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to move smoothly and continuously (মসৃণভাবে চলা)" collocation="glide smoothly, glide into, glide over" example="In fluent speech, the article glides into the noun without a pause."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you choose a or an correctly for at least 5 of your 6 nouns?
- Did the choice come from the sound you heard, not the letter you pictured?
- Did your shadowed pronunciation of "an + vowel" sound smoothly joined, not like two separate words?
:::
