## Introduction

[Lesson 56](/lessons/56/) taught you to separate a number's observation
from its interpretation from a stronger causal claim, and to check what else
might explain a result before crediting one cause. Saying that out loud,
live, is harder than writing it — you have to hold the observation, the
confound, and the careful conclusion together in your head while you speak.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Shipping delays dropped by a third after we switched carriers. But we also
changed our packaging that same month, so I can't say the carrier switch
alone caused the improvement.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice how "but" and "so" carry a small stress — they mark
the exact moment the speaker pulls back from an easy causal claim to a more
honest one.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Pick a number you know that changed after something happened at work, in
your studies, or in a project. Say what the number was and how it changed,
name one other thing that also changed around the same time, and end with a
conclusion that only claims what the number actually supports.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — the number, the change, one confound, an honest conclusion"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your explanation, then check whether you stated the confound clearly instead of rushing past it."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct number to pick — what matters is the shape of the
reasoning. A strong answer sounds like: "Our website's bounce rate fell from
55% to 38% after we redesigned the homepage. But we also ran a big
advertising push that same month, bringing in a different kind of visitor,
so I can't say the redesign alone explains the drop. What I can say is that
bounce rate improved after both changes happened together." Observation,
confound, honest conclusion — all delivered live, without a script.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="confound" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to make it hard to tell which of two things happening together is actually the cause (জটিল করে তোলা)" collocation="confound the results, confounded by, confound the analysis" example="Weather can confound the comparison, because it changes both variables."
:::

::: vocabulary word="hold off on" pos="phrasal verb" register="neutral" meaning="to delay making a strong claim until there's enough evidence (বিরত থাকা)" collocation="hold off on a conclusion, hold off on a decision, hold off until" example="I would hold off on a firm conclusion until we have more data."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you clearly state the number and how it changed before interpreting it?
- Did you name a real confounding factor, not just say "other things changed"?
- Did your final conclusion stay within what the number actually supports?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause to plan the sentence first?
:::
