## Introduction

[Lesson 83](/lessons/83/) gave you a specific test for telling
refinement apart from contradiction: can both statements be true at
once? This drill asks you to run that test live, out loud, on a claim
that shifts across a passage — the kind of judgment call a real
conversation about a report demands in the moment, not after time to
think.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The first claim says clearance time was cut in half. But later, it turns
out that only applied to low-risk shipments — everything else stayed
the same. That's not a refinement, that's a contradiction.
:::

Notice "that's not a refinement, that's a contradiction" is said as one
confident unit — a fluent speaker doesn't hedge the judgment once
they've made it.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
Section 1 of a report claims customs clearance time was cut in half.
Section 4 reveals the cut only applied to low-risk shipments — for
everything else, nothing changed. When the timer starts, say out loud
whether this is refinement or contradiction, and justify it in one
breath.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Say your judgment now — refinement or contradiction, and why"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your judgment and justification, then record it and compare against the model answer."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer sounds like:
"This is a contradiction, not a refinement — the first claim implied a
general effect, but it turns out the reduction only held for a small
group of shipments, so the original claim isn't defensible as stated."
What matters is stating the judgment first, then justifying it
immediately, without a long pause in between.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="defensible" pos="adjective" register="formal" meaning="able to be reasonably supported or argued for (যুক্তিসঙ্গতভাবে রক্ষণযোগ্য)" collocation="defensible position, defensible claim, highly defensible" example="The narrower claim is defensible; the original was not."
:::

:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state your judgment (refinement or contradiction) clearly and first?
- Did you justify it immediately, in the same breath?
- Did you speak without a long planning pause before starting?
:::
