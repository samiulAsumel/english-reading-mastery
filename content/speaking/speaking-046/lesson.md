## Introduction

[Lesson 46](/lessons/46/) taught you to read most of a passage quickly
and slow down only for the sentence that carries the real claim. This
drill flips that into a speaking skill: explain something out loud at a
natural pace, then immediately identify and re-say — slowly, clearly —
the one sentence that actually mattered.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The office moved to a new building last month. The new space has more
desks and better internet. The real change, though, is that the team now
finishes projects almost a week faster.
:::

Listen once without speaking, then shadow it. Notice how the first two
sentences move quickly and flatly, and how the voice slows and adds
weight to "The real change, though" — that's the spoken version of
information density.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick something you know well. In 30 seconds, explain it out loud with 2-3
sentences of fast background, then one clearly slower, more deliberate
sentence that states the actual point. Say "the real point is" before
that sentence if it helps you mark it clearly.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — background fast, then slow down for the real point"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your explanation, then play it back and check whether your pace actually changed for the key sentence."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct explanation — any topic works. What matters is
that your recording has an audible pace change: quick and light for the
background, slower and more deliberate for the sentence that states the
real point, with maybe a short pause right before it.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your background sentences sound genuinely fast and light?
- Did your key sentence sound noticeably slower and more deliberate?
- Did you speak without a written script, thinking on your feet?
:::
