## Introduction

[Lesson 54](/lessons/54/) taught you to reconstruct a written process as a
diagram in your head. Explaining one out loud, live, is the test of whether
that diagram is really there — you have to hold the whole sequence in memory
while you speak, without a paragraph in front of you to check.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Once you submit the form, the system checks your details automatically. If
everything matches, you get approved in seconds. If something's missing, it
gets sent to a person to look at instead.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice how "once," "if," and "instead" all get a small
lift in the voice — they're the spoken signposts marking trigger, condition,
and branch.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Explain a process you know well — at work, in an app you use, anywhere — out
loud. State what triggers it, walk through the stages in order, mention one
exception to the normal path, and end by saying what the final result is.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — trigger, stages, one exception, final result"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your explanation, then check whether a listener with no background could follow every stage."

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct process to pick — what matters is the shape of
the explanation. A strong answer sounds like: "Once you place an order, the
system checks if the item's in stock. If it is, it ships within a day. If
it's out of stock — which happens more than you'd think — you get an email
instead, asking if you want to wait or get a refund. Either way, you hear
back within twenty-four hours." Trigger, stages, exception, final state —
all spoken live, in order.
:::

- Did you mention at least one exception without losing the thread?
- Did you end with a clear statement of the final result?
:::
