## Introduction

[Lesson 82](/lessons/82/) taught you to track a concept's meaning as it
expands, narrows, or gets reframed. This drill asks you to explain that
shift out loud, in real time — holding the "before" and "after" meanings
in your head at once while you speak, the way you'd have to in an actual
conversation about a book or report you just read.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The word "risk" starts out broad — any delay counts. But later, the
report narrows it: a delay only counts as risk if it also breaks a
contract deadline.
:::

Listen to how "narrows" gets slight extra stress — that's the word
carrying the whole point of the sentence, and a fluent speaker leans on
it.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
A report first defines "risk" as "any chance a shipment is delayed."
Later it adds: risk only counts if the delay also causes a missed
contractual deadline. When the timer starts, explain out loud what
happened to the concept — name the type of change and what it now
excludes.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Explain the shift now — out loud, no notes"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Explain the concept shift, then record it and compare against the model answer."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer sounds like:
"The concept of risk gets narrowed — it starts out meaning any delay,
but later the report adds a condition: the delay has to also cause a
missed contract deadline. So a harmless delay with no financial
consequence doesn't count as risk anymore." What matters is naming the
change type (narrowing) and stating the excluded case clearly.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="exclude" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to leave something out of a definition or category (বাদ দেওয়া)" collocation="exclude from, deliberately exclude, exclude cases" example="The definition excludes cases where the delay was weather-related."
:::

::: vocabulary word="condition" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a requirement that must be true for something to count (শর্ত)" collocation="meet the condition, on condition that, necessary condition" example="A shipment counts as delayed only under one condition: arrival beyond two hours."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the correct type of change (narrowing)?
- Did you state clearly what now falls outside the concept's boundary?
- Did you speak in full sentences without long pauses to plan?
:::
