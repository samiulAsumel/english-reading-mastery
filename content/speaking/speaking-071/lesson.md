## Introduction

[Lesson 71](/lessons/71/) taught you to catch an author narrowing a
claim across a passage. This drill makes you do it live: start broad,
correct yourself out loud with "however," and land on a sharper final
claim — all without a script.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Automated systems can reduce processing time. However, faster
processing doesn't always mean better decisions. Therefore, automation
helps most when the decision itself is simple and repetitive.
:::

Listen once straight through, then shadow it. Notice how "however" and
"therefore" both land with a short pause right after them — that pause
is doing work, signalling a turn in the argument, not a hesitation.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given a topic. In 30 seconds, say a broad claim about it, then
say "However," and add a real limitation, then say "Therefore," and
narrow the claim into a final, more precise position. Topic: automated
customs clearance systems.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — broad claim, however, therefore, final position"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your 30-second claim-narrowing, then play it back and check the shape held together."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording — what matters is the shape. One
natural version: "Automated customs clearance can dramatically speed up
processing. However, it only works well when shipment documentation is
already complete and accurate. Therefore, automation helps fastest at
ports that have already standardized their paperwork." Check your own
attempt for the same three moves: broad claim, real limitation, narrower
final position.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak immediately, with no pause to plan the whole thing first?
- Did "however" introduce a real limitation, not just a filler pause?
- Did your final sentence land somewhere more precise than your opening claim?
