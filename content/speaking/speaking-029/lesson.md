## Introduction

[Lesson 29](/lessons/29/) trained you to read a causal claim critically,
checking for a named mechanism and an alternative explanation the author
might be skipping. Doing that live, in your own speech, is harder —
it's tempting to just blurt "X caused Y" and stop. This drill makes you
keep talking past the easy claim into an honest alternative.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The new checklist was largely responsible for fewer shipping errors,
though the team also hired two experienced staff that same month, so
that probably helped too.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice the pivot at "though" — the
speaker's pitch drops slightly and slows down right before naming the
alternative explanation, signaling "here's the honest part."

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Think of a real or plausible cause-and-effect situation you know
something about (work, a habit, a routine). When the timer starts, state
the causal claim and briefly how it works, then immediately add one
honest alternative explanation using "though" or "however." Don't stop
after the first claim — keep speaking into the alternative.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — cause, mechanism, then an honest alternative"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your causal claim and alternative explanation, then check whether you kept speaking past the first claim without stopping."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct claim — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Switching to the new booking app was largely responsible for
shorter queues at the gate, by letting drivers reserve arrival times in
advance. Though the terminal also opened a second lane that same month,
so that probably helped too." Check that you didn't stop after the first
sentence — the alternative explanation has to actually get spoken.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="mechanism" pos="noun" meaning="how a cause actually produces its effect, spoken aloud rather than just asserted — প্রক্রিয়া" example="Saying 'by letting drivers reserve slots' names the mechanism instead of just claiming the app helped."
:::

::: vocabulary word="alternative explanation" pos="noun phrase" meaning="another possible cause for the same effect, voiced honestly instead of ignored — বিকল্প ব্যাখ্যা" example="Naming the extra lane as an alternative explanation kept the spoken claim honest."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state the cause and its mechanism immediately, without a long pause?
- Did you keep speaking into an honest alternative explanation, rather than stopping at the first claim?
- Did you use "largely responsible for" or "contributed to" instead of an unqualified "caused"?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
