## Introduction

[Lesson 79](/lessons/79/) taught you to read what one paragraph inherits
from the one before it. This drill makes you build that connection out
loud, live — two short spoken passages, joined by an explicit spoken
bridge.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Self-checkout kiosks cut wait times during regular hours, but they
struggled to keep up during busy evenings. This limitation led the
store to keep two staffed counters open at peak times alongside the
kiosks.
:::

Shadow this once, then again. Notice how "this limitation" out loud
carries the connection all by itself — a listener knows exactly which
idea is being picked up, without needing anything repeated.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Think of a process you know well. In 45 seconds, say a short
explanation that ends on a real limitation, then say a second part that
opens with an explicit spoken reference back to it ("this limitation…",
"this raises the question of…") and does a new job.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — part one ending on a limitation, part two bridging back to it"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record both parts, then check part two opened with an explicit reference back to part one."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is the explicit bridge.
One natural version: "Switching to online booking cut phone-line wait
times sharply. However, older customers who preferred calling in
struggled to adapt to the new system. This limitation led the clinic to
keep a dedicated phone line staffed just for that group, alongside the
online system." Check that your own part two opened by naming the
limitation from part one directly, not with a fresh, unrelated sentence.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="pick up" pos="phrasal verb" register="neutral" meaning="to continue or return to an idea mentioned earlier (পুনরায় তুলে ধরা)" collocation="pick up on, pick up the thread, pick up where you left off" example="Let me pick up the point I made about cost earlier."
:::

::: vocabulary word="resolve" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to settle or answer a problem or question that was raised (সমাধান করা)" collocation="resolve the question, resolve an issue, resolve a problem" example="The next paragraph resolves the problem raised in the first."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did part two open with an explicit spoken reference back to part one, not a fresh unrelated idea?
- Did part two do a genuinely new job — resolving, extending, or exemplifying — rather than repeating part one?
- Did you speak both parts without a long pause in between?
:::
