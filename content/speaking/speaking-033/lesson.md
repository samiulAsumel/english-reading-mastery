## Introduction

[Lesson 33](/lessons/33/) gave you a precise vocabulary for judging an
argument's strength — "not established" instead of a blunt "wrong," and
eighteen named fallacies to recognize on sight. This drill asks you to
turn that same precise judgment on your own spoken argument, live,
immediately after you make it.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Two of our five staff had fewer errors with the new scanner this month.
That's not established proof it works for everyone — it's a hasty
generalization from a very small sample.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice the flat, level tone on "not
established" — it's spoken as a calm judgment, not a dramatic accusation,
which is exactly the tone calibrated evaluation should carry.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
State a short argument on a real or plausible topic you know something
about — evidence leading to a conclusion. When the timer starts, say the
argument, then immediately evaluate it out loud using "established" or
"not established," and name one specific fallacy (hasty generalization,
post hoc reasoning, or another) your wording avoids or narrowly escapes.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Speak now — argument, then your live evaluation"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your argument and evaluation, then check whether you used 'not established' rather than a blunt 'wrong.'"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Our sales rose the same week we changed the store layout, so
the new layout worked. That's not established, though — it's close to
post hoc reasoning, since the week also had a public holiday that could
easily explain the rise on its own." Check that your evaluation named a
specific fallacy, not just a vague "this might not be true."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="not established" pos="phrase" register="formal" meaning="a calibrated spoken judgment for a claim that is unproven, without claiming it's actually false (অপ্রমাণিত)" collocation="not yet established, remain not established, claim is not established" example="That the policy caused the drop is not established; we only know accidents fell afterwards."
:::

::: vocabulary word="post hoc reasoning" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="assuming that because one event followed another, the first caused the second (পরবর্তী-অতএব-কারণ যুক্তি)" collocation="avoid post hoc reasoning, fall into post hoc reasoning, example of post hoc reasoning" example="Crediting the new rule just because accidents fell afterwards is post hoc reasoning."
:::
