## Introduction

[Lesson 27](/lessons/27/) trained you to catch which half of an
although-sentence an author is really defending. Saying one yourself,
live, is a different skill — you have to commit to the concession clause
before you've fully planned your real point, and keep going anyway.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Although the flight was delayed by two hours, we still made it to the
meeting on time.
:::

Notice how the voice rises slightly through "although the flight was
delayed," then settles into the real point after the comma — the
concession is spoken almost like a side note, and the second clause gets
the stronger, steadier stress.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
Think of something true about a choice you made that could sound like a
downside (cost, time, difficulty). When the timer starts, say an
although/despite sentence that admits that downside, then finishes with
why you still made the choice — don't plan the ending before you start
speaking the concession.
:::

:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Although the new route takes ten minutes longer, it avoids the
traffic that used to make me late every single day." Check that you
didn't trail off or restart after "although" — the sentence should keep
moving straight through to the second clause.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="despite" pos="preposition" register="formal" meaning="in spite of something, followed directly by a noun rather than a full clause (সত্ত্বেও)" collocation="despite the delay, despite the fact that, despite this" example="Despite the storm, the vessel berthed on time."
:::

::: vocabulary word="whereas" pos="conjunction" register="formal" meaning="a word for stating a plain contrast between two things, with no concession implied (পক্ষান্তরে)" collocation="whereas, contrast with whereas, whereas the other" example="Route A is quick, whereas Route B is cheap."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you keep speaking through the concession clause without a long pause?
- Was your real point clearly the stronger, more stressed half of the sentence?
- Did you use despite + noun correctly, or although + full clause, without mixing the two?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
