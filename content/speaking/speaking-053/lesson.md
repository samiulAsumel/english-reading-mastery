## Introduction

[Lesson 53](/lessons/53/) taught you to infer a technical word's meaning
from how it sits in a sentence rather than stopping to look it up. This
drill asks you to do the reverse, live: use a real term from your field and
explain it naturally enough that a listener with zero background picks up
the meaning without you ever saying "this word means...".

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The system went down because of a memory leak — basically, a part of the
program kept grabbing more memory and never giving it back, until there was
none left.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice "basically" signals the explanation is coming, and
the speaker's pace slows very slightly right after it — a natural spoken
cue that a technical listener never needs, but a general listener relies on.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick one technical term from your own field. Explain it out loud, live, for
a listener who has never heard it — fold the explanation naturally into
what you say (using "basically," "which means," or "in other words") rather
than stopping to give a formal definition.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — term, then a natural in-context explanation"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your explanation, then play it back and judge whether someone outside your field would actually understand it."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct term to choose. A strong answer sounds natural,
not textbook-stiff: "We had to roll back the update — basically, undo it
and go back to the version that was working before — because the new one
broke the login page." Notice the explanation is folded into the sentence
with a dash and "basically," not delivered as a separate, stand-alone
definition.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="on the fly" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="done immediately, without preparation (তাৎক্ষণিকভাবে)" collocation="on the fly, decide on the fly, explain on the fly" example="You often have to explain a technical term on the fly."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use one real technical term correctly, not a vague general word?
- Did your explanation stay folded into natural speech rather than sounding like a read-aloud definition?
- Would a listener outside your field actually follow it?
- Did you speak immediately, with no planning pause?
:::
