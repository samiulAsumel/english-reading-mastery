## Introduction

[Lesson 64](/lessons/64/) taught you to read "should," "must," "may,"
and "could" as precise signals, not interchangeable filler. This drill
asks you to give a live recommendation using the exact modal that
matches how strongly you mean it — decided on the spot, not planned in
advance.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Drivers must log every delivery on arrival. They should also confirm
the address by phone beforehand, unless the customer already confirmed
it in writing.
:::

Notice "must" and "should" carry slightly different weight in the
speaker's voice — "must" lands firmly, "should" a touch lighter — even
though grammatically they're both just modal verbs before a base form.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
You'll be given a short workplace scenario. When the timer starts,
speak one rule with "must," one recommendation with "should," and one
exception with "unless" — all in one connected answer.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Give your recommendation now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Give your recommendation, then record it and compare your modal choices against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer keeps "must" for
a genuine requirement and "should" for something short of that, and
adds the exception in the same breath rather than as an afterthought
tacked on at the end.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="requirement" pos="noun" meaning="something that must happen, not merely something advised — প্রয়োজনীয়তা" example="A signed form is a requirement before the shipment can leave."
:::

::: vocabulary word="advisable" pos="adjective" meaning="worth doing, though not strictly required — উপদেশযোগ্য" example="It's advisable to double-check the address, even though it isn't required."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did "must" and "should" come out with genuinely different weight, not used interchangeably?
- Did you state the exception in the same breath as the rule, not as a separate afterthought?
- Did you respond immediately, with no planning pause?
:::
