## Introduction

[Lesson 48](/lessons/48/) taught you to preview a chapter's structure —
title, headings, conclusion — before reading its body. This drill has you
give that same preview out loud, live: naming a topic's structure and
what each part will do, before you've actually explained anything.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Today I'll explain three things about the new schedule: why it changed,
what stays the same, and what you need to do next.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice the pattern: a number ("three
things"), then each one named in a short phrase — that's a spoken
preview, and it's exactly what a chapter's headings do on the page.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick a topic you could explain in three parts. In 30 seconds, say a
one-sentence preview naming all three parts and, briefly, the job each
one will do — before explaining any of the actual content.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — preview the structure, don't explain the content yet"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your three-part preview, then play it back and check it names a clear structure."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
"I want to explain three things about our new onboarding plan: the
problem we found, what we're changing, and how we'll measure whether it
worked." There's no single correct topic — what matters is a clear count,
three distinct parts, and a short phrase naming each part's job.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name exactly three distinct parts, not two or four?
- Did you avoid explaining any actual content — just the structure?
- Did you speak this live, without reading from notes?
:::
