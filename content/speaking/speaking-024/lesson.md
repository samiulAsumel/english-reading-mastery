## Introduction

[Lesson 24](/lessons/24/) taught you to read the exact strength of a
causal claim. Speaking one live is harder — under time pressure, the
easy habit is to reach for the strongest word, *caused*, even when the
evidence doesn't earn it. This drill trains the harder, more honest
habit.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Sales dropped the same month a competitor opened nearby, so the two are
probably related — but there isn't enough evidence yet to say the new
shop caused the drop.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it, paying attention to how naturally
*probably related* and *isn't enough evidence yet* carry the hedge —
they aren't awkward add-ons, they're the normal rhythm of careful
spoken English.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="10s"
Scenario: a website's traffic dropped the same week its main competitor
launched a big ad campaign. That's the only information you have. When
the timer starts, say one sentence describing the relationship — with
the right causal strength for evidence this weak.
:::

::: timer seconds="10" label="Speak your hedged causal sentence now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your sentence, then record it and check you avoided 'caused.'"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Something like: "The traffic drop is probably related to the ad
campaign, but there's no way to be sure it actually caused the drop
without more information." What matters is avoiding a flat "caused"
claim and speaking the hedge naturally, not as an awkward afterthought.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="related to" pos="collocation" meaning="connected to something, without specifying exactly how — সম্পর্কিত" example="The traffic drop is probably related to the competitor's ad campaign."
:::

::: vocabulary word="evidence" pos="noun" meaning="facts or information that support a conclusion — প্রমাণ" example="There isn't enough evidence yet to say the campaign caused the drop."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you avoid saying "caused" for evidence this weak?
- Did your hedge word come out naturally, with normal sentence stress?
- Did you speak within the time limit, without a long planning pause?
:::
