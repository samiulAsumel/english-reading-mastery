## Introduction

[Lesson 62](/lessons/62/) taught you to build a timeline and a causal
map for a passage in motion. This drill asks you to narrate change out
loud, live — stage by stage, with the causal links you actually mean to
claim, and none you don't.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Initially, the system ran on manual checks. Within a year, automated
alerts had been introduced, which cut response time significantly.
Around the same time, the team also grew — though that wasn't directly
caused by the automation.
:::

Notice how "which cut response time significantly" is spoken as one
connected phrase, immediately after the cause — that tight link is what
signals genuine causation, versus the looser "around the same time" for
events that just happened to overlap.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given a short scenario describing 3-4 stages of change in a
system, role, or process. When the timer starts, narrate it out loud in
order, using at least two time markers and one causal verb you can
justify — and explicitly flag anything you're not claiming caused the
next stage.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Narrate the sequence now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Narrate the sequence, then record it and compare your time markers and causal language against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer moves cleanly
through each stage with a time marker, lands one clear causal claim
("which led to," "triggered"), and explicitly separates any event that
merely happened around the same time from one it actually caused.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="stage" pos="noun" meaning="one step or phase within a longer process — পর্যায়" example="Describe each stage of the process before jumping to the outcome."
:::

::: vocabulary word="overlap" pos="verb" meaning="to happen at the same time as something else, without necessarily being connected to it — একই সময়ে ঘটা" example="The two changes happened to overlap, but one didn't cause the other."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you narrate the stages in order, using at least two explicit time markers?
- Did you use a causal verb only for a link you could actually justify?
- Did your narration flow as one connected explanation, without long pauses?
:::
