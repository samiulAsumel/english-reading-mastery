## Introduction

[Lesson 73](/lessons/73/) taught you to spot the assumption an author
never says out loud. This drill makes you catch your own — say an
argument, then immediately expose the gap in it yourself, live.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Sales rose after the new packaging launched. Therefore, the new
packaging caused the increase. Hidden assumption: nothing else changed
at the same time.
:::

Shadow this, paying attention to how flatly "hidden assumption" is
said — it's a plain label, not a dramatic reveal, because naming a gap
should feel like routine checking, not a gotcha.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Think of a change at your workplace and a claim someone made about its
effect. In 30 seconds, say the evidence, say the "therefore" conclusion,
then say "Hidden assumption:" and name what's being silently assumed.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — evidence, conclusion, hidden assumption"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your argument and the hidden assumption you named, then play it back and check the assumption is specific, not vague."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is a real, specific gap.
One natural version: "Employee turnover dropped after we introduced
flexible hours. Therefore, flexible hours reduced turnover. Hidden
assumption: nothing else affecting turnover — like a recent pay raise —
changed during the same period." Check that your own assumption names a
concrete alternative cause, not just "other factors."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="gap" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the missing logical step between evidence and a conclusion (যুক্তির ফাঁক)" collocation="logical gap, gap in the argument, bridge the gap" example="There is a gap between 'complaints fell' and 'customers are happy'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="expose" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to reveal something that was hidden or unstated (উন্মোচন করা)" collocation="expose a weakness, expose an assumption, be exposed" example="The question exposed the assumption behind the forecast."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your conclusion genuinely reach beyond what the evidence alone proves?
- Was the hidden assumption you named specific, not a vague "something else might explain it"?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause before naming the assumption?
:::
