## Introduction

[Lesson 28](/lessons/28/) trained you to read exactly how confident an
author is from their modal verbs and hedges. Speaking with that same
precision is harder — under time pressure, it's tempting to just say
"this is true" instead of the more honest "this might be true." This
drill trains you to reach for the right modal verb live, without
overclaiming.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
A recent internal report suggests that the new schedule may be reducing
overtime, though this hasn't been confirmed across every team yet.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice how "suggests," "may," and
"though this hasn't been confirmed" are spread across the whole
sentence — the hedge isn't one word bolted on at the end, it runs
through the entire claim.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Think of something you believe but aren't fully certain about (a change
at work, a habit, something you've heard secondhand). When the timer
starts, say the claim using one modal verb or hedging adverb (may,
might, possibly, seems to) and name the source if it isn't your own
direct experience. Don't let your voice sound more certain than your
words by the end of the sentence.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — one claim, one hedge, one honest source"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your hedged claim, then play it back and check whether your certainty stayed steady from first word to last."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct claim — what matters is the shape. For
example: "I've heard from a couple of colleagues that the new shift
system might be cutting down on late arrivals, though I haven't seen the
actual numbers myself." Check that your hedge word appeared early, not
tacked on as an afterthought, and that your source was named honestly.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="modal verb" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="a verb like may, might, or could that marks how certain a statement is (মোডাল ক্রিয়া)" collocation="use a modal verb, modal verb such as, weak modal verb" example="'Might' is a weaker modal verb than 'will'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="overclaim" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to state something with more confidence than the evidence actually supports (অতিরিক্ত দাবি করা)" collocation="overclaim the results, risk overclaiming, accused of overclaiming" example="Saying the system 'eliminates errors' after one month would overclaim."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your modal verb or hedge word appear early in the sentence, not as a late add-on?
- Did you name your source when the claim wasn't from your own direct experience?
- Did your spoken confidence stay level, without sounding surer by the sentence's end?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
