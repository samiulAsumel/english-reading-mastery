## Introduction

[Lesson 17](/lessons/17/) taught you that "may cause" and "causes" are
two different claims. This drill asks you to make that same choice live
— hearing a scenario and instantly choosing, out loud, the modal or hedge
word that matches how sure you actually are.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The delay may have been caused by heavy traffic near the port.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice "may" gets
nearly the same stress as "caused" — in fluent speech, a hedge word is
never mumbled, because it's carrying real information about how
confident the speaker is.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="10s"
Scenario: you see the office lights off and the door locked, but you
don't know for certain why. When the timer starts, say one sentence
giving a properly hedged explanation — out loud, immediately, no
planning pause.
:::

::: timer seconds="10" label="Speak your hedged explanation now"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "The office may be closed for the
holiday" and "The staff have probably left early today" are both
natural. What matters is a real modal or hedge word landing naturally,
spoken immediately, rather than a flat claim stated as certain fact.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="locked" pos="adjective" meaning="fastened shut, usually with a lock — তালাবদ্ধ" example="The door was locked, so the office may be closed today."
:::

::: vocabulary word="holiday" pos="noun" meaning="a day of rest or celebration when normal work stops — ছুটির দিন" example="The staff have probably left early because of the holiday."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your sentence include a genuine modal or hedge word, spoken with normal stress?
- Did you speak immediately, with no pause to plan the sentence?
- Did you avoid stating the cause as a confirmed fact when the scenario gave you no proof?
:::
