## Introduction

[Lesson 60](/lessons/60/) taught you that a bare "System A is better" is
an incomplete claim until the dimension — speed, cost, reliability — is
named. Making that comparison out loud, live, is the real test: it's easy
to reach for "better" as a shortcut when you're speaking under time
pressure, and this drill forces you to name the exact dimension instead.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Compared with the old app, the new one is much faster to load. It says
nothing about battery use, though — that's a completely different
dimension.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice how "faster to load" and "battery use" both get a
clear stress — naming the specific dimension, twice, is what keeps the
comparison from collapsing into a vague "better."

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Compare two things you know well, out loud. Name the one specific dimension
you're comparing them along, state the difference using a precise contrast
word like "whereas" or "compared with," and end by naming one other
dimension your comparison doesn't address.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — name the dimension, state the contrast, then name what it doesn't cover"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct comparison to pick — what matters is naming the
dimension clearly. A strong answer sounds like: "Compared with our old
supplier, the new one is much more reliable — on-time deliveries went from
about 70% to over 95%. That's about reliability specifically, though. It
says nothing about price, and actually the new supplier charges a bit more,
so whereas the old one won on cost, the new one wins on reliability." One
named dimension, a clear contrast word, and an honest limit — all live.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="on that front" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="regarding that specific dimension or aspect just mentioned (ওই ক্ষেত্রে)" collocation="on that front, progress on that front, nothing new on that front" example="On that front, the terminal has made real progress."
:::

::: vocabulary word="compared with" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="introduces the baseline something is being measured against (তুলনায়)" collocation="compared with, compared with last year, favourably compared with" example="Compared with last year, waiting times have fallen by a fifth."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the specific dimension your comparison was made along, out loud?
- Did you use a precise contrast word rather than a vague "better than"?
- Did you name a dimension your comparison didn't address, live?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause to plan first?
:::
