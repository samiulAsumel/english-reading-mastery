## Introduction

[Lesson 44](/lessons/44/) taught you to read a paragraph as claim,
evidence, and qualification, in that order. Saying one live is a
genuine test of extended spoken explanation: you have to hold all three
moves in mind at once, in the right sequence, while speaking at normal
pace with no script.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Automated scheduling can reduce waiting times. One facility cut delays
by fifteen percent after switching. But that drop might also reflect
other staffing changes made at the same time.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice the short pause
before "but" — that pause is the spoken signal that the qualification is
starting, the same job "however" does in writing.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
You'll be given a short topic. In 30 seconds, speak a claim about it,
back it with one example, and close with a qualification (using
however, although, or but) that limits how much the example actually
proves.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak your claim, example, and qualification now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Speak your explanation, then record it and check whether all three moves — claim, example, qualification — were clearly present."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Given the topic *a warehouse introduced barcode scanning*: *"Barcode
scanning can reduce picking errors. One warehouse cut mistakes by nearly
a third after adopting it. However, that same warehouse also retrained
its staff at the same time, so the scanners alone can't take full
credit."* There is no single correct answer here — what matters is that
the claim, the example, and the qualification are all present, in
order, and spoken fluently without a long pause before the qualification
begins.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="qualification" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="a statement that limits or narrows an earlier, broader claim (শর্তারোপ)" collocation="add a qualification, important qualification, with one qualification" example="I would add one qualification: this applies only to peak season."
:::

::: vocabulary word="illustration" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="a single concrete example, weaker on its own than systematic evidence (দৃষ্টান্ত)" collocation="a vivid illustration, illustration of, serve as an illustration" example="One illustration is last month's delayed shipment."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak all three moves — claim, example, qualification — in order?
- Did a spoken connector (however/although/but) clearly signal the start of the qualification?
- Did your qualification genuinely limit the claim, rather than just repeating it?
- Did you speak fluently within the time limit, without a long pause to plan the ending?
:::
