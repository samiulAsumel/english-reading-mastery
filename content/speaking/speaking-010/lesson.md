## Introduction

[Lesson 10](/lessons/10/) taught you that a fluent writer almost never
repeats the same noun twice in a row — a pronoun carries it forward
instead. In speech, native speakers go one step further: pronouns get
said quickly and lightly, almost swallowed into the words around them.
This drill trains both the grammar and that reduced, natural rhythm.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The manager checked the report. She approved it. The team finished the
task. They left early.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it — notice how lightly "she," "it," and
"they" are said compared to the nouns before them. A pronoun almost
never carries the stress of the sentence; the noun it replaces already
did that job one sentence earlier.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="7s"
Say one short sentence naming a person or thing. Immediately after,
without pausing, say a second sentence about it using only a pronoun —
no repeating the noun. Do this five times.
:::

::: timer seconds="7" label="Say sentence one, then sentence two with a pronoun — no repeated noun"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say five noun-then-pronoun sentence pairs out loud, then compare your rhythm to the shadowed model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct pair — "The driver arrived late. He apologized
right away." or "The containers were damaged. They had to be replaced."
both work. What matters is that the second sentence used a pronoun
instead of repeating the noun, and the pronoun's number matched.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="reduced" pos="adjective" register="technical" meaning="pronounced lightly and quickly, without stress (লঘুভাবে উচ্চারিত)" collocation="reduced form, reduced pronunciation, reduced vowel" example="In fast speech, 'them' is reduced to 'em'."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your second sentence use a pronoun instead of repeating the noun?
- Did the pronoun's number correctly match its antecedent?
- Did your shadowed pronouns sound light and quick, not stressed like the noun before them?
:::
