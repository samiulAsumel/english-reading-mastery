## Introduction

[Lesson 67](/lessons/67/) taught you to read conditional language by
its logical direction, not just its surface words. This drill asks you
to state a conditional rule out loud, live, getting the direction right
under time pressure — no chance to plan it on paper first.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
If payment is received before the deadline, the order ships the same
day. But the order can only be shipped if the address has been
verified.
:::

Notice how "only if" is spoken as a tight, fixed unit — never split
apart or paused in the middle — since breaking it up would blur the
exact condition being named.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
You'll be given a short scenario. When the timer starts, state one rule
using "if... then" for a sufficient condition, and one using "only if"
for a necessary condition — getting the direction right for each, with
no planning pause.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="State the rules now — no planning, just speak"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer keeps "if" for a
condition that genuinely guarantees the outcome, and "only if" for a
condition that's required but doesn't by itself guarantee anything —
and doesn't mix the two up under pressure.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="condition" pos="noun" meaning="a requirement that must be met before something else can happen — শর্ত" example="Verification is a condition for entry, not a guarantee of it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="guarantee" pos="verb" meaning="to promise an outcome will definitely happen — নিশ্চয়তা দেওয়া" example="Wearing a vest doesn't guarantee entry — the induction is also required."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your "if" statement genuinely claim sufficiency, not just correlation?
- Did your "only if" statement avoid overclaiming that the condition guarantees the outcome?
- Did you respond immediately, with no planning pause?
:::
