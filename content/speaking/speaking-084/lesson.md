## Introduction

[Lesson 84](/lessons/84/) taught you to evaluate an argument on three
separate levels rather than giving one vague verdict. This drill forces
you to hold all three levels in mind at once while speaking — much
harder than writing them out one at a time, since there's no chance to
go back and check you covered all three.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The evidence itself is fine for a small survey. It does support a narrow
finding. But the conclusion goes much further than the evidence can
carry it.
:::

Notice each of the three sentences carries one judgment — reliability,
support, justification — spoken as three distinct beats, not blurred
into one.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
A survey of 25 dockworkers at one terminal finds 70% favor a four-day
work week. The report concludes the whole shipping industry should
adopt it. When the timer starts, evaluate this out loud on all three
levels: is the evidence reliable, does it support the finding, is the
conclusion justified?
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Evaluate all three levels now — out loud"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Give your three-level evaluation, then record it and compare against the model answer."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer sounds like:
"The evidence is reliable for what it is — a small, honest survey. It
does support the narrow claim that most surveyed workers want a
four-day week. But the conclusion that the whole industry should adopt
it isn't justified — twenty-five workers at one terminal can't represent
a global industry." What matters is hitting all three levels distinctly,
without collapsing them into one sentence.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="represent" pos="verb" meaning="for a small sample to stand in accurately for a much larger group — প্রতিনিধিত্ব করা" example="Twenty-five workers cannot represent an entire industry."
:::

::: vocabulary word="finding" pos="noun" meaning="a specific result a study actually shows, narrower than its conclusion — ফলাফল" example="The finding was narrow; the conclusion was not."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you address reliability, support, and justification as three distinct judgments?
- Did you avoid a single flat verdict?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long silent-planning pause?
:::
