## Introduction

[Lesson 55](/lessons/55/) taught you to interrogate a number before
accepting its conclusion. Talking about data out loud is harder than
writing about it, because there's no time to double-check the comparison
before the words are already out — you have to reach for precision on the
first try.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Response times dropped by almost half compared to last quarter. That's a
real improvement in speed — it doesn't necessarily mean customers are
happier, since we haven't measured that separately.
:::

Shadow this once. Notice how "doesn't necessarily mean" is said slightly
more carefully and slowly — a spoken signal of appropriate hedging, not
hesitation.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="30s"
Pick a number you actually know — a statistic, a score, a metric from work
or study. Say it out loud, state what it measures and what it's compared
with, and give one conclusion it genuinely supports, without stretching it
further than the evidence allows.
:::

::: timer seconds="30" label="Speak now — the number, what it measures, what it supports"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your answer, then check whether your conclusion actually matches what the number shows."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct number to pick — what matters is the structure.
A strong answer sounds like: "Attendance was up 20% this term compared to
last term. That tells us more people showed up — it doesn't tell us why,
so I wouldn't claim the new schedule caused it without checking other
factors too." The number, the comparison, and the honestly limited
conclusion are all there, live.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="hedge" pos="verb" meaning="to soften a claim so it doesn't overstate what the evidence supports — সতর্কভাবে দাবি করা" example="A careful speaker hedges: 'this suggests,' not 'this proves.'"
:::

::: vocabulary word="stretch" pos="verb" meaning="(figurative) to push a conclusion further than the evidence actually allows — বেশি দূর টেনে নেওয়া" example="Don't stretch one good month into a claim about the whole year."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state a real number and what it measures?
- Did you give a comparison point that makes the number meaningful?
- Did your spoken conclusion stay within what the number actually supports?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long planning pause?
:::
