## Introduction

[Lesson 80](/lessons/80/) taught you to find a chapter's central question and
compress its spine into one sentence — on the page, where you can revise it
as many times as you like. This drill takes the same compression and forces
it out loud, live, the first time you say it. There's no draft to polish;
whatever question and answer you find, you have to say them as you find
them.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Central question: does automating berth scheduling actually pay off?
Answer: it pays off in high-volume ports, but the benefit depends almost
entirely on cargo volume, so the fix is conditional, not universal.
:::

Shadow this once, then again. Notice the question and its answer arrive in
one continuous breath — the speaker doesn't pause to plan between naming the
question and giving the compressed answer — and listen for the small extra
stress on "conditional," the word carrying the whole point.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="60s"
Think of a topic you know well with at least three connected points — a
process, a technology, a policy. When the timer starts, say the single
central question all of those points are really answering, then say a
compressed spoken answer, no more than three sentences, built from those
points — live, with no drafting first.
:::

::: timer seconds="60" label="Speak now — central question, then your compressed answer"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is the shape. One natural
version: "Central question: what actually determines whether a new
scheduling system reduces missed shifts? Answer: mostly staff training
before rollout and clean integration with existing payroll tools — without
both, the system's theoretical benefits rarely show up, and missed shifts
can even rise during a rough transition." Check that your own answer stayed
under three sentences and didn't drift into a fourth, unplanned point.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="spine" pos="noun" meaning="the single underlying question or argument that structures a longer piece of writing or speech — মেরুদণ্ড/মূল কাঠামো" example="She found the talk's spine in ten seconds and compressed it on the spot."
:::

::: vocabulary word="off the cuff" pos="phrase" meaning="said without preparation or notes, spontaneously — পূর্ব প্রস্তুতি ছাড়া" example="He gave the compressed answer off the cuff, with no time to plan it first."

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your central question genuinely capture what all your points were answering together?
- Did your spoken answer stay to three sentences and read as one coherent position, not a list?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause to plan the sentence first?
:::
