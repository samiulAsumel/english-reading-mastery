## Introduction

[Lesson 72](/lessons/72/) taught you to spot an argument's working
parts on the page. This drill makes you build one live — say it, then
immediately name each piece out loud, with no time to plan the labels
in advance.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Late shipments damage client trust. That's the premise. Complaint
records show a rise in late shipments last month. That's the evidence.
This suggests trust may already be slipping. That's the inference. So
the shipping process needs review. That's the conclusion.
:::

Shadow this once, then try it again faster and more naturally — notice
how "that's the premise / that's the evidence" becomes a short, fixed
labelling phrase you can reuse for any argument.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="40s"
Pick a workplace problem you know well. In 40 seconds, say a premise, a
piece of evidence, an inference, and a conclusion about it — labelling
each one right after you say it ("that's the premise," and so on).
:::

::: timer seconds="40" label="Speak now — build the argument, label each part as you go"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your labelled argument, then play it back and check every label matched the right sentence."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is the structure. One
natural version: "Customers wait too long at checkout during peak hours.
That's the premise. Yesterday's data showed average wait times over
eight minutes. That's the evidence. This suggests we're losing sales
during rush periods. That's the inference. So we should add a second
checkout counter at peak times. That's the conclusion." Check that your
own conclusion didn't overreach what your evidence actually supports.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="label" pos="verb" meaning="to name what kind of statement something is, right after saying it — চিহ্নিত করা" example="She labelled each sentence as she went: premise, evidence, inference, conclusion."
:::

::: vocabulary word="overreach" pos="verb" meaning="to claim more than the evidence actually supports — সীমা অতিক্রম করা" example="A conclusion that overreaches its evidence sounds confident but isn't earned."

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you say all four parts in order without a long planning pause?
- Did you label each part immediately after saying it, not all at the end?
- Did your conclusion stay honestly matched to what your evidence actually showed?
:::
