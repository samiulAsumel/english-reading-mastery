## Introduction

[Lesson 37](/lessons/37/) taught you to unpack dense written sentences.
Speaking naturally compresses too — a fluent speaker doesn't say two flat
sentences back to back when one combined sentence, with the right pause
and emphasis, says the same thing more naturally.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The engineer, a specialist in port automation, redesigned the gate
system.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it, matching how the voice drops slightly and
speeds up through the appositive — "a specialist in port automation" —
before returning to normal pace and stress for "redesigned the gate
system."

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
You'll hear two plain facts about a person. In 15 seconds, combine them
out loud into one sentence using an appositive — pause slightly before
and after the inserted phrase, the way the shadowed model did.
:::

::: timer seconds="15" label="Speak the combined sentence now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your combined sentence, then record it and compare your pausing to the shadowed model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence, but the shape should match: *"The
supervisor, a twelve-year veteran of the terminal, signed off on the
new schedule."* What matters is a clear pause before and after the
appositive, and that the sentence still makes sense with the appositive
removed.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="appositive" pos="noun" meaning="a noun phrase placed beside another to explain or identify it — ব্যাখ্যামূলক বিশেষ্যপদ" example="Saying 'the engineer, a specialist in automation,' inserts a spoken appositive."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you pause naturally before and after the appositive, rather than running it flat into the sentence?
- Is the combined sentence still grammatical if you mentally remove the appositive?
- Did you speak within the time limit, without planning the whole sentence silently first?
:::
