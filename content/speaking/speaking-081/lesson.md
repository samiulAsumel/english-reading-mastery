## Introduction

[Lesson 81](/lessons/81/) taught you to compress a whole book into one
qualified thesis sentence on the page, where you can revise it. This
drill takes the same compression and forces it out loud, live, the first
time you say it — the real test of whether you actually understood the
book's structure or just recognized the words for it.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Automating berth scheduling reduces turnaround time in high-volume
ports, but the gains shrink sharply once cargo volume drops below a
certain threshold.
:::

Notice how the sentence doesn't pause between the claim and its limit —
"but the gains shrink" arrives without a breath, because in fluent
academic speech the qualification is part of the same thought, not an
afterthought tacked on.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
A short book argues that automating berth scheduling cut turnaround
time by 30% in three large ports, but barely helped two smaller ports —
because the real driver was cargo volume, not the technology. When the
timer starts, say the book's thesis out loud in one sentence: a
position, its scope, and an honest limit.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Say the qualified thesis now — one sentence, out loud"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say the thesis, then record it and check it against the model answer below."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer sounds like:
"Automating berth scheduling speeds up large, high-volume ports, but the
book shows that benefit mostly disappears in smaller ports, so the fix
is conditional, not universal." What matters is that the limit arrived
in the same breath as the claim, not as a separate, hesitant add-on.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="conditional" pos="adjective" register="formal" meaning="true only under a certain condition, not universally (শর্তসাপেক্ষ)" collocation="conditional claim, conditional approval, conditional on" example="The thesis is conditional: it holds only for large terminals."
:::

::: vocabulary word="compress" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to state something long in a much shorter form without losing its meaning (সংক্ষিপ্ত করা)" collocation="compress an explanation, compress into one sentence, compress without losing meaning" example="Compress the book's argument into a single spoken sentence."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state a real position, not just the topic?
- Did the scope/limit arrive in the same sentence, without a long pause?
- Did you speak immediately, without silently rehearsing the sentence first?
:::
