## Introduction

[Lesson 31](/lessons/31/) trained you to find the unwritten idea an
argument secretly depends on. Finding your own hidden assumption live,
while you're still speaking the argument, is a harder and more honest
skill — there's no time to hide it. This drill forces the assumption out
into the open, in your own voice.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Sales rose after we added self-checkout, so self-checkout increased
sales. That depends on nothing else explaining the rise — and if the
store also extended its hours that month, this doesn't hold up.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice how "that depends on" is spoken
almost like the speaker is thinking aloud — a genuine, unrehearsed pivot
into naming the assumption, not a polished script.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Think of a short argument you could make about work, a habit, or a
decision — evidence leading to a conclusion. When the timer starts, say
the argument, then immediately say "that depends on..." and name the
hidden assumption it relies on, then finish with what happens to your
conclusion if that assumption turns out false.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Speak now — argument, then 'that depends on...', then the negation"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Turnaround times improved the same month we installed the new
scheduling software, so the software must be why. That depends on
nothing else changing that month — and if vessel traffic was also lower
because of a seasonal lull, this conclusion doesn't hold up as cleanly."
Check that you actually said the negation out loud, not just implied it.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="depends on" pos="phrase" register="neutral" meaning="a spoken pivot phrase for introducing the hidden assumption an argument relies on (নির্ভর করে)" collocation="it depends on, largely depends on, entirely depends on" example="Our forecast depends on the assumption that fuel prices stay flat."
:::

::: vocabulary word="hold up" pos="phrasal verb" register="neutral" meaning="to remain valid or convincing under closer examination (টিকে থাকা)" collocation="hold up under scrutiny, hold up well, may not hold up" example="That conclusion may not hold up if the sample was biased."
:::

- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
