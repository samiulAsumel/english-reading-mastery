## Introduction

[Lesson 86](/lessons/86/) taught you to reconstruct how an author's
distributed ideas relate to each other, not just list what they said. This
drill forces that same synthesis out loud, live — combining two sources
that don't fully agree, with no time to plan a clean paragraph first.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Taken together, the pilot's ten percent drop and the larger study's null
result don't really contradict each other — they suggest the signage only
helps where staff turnover stays low.
:::

Listen for how "taken together" carries the whole synthesis move by itself
— a listener knows immediately that two separate findings are about to be
combined, not just reported one after another.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Source A: a six-month pilot at one warehouse found new signage cut picking
errors by 10%. Source B: a larger twelve-warehouse study found no
significant change, and noted that results depended heavily on staff
turnover. When the timer starts, say a single spoken synthesis — not two
separate summaries — that states the relationship between these two
findings and preserves both of their hedges.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak your synthesis now — one connected account, not two summaries"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your synthesis, then compare it against the model answer below."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct wording, but a strong answer sounds like: "These
two studies don't really disagree — the pilot's ten percent improvement and
the bigger study's null result both fit if staff turnover is the real
variable, since the larger, more turnover-affected sample simply didn't
share the stable conditions the pilot had." What matters is that you spoke
one combined account, out loud, rather than reporting the pilot, pausing,
and then reporting the larger study as a separate, unconnected fact.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="taken together" pos="phrase" register="formal" meaning="a spoken signal that separate findings are about to be combined into one conclusion (একত্রে বিবেচনা করলে)" collocation="taken together, the findings, taken together these results" example="Taken together, the two reports suggest a link."
:::

::: vocabulary word="moderating factor" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="a condition that changes how strongly, or whether, an effect appears (প্রভাব-নিয়ন্ত্রক উপাদান)" collocation="a moderating factor, key moderating factor, moderating factor such as" example="Experience is a moderating factor: senior staff make fewer errors under pressure."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state one explicit relationship between the two sources, rather than reporting them as two separate summaries?
- Did your spoken synthesis preserve both sources' hedges and conditions?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause to plan the sentence first?
:::
