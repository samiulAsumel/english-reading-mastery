## Introduction

[Lesson 32](/lessons/32/) trained you to test a causal claim by
imagining the world where the cause never happened. Asking that question
live, right after making the claim, is the harder skill — most speakers
just state the result and stop. This drill trains you to keep going into
the counterfactual question yourself.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Complaints dropped after the new training program started. But would
they have dropped anyway, since last month's number was unusually high
to begin with?
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice the rising pitch on "would they
have dropped anyway" — a real question, not a rhetorical throwaway, with
genuine curiosity in the voice.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="20s"
Think of a real or plausible before/after result you know something
about. When the timer starts, state the result without letting "after"
imply "because," then immediately ask the specific counterfactual
question that would test it — "would this have happened anyway if...?"
Mention regression to the mean if your starting point was unusually
extreme.
:::

::: timer seconds="20" label="Speak now — the result, then the counterfactual question"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your before/after claim and your counterfactual question, then check whether the question was specific, not vague."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Yard incidents fell for three months after we started
mandatory safety briefings. But would they have fallen anyway, since the
month before the change had an unusually high number of incidents to
begin with? That's regression to the mean, and it might explain part of
this on its own." Check that your question named the specific cause you
were testing, not a generic "I wonder why."
:::

- Did you mention regression to the mean if your starting point was unusually extreme?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
