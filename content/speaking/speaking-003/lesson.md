## Introduction

[Lesson 3](/lessons/3/) taught you that *can learn*, *has finished*, and
*is influenced* are each one verb phrase, not separate words to
translate one at a time. Reading them that way is one skill; producing
them that fast, out loud, is another. This drill trains your mouth to
say the whole chunk as a single unit.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
People can learn. She has finished. The report was reviewed. They should
practice.
:::

Listen once without speaking. Then shadow it, noticing how tightly the
two words of each verb phrase run together — *can learn* almost sounds
like one word, not two.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="6s"
Pick one of these patterns at random before the timer starts: a modal
(*can/should/must*), a helping verb (*has/have*), or a passive form
(*is/was*). When the timer starts, say one full sentence using that
verb phrase, immediately. Do this five times, mixing the three patterns.
:::

::: timer seconds="6" label="Say the full verb phrase as one unit — don't pause between its two words"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say five sentences using modal, helping-verb, and passive verb phrases, then compare your speed to the shadowed model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "We must finish," "He has
arrived," and "The cargo was checked" are all correct examples of the
three patterns. What matters is that each verb phrase came out as one
smooth unit, not as two separately pronounced words with a pause
between them.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="modal" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="a helping verb such as can, should, or must that adds possibility, necessity, or advice (মোডাল ক্রিয়া)" collocation="modal verb, use a modal, modal of possibility" example="'Should' is a modal that turns an order into advice."

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did each verb phrase come out as one connected unit, with no pause in the middle?
- Did you produce at least one example each of a modal, a helping verb, and a passive form?
- Did your shadowed pace get noticeably closer to natural speed on your second attempt?
:::
