## Introduction

Greetings are the first thing you say in almost every English
conversation, and also the thing that most reveals a translated,
hesitant speaker versus a fluent one — because a native speaker doesn't
think about "How are you?", they just say it, fast, with barely any
stress on any single word. This lesson is pure rhythm training: no new
grammar, just repetition until it stops sounding like a translated
phrase and starts sounding like a reflex.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Hi, how are you? I'm good, thanks. How about you? Not bad. Good to see
you. See you later.
:::

Listen once, all the way through, without trying to speak. Then play it
again and speak along, matching the rhythm exactly — including how fast
"how are you" runs together as almost one sound, and how "not bad" is
said quickly and lightly, not slowly and carefully like a translated
sentence.

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Nice to meet you. Thanks for coming. No problem at all. Take care.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="6s"
Someone says "Hi, how are you?" to you. When you start the timer, say
your reply out loud immediately — don't plan the sentence first, just
respond the way you would to a real greeting.
:::

::: timer seconds="6" label="Reply now — no planning, just respond"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Shadow both phrases above, then record your own version and compare the rhythm."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct reply — "I'm good, thanks. How about you?" and
"Not bad, thanks" are both natural. What matters is that you replied
immediately, without a pause to plan the sentence, and that your rhythm
matched the shadowed model reasonably closely.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="stress" pos="noun" meaning="extra emphasis given to certain syllables or words in speech — শব্দে জোর" example="Native speakers put stress on 'good' in 'I'm good, thanks.'"
:::

::: vocabulary word="reflex" pos="noun" meaning="an automatic response that happens without conscious thought — প্রতিবর্ত ক্রিয়া" example="A greeting should become a reflex, not something you plan."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your shadowed version keep pace with the recording, not lag half a sentence behind?
- Did you reply to the greeting instantly, with no planning pause?
- Did you notice which words the model said quickly versus which it stressed?
:::
