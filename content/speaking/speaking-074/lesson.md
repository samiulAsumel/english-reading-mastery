## Introduction

[Lesson 74](/lessons/74/) taught you to ask "is this evidence enough?"
rather than just "does evidence exist?" This drill puts that judgment
under a timer — no rehearsing your verdict in advance.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Three customers complained about slow delivery this month. Therefore,
our delivery service has gotten worse. That judgment is weak — three
complaints out of an unknown total isn't enough to prove a system-wide
decline.
:::

Shadow this twice. Notice the judgment doesn't just say "weak" — it
immediately gives the reason, in the same breath.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="35s"
Think of a piece of evidence and a conclusion from your own work or
studies. In 35 seconds, state the evidence, state the conclusion, then
judge out loud whether the evidence is sufficient, weak, or mixed — and
why.
:::

::: timer seconds="35" label="Speak now — evidence, conclusion, judgment with a reason"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your evidence, conclusion, and judgment, then check your reason was specific, not just 'because it's not enough.'"
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is a real judgment with a
reason attached. One natural version: "Two of our twelve warehouses
reported shortages last month. Therefore, the inventory system needs a
full redesign. That's weak — two out of twelve is a small fraction, and
we don't know if those two share a local cause instead of a system-wide
one." Check that your own verdict names a specific weakness, not a vague
"it's not enough evidence."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="verdict" pos="noun" meaning="a judgment reached after weighing evidence — রায়/সিদ্ধান্ত" example="Her verdict on the evidence was that it was suggestive but not conclusive."
:::

::: vocabulary word="fraction" pos="noun" meaning="a small part of a whole — অংশ" example="Two out of twelve warehouses is a small fraction of the total."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you speak the evidence, conclusion, and judgment without a long planning pause?
- Did your judgment include an actual reason, not just a bare word like "weak"?
- Was your reasoning about the evidence's size, relevance, or reliability — not something unrelated?
:::
