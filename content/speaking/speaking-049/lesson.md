## Introduction

[Lesson 49](/lessons/49/) taught you to reconstruct an author's overall
position and the reasons behind it before evaluating any of it. This
drill flips that into production: state your own position out loud, live,
and immediately back it with two distinct reasons — no time to write
anything down first.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
I think we should switch to a four-day work week. First, the data shows
productivity doesn't actually drop. Second, our own team survey found
this was the change people wanted most.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice the signposting words — "I think,"
"First," "Second" — they carry almost as much structural weight as the
content itself, and they're what let a listener follow an unscripted
argument.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="45s"
Pick something you actually believe — about your work, your studies, or
daily life. In 45 seconds, state your position in one sentence, then give
two distinct reasons for it, out loud, live. Use "First" and "Second" to
mark them clearly.
:::

::: timer seconds="45" label="Speak now — state your position, then give two reasons"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your position and two reasons, then play it back and check the reasons are genuinely different."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct position. What matters is a clear, arguable
first sentence, followed by two reasons that are genuinely distinct from
each other — not the same point said twice — delivered without a long
planning pause beforehand.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="signpost" pos="verb" register="technical" meaning="to use a word or phrase that shows a listener where an explanation is going (নির্দেশক শব্দ ব্যবহার করা)" collocation="signpost the transition, signpost clearly, signpost such as" example="Use a signpost like 'my main point is' so the listener knows where you are going."
:::

::: vocabulary word="arguable" pos="adjective" register="formal" meaning="able to be reasonably disagreed with (তর্কযোগ্য)" collocation="arguable claim, highly arguable, it is arguable that" example="That the merger cut costs is arguable, since the figures cover only six months."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Was your opening sentence a genuine, arguable position?
- Did your two reasons say something genuinely different from each other?
- Did you speak without a long pause to plan the reasons first?
:::
