## Introduction

[Lesson 39](/lessons/39/) taught you to resolve what a pronoun points to
when reading. A native speaker never repeats a noun three times in one
breath — they lean on "it" and "this" constantly, and a listener tracks
the reference automatically. This drill builds that same habit out loud.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The company introduced a new scheduling system. It reduced delays right
away. This improvement, though, came with a learning curve for staff.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice how naturally "it" and "this
improvement" replace "the scheduling system" — a native speaker never
even considers repeating the full noun phrase a second time.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
You'll be given one entity: a new mobile app. In 15 seconds, say three
short connected sentences about it — introduce it once by name, then
refer back to it twice using only pronouns or demonstratives, never
repeating "the app" again.
:::

::: timer seconds="15" label="Speak your three sentences now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your three sentences, then record them and check you never repeated the noun."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
*"The company launched a new mobile app last month. It quickly became
the most-downloaded tool in its category. This success surprised even
the development team."* "It" and "this success" carry the reference
forward — what matters is fluency and that a listener never loses track
of what you're talking about.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="reference" pos="noun" meaning="a word that points back to something already mentioned, instead of repeating it — নির্দেশ" example="'It' is a reference back to 'the app' in the previous sentence."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you avoid repeating the entity's name after introducing it once?
- Would a listener always know exactly what "it" or "this" referred to?
- Did you speak all three sentences fluently within the time limit?
:::
