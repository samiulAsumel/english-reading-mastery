## Introduction

[Lesson 23](/lessons/23/) taught you to trace how writers keep one idea
alive across a paragraph without repeating the same noun. Fluent
speakers do the same thing automatically, in real time — this drill asks
you to do it live, with no chance to plan your pronouns and synonyms in
advance.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
The company launched a new app last month. It quickly became popular
with customers, and this success has encouraged the team to plan a
second version.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice how *it*, *this success*, and *the
team* each keep the sentence moving without a single repeated noun —
that's the rhythm to match, not just the words.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
Pick something you know well — your job, a hobby, your phone. When the
timer starts, speak three connected sentences about it out loud: name it
in the first sentence, then refer back to it in the next two using a
pronoun and then a different word or phrase — no repeating the same
noun.
:::

::: timer seconds="15" label="Speak your three connected sentences now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Speak your three sentences, then record and listen back for repeated nouns."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
"I work at a shipping company. It handles container tracking for
several ports. This job has taught me more about logistics than I
expected." There's no single correct topic — what matters is that the
second and third sentences never repeat the first sentence's exact
noun.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="popular" pos="adjective" register="neutral" meaning="liked or used by many people (জনপ্রিয়)" collocation="popular choice, increasingly popular, popular with" example="Online booking is popular with drivers because it saves time."
:::

::: vocabulary word="encourage" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to make someone more likely to do something (উৎসাহিত করা)" collocation="encourage staff, strongly encourage, encourage to" example="The company encourages drivers to book slots in advance."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name your topic directly in the first sentence only?
- Did the next two sentences use a pronoun and a different word, not the same noun again?
- Did you speak all three sentences without a long pause between them?
:::
