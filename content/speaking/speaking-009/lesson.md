## Introduction

[Lesson 9](/lessons/9/) taught you that "certainly," "probably," and
"possibly" make three genuinely different claims. In conversation, most
learners default to a flat, unhedged statement because it's the fastest
thing to say — this drill trains you to reach for the honest level of
confidence instead, without slowing down.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
This is certainly the reason. This is probably the reason. This is
possibly the reason.
:::

Same five words at the end of each sentence, three completely different
claims. Shadow all three and notice how the certainty word carries the
real weight of the sentence, even though it's said quickly.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="7s"
Think of something you're not 100% sure about — why a delay happened,
whether a plan will work, what caused a problem. When the timer starts,
say your answer with a certainty adverb that honestly matches how sure
you really are.
:::

::: timer seconds="7" label="Answer now — pick the honest certainty word, not the fastest one"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Answer three uncertain questions out loud, each with a different certainty level, then play yourself back."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "This is probably the cause" and
"This is certainly the cause" are both correct if they match your real
confidence. What matters is that you consciously picked the certainty
word instead of defaulting to a flat statement out of habit.
:::
