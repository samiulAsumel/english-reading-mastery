## Introduction

[Lesson 20](/lessons/20/) taught you to read a paragraph's claim,
reason, and conclusion as one connected argument. This drill asks you to
build a small version of that same architecture live — speaking a claim,
a reason, and a conclusion, signposted the moment each one leaves your
mouth.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Regular breaks improve focus, because the mind gets tired after long,
uninterrupted work. So a short walk every hour is worth the time it
costs.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice "because" and
"so" each get a small stress of their own — they're not filler words
here, they're the signposts telling the listener exactly how the two
ideas connect.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="15s"
When the timer starts, say a three-part mini-argument out loud about one
habit you think is useful: a claim, a reason signposted with "because,"
and a conclusion signposted with "so" or "therefore." Speak immediately
— no planning pause, and keep all three parts connected as one spoken
argument.
:::

::: timer seconds="15" label="Speak your mini-argument now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your mini-argument, then record it and compare against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Something close to: "Writing a short summary after reading helps you
remember it, because putting an idea into your own words forces you to
actually understand it. So a two-minute summary is worth the extra
time." There's no single correct wording — what matters is that claim,
reason, and conclusion came out as one connected sequence, signposted
clearly, with no planning pause.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your claim, reason, and conclusion come out as one connected sequence, not three separate sentences?
- Did you signpost the reason with "because" and the conclusion with "so" or "therefore"?
- Did you speak immediately, with no pause to plan the argument first?
:::
