## Introduction

[Lesson 35](/lessons/35/) trained you to detect an author's tone from
word choice and framing. Producing two different tones yourself, live,
around one unchanged fact, proves you actually understand that tone
lives in wording and delivery — not in the underlying truth of what
happened.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Neutral: the report found processing time fell by fifteen percent.
Skeptical: the report claims processing time fell by fifteen percent —
a figure nobody outside the company has confirmed.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice how the skeptical version isn't
just different words — the voice also slows slightly on "claims" and
"nobody outside the company," giving the distancing language extra weight.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
Think of one real or plausible fact or finding you know something about.
When the timer starts, say it once in a neutral tone using a plain
reporting verb (found, reported, shows), then immediately say it again
in a skeptical tone using distancing language (claims, has yet to be
confirmed) — without changing a single fact between the two versions.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Speak now — the fact, neutral, then the same fact, skeptical"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record both versions, then play them back and check that the underlying fact stayed identical while only the tone shifted."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Neutral: a survey of our trucking partners found that the new
booking system cut gate waiting time by twenty minutes. Skeptical: the
port claims its new booking system cut gate waiting time by twenty
minutes, based on a survey it ran itself, and no outside study has
confirmed it." Check that both versions reported the exact same number.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="distancing language" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="spoken wording like claims or has yet to be confirmed that signals doubt without changing the underlying fact (দূরত্ব সৃষ্টিকারী ভাষা)" collocation="use distancing language, distancing language such as, signal with distancing language" example="Saying 'the company claims' is distancing language: I am not vouching for it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="objectivity" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="reporting a fact and its real limits without letting tone push the listener toward belief or doubt (নিরপেক্ষতা)" collocation="maintain objectivity, journalistic objectivity, lack of objectivity" example="I tried to keep objectivity by stating both the results and their limits."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did both spoken versions report exactly the same underlying fact, with nothing added or removed?
- Did your neutral version use a plain reporting verb, free of evaluative words?
- Did your skeptical version use genuine distancing language, not just a flatter voice?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
