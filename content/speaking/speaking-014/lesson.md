## Introduction

[Lesson 14](/lessons/14/) taught you to tell a gerund from an infinitive
by checking the job it's doing. This drill asks you to build both live:
naming an activity as your subject, then stating a purpose with "to +
verb" — instantly, with the timer already running.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Checking the schedule twice a day helps me avoid missing a deadline.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice "Checking" opens
the sentence at full, even stress, exactly like a noun would — it never
gets swallowed the way a quick continuous-tense "is checking" might.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="12s"
When the timer starts, say two sentences out loud: first, one sentence
with a gerund phrase as the subject, naming something you do regularly;
second, one sentence using "want to," "need to," or "decide to" + a
verb, stating why you do it. Speak immediately — no planning pause.
:::

::: timer seconds="12" label="Speak your two sentences now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your two sentences, then record them and compare against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct pair — "Reading every night improves my
vocabulary" followed by "I want to finish this book by Friday" is one
natural option. What matters is that the gerund opened the first
sentence cleanly, and "to + base verb" (never "to + -ing") followed the
second sentence's verb immediately.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="deadline" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the latest time by which something must be finished (নির্দিষ্ট সময়সীমা)" collocation="meet a deadline, tight deadline, miss the deadline" example="We must meet the deadline for filing the customs declaration."
:::

::: vocabulary word="avoid" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to stay away from something, or prevent it from happening (এড়ানো)" collocation="avoid delays, avoid making, narrowly avoid" example="Pre-booking helps drivers avoid long queues at the gate."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your first sentence open with a gerund functioning as the subject?
- Did your second sentence use "to + base verb," spoken correctly under time pressure?
- Did you speak both sentences immediately, without a pause to plan them?
:::
