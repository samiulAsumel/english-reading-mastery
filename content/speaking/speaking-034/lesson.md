## Introduction

[Lesson 34](/lessons/34/) trained you to read the persuasive structures
beneath an argument's surface, including concession-then-rebuttal.
Speaking that structure live, without a script, forces you to actually
commit to the fair concession before you've planned your stronger
comeback — the same discipline that makes the written version work.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Critics fairly say automated sorting costs more upfront. However, it
pays for itself within a year through fewer misrouted packages.
:::

Listen once all the way through. Notice the short pause right before
"However" — that pause is the pivot doing its work, signaling the
stronger point is about to arrive.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="25s"
Think of a tool, method, or policy you have a genuine opinion about. When
the timer starts, say a fair concession about the other side, pivot with
"however" or "but," then state your stronger point. Finish by saying,
out loud, your purpose (to persuade, to recommend, to inform) and naming
one word you chose deliberately to serve it.
:::

::: timer seconds="25" label="Speak now — concession, pivot, rebuttal, then your stated purpose"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your concession-then-rebuttal passage and stated purpose, then check whether the pivot word carried a real pause before it."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — what matters is the shape. For
example: "Critics fairly note that electronic bills of lading mean
retraining every clerk. However, the switch pays for itself within
months through fewer processing errors. My purpose was to recommend the
switch, and I chose the word 'fairly' deliberately, to respect the
retraining concern before my rebuttal." Check that your purpose sentence
named one specific word, not just a general claim.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="pivot" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the spoken turning point, often on 'however' or 'but,' where the speaker moves from concession to their real point (মোড়)" collocation="pivot from, the pivot in the sentence, clear pivot" example="The pivot in 'The cost is high, but the savings are larger' is 'but'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="loaded language" pos="noun phrase" register="neutral" meaning="word choice that carries an evaluative charge beyond the plain fact, chosen deliberately to serve a purpose (পক্ষপাতদুষ্ট ভাষা)" collocation="avoid loaded language, loaded language such as, use loaded language" example="Saying 'the reckless plan' is loaded language; 'the unapproved plan' is neutral."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your concession sound genuinely fair, not a weak point dismissed instantly?
- Did you pause briefly before "however" or "but," letting the pivot land?
- Did you state your purpose and name one specific word choice that served it?
- Did you speak directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
