## Introduction

[Lesson 87](/lessons/87/) — the final lesson in this course's reading
track — taught you to sort a paragraph's sentences into a hierarchy: main
idea, real support, and mere detail. This drill, the last speaking drill in
the course so far, makes you build that hierarchy live, out loud, with no
time to plan which sentence is which before you start speaking.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
Automating berth scheduling only pays off in high-volume ports. In busy
ports, the system had enough bookings to optimize assignments and cut
turnaround time by nearly a third. In smaller ports, there simply weren't
enough vessels scheduled for the algorithm to find any real gain.
:::

Notice how the first sentence carries the whole point, and the two "In
___ ports" sentences that follow are doing real supporting work — remove
either one mentally, and the claim gets weaker, not just shorter.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="60s"
Pick a process, technology, or policy you know well. When the timer starts,
say one sentence stating your main idea — not just the topic — followed by
two or three spoken sentences that genuinely support it, live, with no
drafting first.
:::

::: timer seconds="60" label="Speak now — main idea first, then genuine support"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Record your main idea and supporting sentences, then compare against the model answer below."

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct topic — what matters is the structure. One
natural version: "A new scheduling system only reduces missed shifts when
staff are trained before rollout. Without that training, employees revert
to the old paper process the moment something unusual happens. And when the
system doesn't integrate cleanly with payroll, managers stop trusting its
numbers within a few weeks." Check that each of your own supporting
sentences would genuinely weaken your point if removed — not just add a
loosely related fact.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="removal test" pos="noun phrase" register="course term" meaning="a way to judge a sentence's importance by imagining it deleted and seeing what, if anything, is lost (অপসারণ পরীক্ষা)" collocation="apply the removal test, use the removal test, removal test for" example="Apply the removal test: if cutting the sentence changes nothing, leave it out."
:::

::: vocabulary word="tangent" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a spoken sentence that relates loosely to the topic but doesn't actually support the main idea (মূল বিষয় থেকে বিচ্যুতি)" collocation="go off on a tangent, tangent from, brief tangent" example="A sentence about parking is a tangent in a talk about gate delays."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your first sentence state a real main idea, not just the topic?
- Would each supporting sentence genuinely weaken your point if removed, rather than just adding a related fact?
- Did you speak immediately, without a long pause to plan the structure first?
:::
