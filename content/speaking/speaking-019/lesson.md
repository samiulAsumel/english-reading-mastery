## Introduction

[Lesson 19](/lessons/19/) taught you to notice when a writer lists
several ideas in the same grammatical shape. Speaking a parallel list
live is harder than reading one — the moment you commit to a form for
the first item, you have to keep matching it for the second and third,
with no time to check your own grammar.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
A good driver checks the mirrors, signals early, and watches the road
ahead.
:::

Listen once all the way through, then shadow it. Notice all three verbs
— checks, signals, watches — land with the same rhythm and stress; that
even beat is what makes a spoken list sound deliberate rather than
accidental.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="12s"
When the timer starts, say one sentence listing three things a good
student does, using three matching base verbs (like "checks, signals,
watches" above). Speak immediately — no planning pause, and don't let
the third item slip into a different grammatical form.
:::

::: timer seconds="12" label="Speak your parallel list now"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Say your parallel list, then record it and compare against the model."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct sentence — "A good student reviews notes
daily, asks questions in class, and practices new vocabulary" is one
natural option. What matters is that all three verbs kept the same
grammatical form, spoken at an even pace with no hesitation before the
third item.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="signal" pos="verb" meaning="to give a sign showing what you're about to do — সংকেত দেওয়া" example="A good driver checks the mirrors and signals early before turning."
:::

::: vocabulary word="ahead" pos="adverb" meaning="in front, further along in space or time — সামনে" example="Watching the road ahead helps a driver react early."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did all three items in your spoken list share the same grammatical form?
- Did you speak the full sentence immediately, with no pause to plan it?
- Did your third item keep the same rhythm and stress as the first two?
:::
