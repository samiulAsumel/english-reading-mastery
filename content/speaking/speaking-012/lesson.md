## Introduction

[Lesson 12](/lessons/12/) taught you to read a past-tense verb after
"if" as a signal of possibility, not past time. Speaking a hypothetical
answer on the spot is a genuinely different skill from reading one — you
have to build "if + past simple, would + base verb" live, in real time.

## Shadow the Model

::: shadow rate="0.9"
If I had more time, I would learn a new skill every month.
:::

Listen once, then shadow it. Notice "if I had" is said quickly and
lightly — it's the setup, not the point of the sentence. The stress
falls on "learn a new skill," the actual answer.

## Your Turn

::: prompt time="8s"
Question: "What would you do if you had an extra hour every day?" When
the timer starts, answer out loud immediately with one full
second-conditional sentence — don't plan the grammar first, just answer.
:::

::: timer seconds="8" label="Answer now — one hypothetical sentence, no planning"
:::

## Record Yourself

::: record label="Answer the question, then record yourself and check the if-clause used past simple, not 'would'."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
There's no single correct answer — "If I had an extra hour, I would
read more" and "If I had an extra hour, I would exercise" are both
fine. What matters is the structure: past simple in the if-clause,
"would" only in the main clause, spoken without a planning pause.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="extra" pos="adjective" meaning="more than what is usual or expected — অতিরিক্ত" example="If I had an extra hour every day, I would learn something new."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you answer immediately, with no pause to plan the sentence?
- Did your if-clause use past simple, not "would"?
- Did your main clause correctly use "would" + base verb?
:::
