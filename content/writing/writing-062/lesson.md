## Introduction

[Lesson 62](/lessons/62/) taught you to read a passage in motion — to
build a timeline, spot turning points, and never assume that "then"
means "because of." This task asks you to write that kind of passage
yourself: a short account of a process or change that keeps sequence
and causation cleanly separate.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Order events with explicit time markers (initially, by the following
year, eventually), then only claim causation where you can defend it —
use "which led to" or "triggered" for a real causal link, and a neutral
"after this" or "around the same time" when you only know the order,
not the cause.
:::

::: example
*The terminal initially processed all bookings manually. Within two
years, a digital booking system had been introduced, which reduced
average processing time by half. Around the same time, the port also
adopted new customs software — though the two changes were not
directly connected.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="100-150 words" time="20 min"
Describe a real or realistic process of change in your field over
several stages (e.g. how a system, a role, or a procedure evolved).
Use at least three explicit time markers, name one clear turning point,
and use one strong causal verb (triggered, led to, resulted in) for a
link you can actually justify — while marking anything you're not
claiming caused the next stage as merely sequential.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Initially, ... / By [time], ... / This led to... / Around the same time, ... (not directly caused by the above)"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="turning point" pos="noun phrase" meaning="the moment a process changes direction or accelerates — মোড়ঘোরানো মুহূর্ত" example="The turning point came when the port switched to automated gate systems."
:::

::: vocabulary word="triggered" pos="verb" meaning="directly caused something to start happening — সূচনা ঘটানো" example="A single equipment failure triggered a full review of the maintenance schedule."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The warehouse initially tracked inventory using paper logs. By the
second year of operation, a barcode scanning system had been
introduced, which reduced counting errors by nearly a third. The real
turning point came the following year, when a stock shortage triggered
a full switch to real-time digital tracking. Around the same time, the
company also relocated to a larger facility — though this move was not
directly caused by the inventory changes, the two happened to overlap.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use at least three explicit time markers to order the stages?
- Did you name one clear turning point?
- Did you use a strong causal verb only where you could genuinely justify it, and mark anything merely sequential as such?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the paragraph in Bangla first?
:::
