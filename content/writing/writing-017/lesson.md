## Introduction

[Lesson 17](/lessons/17/) taught you to read "may," "tends to," and "is
associated with" as signals of exactly how confident an author is.
Writing your own hedged claim means making that confidence level a
deliberate choice, not something that just happens to the sentence.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Start from a flat claim (*X causes Y*), then add exactly one layer of
caution at a time. A modal like *may* or *could* turns certainty into
possibility. A phrase like *is associated with* or *tends to* weakens
causation into correlation or tendency. Stack both together for a claim
as cautious as the evidence genuinely allows — never hedge more than the
evidence requires, and never hedge less.
:::

::: example
**Flat:** Overtime reduces productivity.
**Hedged with a modal:** Overtime may reduce productivity.
**Hedged further:** Overtime may be associated with reduced productivity
over time.
Each version claims something slightly different — and slightly weaker —
than the one before it.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="3 sentences" time="10 min"
Choose one idea: *late deliveries damage customer trust.* Write it three
ways — once as a flat, unhedged claim; once hedged with a single modal
(may/might/could); and once hedged further using a phrase like "is
associated with" or "tends to."
:::

::: draft placeholder="Flat: ... / Modal: ...may... / Further hedged: ...is associated with / tends to..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="trust" pos="noun" meaning="firm belief in the reliability of someone or something — বিশ্বাস" example="Late deliveries may be associated with a gradual loss of customer trust."
:::

::: vocabulary word="associated" pos="adjective" meaning="linked or connected with something, without necessarily causing it — সম্পর্কিত" example="Overtime is associated with reduced productivity, though the link isn't always direct."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Flat:** Late deliveries damage customer trust.
**Hedged with a modal:** Late deliveries may damage customer trust.
**Hedged further:** Late deliveries may be associated with a gradual
decline in customer trust.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your flat sentence contain no hedge word at all?
- Does your modal sentence use "may," "might," or "could" correctly, in front of the base verb?
- Does your final sentence genuinely sound more cautious than the first two, not just longer?
- Did you write all three sentences directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
