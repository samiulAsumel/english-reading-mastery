## Introduction

[Lesson 41](/lessons/41/) taught you to unpack a dense sentence into two
or three plain ones without quietly changing its claim. This task asks
you to do both directions: write one genuinely dense sentence, then
paraphrase your own sentence back into plain English — proving you can
compress and decompress the same idea without losing anything along the
way.

## Nominalization: The Main Compression Tool

::: concept
A **nominalization** turns a verb into a noun: *implement → the
implementation*, *reduce → a reduction*, *evaluate → an evaluation*.
Academic English strings several nominalizations together to compress
what would otherwise take two or three sentences.
:::

::: example
**Plain:** *The company changed its system. The change reduced errors.*
**Dense:** *The company's implementation of a redesigned system resulted
in a reduction in processing errors.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="1 dense sentence + a 2-3 sentence paraphrase" time="20 min"
Write one dense sentence (25-40 words) about a workplace change, using
at least two nominalizations and one hedge word (may, appears to,
suggests). Then paraphrase your own sentence into two or three plain
sentences with active verbs, keeping the hedge intact — don't let
"appears to reduce" quietly become "reduces."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="nominalization" pos="noun" meaning="a noun formed from a verb, used to compress a sentence — ক্রিয়া থেকে গঠিত বিশেষ্য" example="'Implementation' is the nominalization of 'implement.'"
:::

::: vocabulary word="hedge" pos="noun" meaning="a word that softens a claim's certainty, such as 'may' or 'appears to' — সতর্কতাসূচক শব্দ" example="Dropping the hedge 'appears to' turns a cautious claim into an overstatement."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Dense:** *The organization's adoption of automated scheduling appears
to have resulted in a modest reduction in staff overtime, although
attribution to the system alone remains uncertain.* **Plain paraphrase:**
*The organization started using automated scheduling. Staff overtime
seems to have dropped a little since then. It's not fully clear,
though, that the scheduling system alone caused the drop.* The hedge
("appears to," "uncertain") survives intact in both versions.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your dense sentence use at least two real nominalizations, not just long words?
- Does your plain paraphrase keep the same hedge strength as the original, neither stronger nor weaker?
- Are the two versions provably the same claim, just packaged differently?
- Did you convert each nominalization back to its verb form correctly in the paraphrase?
:::
