## Introduction

[Lesson 40](/lessons/40/) taught you that a sentence's true claim often
hides in a handful of small words — *only, not, most* — and that moving
one of them changes what's actually being said. Writing forces the
opposite discipline: you have to place these words correctly the first
time, because there's no author's intention to reconstruct — you *are*
the author.

## Placement Is the Whole Skill

::: concept
Put *only* directly before the exact word or phrase it should restrict.
*Only the manager approved the proposal* restricts *who*; *the manager
approved only the proposal* restricts *what*. Misplacing it is the most
common way a writer accidentally claims more, or less, than intended.
:::

::: example
**Overclaiming:** *The policy failed.* (sounds total) **Precisely
scoped:** *The policy did not necessarily fail — it reduced costs in
some regions but not others.* *Not necessarily* keeps the door open
instead of slamming it shut.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="3 sentences" time="20 min"
Write three separate sentences. Sentence 1: use "only" so it clearly
restricts one specific word or phrase (not the whole sentence).
Sentence 2: use a qualified negative ("not necessarily," "not all," or
"not entirely") to make a claim honestly partial rather than absolute.
Sentence 3: choose the most accurate quantifier (few, some, many, most,
almost all) for a claim about a group, and make sure it's not
interchangeable with a stronger or weaker one.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="scope" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="the specific part of a sentence a word's logical effect actually reaches (পরিসর)" collocation="scope of negation, wide scope, narrow scope" example="In 'Not all containers were delayed', the scope of 'not' covers 'all'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="quantifier" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="a word indicating amount or proportion without an exact number (পরিমাণবাচক শব্দ)" collocation="use a quantifier, quantifier such as, precise quantifier" example="'Most', 'some', and 'few' are quantifiers that state a proportion without a number."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Only:** *Only the night-shift supervisors received the updated safety
checklist.* (scope: *night-shift supervisors*, nobody else). **Qualified
negative:** *The new checklist does not necessarily prevent every
incident, but it has reduced their frequency.* **Quantifier:** *Most
supervisors adopted the checklist within a week, though a few still
relied on the older paper version.* ("Most," not "almost all" — the
sentence itself admits a real exception.)
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does "only" attach to exactly the word or phrase you intended, with no other reading possible?
- Does your qualified negative avoid accidentally sounding like an absolute denial?
- Does your chosen quantifier match the actual strength of the claim, not just the first word that came to mind?
- Could a careful reader misplace the scope of any of your three sentences?
:::
