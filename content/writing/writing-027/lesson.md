## Introduction

[Lesson 27](/lessons/27/) taught you to find which half of an
"although X, Y" sentence the author is actually defending — the
concession is never the point. Now you build that pattern yourself: say
something fair about the other side, then land, unmistakably, on your
own.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
An although/despite sentence has two jobs done by two different clauses:
the concession (something true you're willing to admit) and the main
point (what you actually want the reader to take away). Put the weaker
idea in the concession clause and the stronger one in the main clause —
readers remember whichever clause comes last.
:::

::: example
**Although the new scheduling software has a steep learning curve, it
has cut missed shipments by half in its first quarter.** The concession
(steep learning curve) is real, but the sentence's actual point is the
second half. Compare: **Manual scheduling is simple, whereas the new
software requires training** — this is a plain comparison, no concession,
no clear winner stated.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="3-4 sentences" time="18 min"
Write three or four sentences comparing two real options (two tools, two
approaches, two routines — your choice). Use at least one although/despite
sentence with a genuine concession followed by your real point, and at
least one whereas or in contrast sentence that states a plain difference
without conceding anything.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Although .../despite ..., [your real point]. / X, whereas Y. / Rather than ..., I chose to ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="concession" pos="noun" meaning="a point you admit is true even though it doesn't change your main position — স্বীকৃতি" example="The concession that the software is slow to learn didn't change the writer's recommendation."
:::

::: vocabulary word="trade-off" pos="noun" meaning="a situation where gaining one benefit means giving up another — বিনিময়/ছাড়" example="Choosing the cheaper option meant accepting a trade-off in build quality."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Despite its higher upfront cost, the new container-tracking system has
already paid for itself in reduced search time. Manual logs are simple
to maintain, whereas the digital system requires staff training before
it saves anyone time. Rather than replacing the old system overnight,
the terminal chose to run both in parallel for one full quarter.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- In your although/despite sentence, is it obvious which clause is the concession and which is your real point?
- Does your whereas/in contrast sentence state a plain difference, without quietly favoring one side?
- Did you avoid burying your real point in the first clause, where a reader's attention is weakest?
- Did you write directly in English, without translating a Bangla sentence first?
:::
