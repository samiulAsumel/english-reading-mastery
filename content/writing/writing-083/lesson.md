## Introduction

[Lesson 83](/lessons/83/) taught you to track a claim across a whole
book, asking after every section: did its certainty rise or fall, did
its scope narrow, was a limit added? And it gave you a specific test for
telling refinement apart from contradiction. This task asks you to apply
that test in writing, to a claim that changes across a short passage.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Refinement adds precision without denying the earlier claim — "ports
save time" becomes "ports save time, mainly above a certain volume."
Contradiction denies what was said before — "ports save time" becomes
"ports do not actually save time." The test: can both statements be true
at once? If yes, it's refinement. If no, it's contradiction.
:::

::: example
"Early evidence suggested the new schedule cut delays." Later: "on
closer inspection, the delay reduction was mostly seasonal, not caused
by the new schedule." These cannot both be fully true — the second
statement denies the causal claim of the first. That's contradiction,
not refinement, even though the author states it politely.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="70-100 words" time="20 min"
Section 1 of a report claims: "the new customs process reduced
clearance time by half." Section 4 states: "on closer inspection, the
reduction only applied to shipments already flagged as low-risk; for
all other shipments, clearance time stayed the same." Write a paragraph
tracking what changed about the claim — its scope, its certainty — and
say clearly whether this is refinement or contradiction, using the test
above.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="refine" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to add precision to a claim without denying it (পরিমার্জন করা)" collocation="refine a claim, refine the argument, further refine" example="The later chapters refine the claim without abandoning it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="on closer inspection" pos="phrase" register="formal" meaning="a signal phrase marking that a claim is about to be corrected or narrowed (গভীরভাবে পর্যবেক্ষণ করলে)" collocation="on closer inspection, upon closer inspection, after closer inspection" example="On closer inspection, the improvement applied only to export cargo."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Section 1 claims a broad win: customs clearance time was cut in half.
Section 4 narrows the scope drastically — the reduction only held for
shipments already flagged low-risk, and for everything else, nothing
changed. Applying the test: can both statements be true at once? No —
"clearance time was cut in half" implies a general effect, but Section 4
shows the effect applied to a small subset of shipments, not the whole
process. This is a contradiction dressed as a refinement: the scope
shrank so much that the original claim is no longer defensible as
stated.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you correctly identify this as contradiction rather than refinement?
- Did you apply the "can both be true at once" test explicitly?
- Did you cite the specific words that mark the scope change?
:::
