## Introduction

[Lesson 12](/lessons/12/) taught you that a past-tense verb after "if"
usually signals something hypothetical, not something that already
happened. Now build that structure yourself: write sentences where the
grammar looks past, but the meaning is "this isn't real — but if it
were."

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
**Second conditional** (unreal present/future): *if* + past simple, ...
*would* + base verb. Use it for something unlikely or imagined right
now. **Third conditional** (unreal past): *if* + past perfect, ...
*would have* + past participle. Use it for something that didn't happen
in the past. In both, "would" belongs only in the main clause, never
inside the if-clause.
:::

::: example
Second conditional: "If the port **had** more cranes, ships **would
unload** faster." Third conditional: "If the crane **had not broken**
down, the ship **would have left** on schedule." Notice "if" never
pairs with "would" in the same clause.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="10 min"
Write two second-conditional sentences about your work or daily life
(imagined present/future situations), and two third-conditional
sentences about something that could have gone differently in the past.
:::

::: draft placeholder="If I had... / If I hadn't..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="unload" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to remove cargo from a ship, truck, or container (খালাস করা)" collocation="unload cargo, unload a vessel, unload a truck" example="Crews began to unload the vessel as soon as it reached the berth."
:::

::: vocabulary word="schedule" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a planned time for something to happen (সময়সূচি)" collocation="on schedule, behind schedule, schedule of" example="The vessel arrived on schedule despite the storm."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Second conditional: "If I worked closer to the port, I would walk to
work every day." "If I spoke fluent English, I would apply for that
job." Third conditional: "If I had studied harder in school, I would
have finished my degree sooner." "If the shipment hadn't been delayed,
we would have met the deadline."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did every if-clause use past simple (second conditional) or past perfect (third conditional), never "would"?
- Did every main clause correctly use "would" + base verb or "would have" + past participle?
- Is each sentence clearly about something unreal or imagined, not a real fact?
:::
