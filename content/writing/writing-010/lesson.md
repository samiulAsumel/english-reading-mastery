## Introduction

Repeating the same noun in every sentence reads like a checklist, not
like writing: "The manager reviewed the report. The manager approved
the report. The manager sent the report." [Lesson 10](/lessons/10/)
taught you to trace a pronoun back to what it means; this task asks you
to build that same chain yourself, so your writing sounds connected
instead of repetitive.

## Building a Reference Chain

::: concept
Introduce a noun once, then let a pronoun carry it forward: "The
manager reviewed the report. **She** approved **it** the same day."
*She* → the manager, *it* → the report — both singular, matching their
antecedents exactly. When you want to refer back to a whole previous
idea rather than one noun, **this** or **these** does that job: "The
system went down twice this week. **This** cost us several hours." —
*this* refers to the whole event, not to any single noun in the
sentence before it.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="12 min"
Write two short pairs of sentences about your work. In the first pair,
introduce a person or thing by name, then refer back to it with a
pronoun (he/she/it/they) in the second sentence. In the second pair,
describe an event, then use *this* or *these* in the following sentence
to refer back to the whole idea, not one noun.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="antecedent" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="the noun or idea a pronoun refers back to (পূর্ববর্তী নির্দেশ্য)" collocation="clear antecedent, identify the antecedent, unclear antecedent" example="In 'The auditor reviewed the file and approved it', the antecedent of 'it' is 'the file'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="reference chain" pos="noun" register="course term" meaning="a series of pronouns pointing back to the same noun or idea across several sentences (নির্দেশ শৃঙ্খল)" collocation="follow the reference chain, build a reference chain, reference chain of pronouns" example="The reference chain 'the supplier, it, its' keeps the paragraph focused on one company."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The supervisor checked every container before loading began. She
signed off on the final count herself.

The delivery arrived late twice this month. This forced us to adjust
next week's schedule as well.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your pronoun match its antecedent in number — singular for singular, plural for plural?
- Could a reader identify exactly what "she," "it," or "they" refers to without guessing?
- Does your "this/these" sentence genuinely refer to a whole previous idea, not one noun?
:::
