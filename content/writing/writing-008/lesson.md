## Introduction

A list of correct sentences isn't the same as writing that reasons. "I
was tired. I went to work." is grammatically fine, but it doesn't tell
the reader *how* those two facts relate. [Lesson 8](/lessons/8/) showed
you that *and, but, because, so* — and their more formal cousins
*however* and *therefore* — are exactly the tool that fixes this.

## Four Connectors, Then Their Formal Versions

::: concept
**And** adds one idea to another. **But** shows contrast — and its
formal twin **however** carries the identical logic in a separate
sentence: "The task was difficult. However, we finished it." **Because**
gives a reason, answering "why?" **So** shows a result — and its formal
twin **therefore** works the same way: "We were behind schedule.
Therefore, we worked through lunch." Pick the connector by asking which
silent question your second idea is actually answering.
:::

::: warning
The most common mix-up: using *because* when you mean *so*, or the
reverse. Test it by asking which half is the cause. "I was tired
**because** I worked late" — the cause comes *after* *because*. "I
worked late, **so** I was tired" — the cause comes *before* *so*.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="5 sentences" time="12 min"
Write five sentences about a recent day at work or study: one using
*and*, one using *but*, one using *because*, one using *so*, and then a
fifth sentence that rewrites your *but* or *so* sentence using its
formal cousin (*however* or *therefore*) as two separate sentences.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="formal" pos="adjective" register="neutral" meaning="suited to serious or professional writing, rather than everyday speech (আনুষ্ঠানিক)" collocation="formal tone, formal register, formal writing" example="A formal report says 'therefore', where a message to a friend might say 'so'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="cause" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the reason something happens (কারণ)" collocation="root cause, cause of, cause and effect" example="The report names a faulty sensor as the cause of the shutdown."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
I checked the schedule and confirmed the vessel's arrival time.

The weather was bad, but the ship still arrived on schedule.

We finished the report early because the team worked well together.

The system went down for an hour, so we had to record everything by
hand.

The weather was bad. However, the ship still arrived on schedule.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use all four basic connectors correctly, one per sentence?
- For "because" and "so," can you point to which half of the sentence is the cause?
- Does your formal rewrite (however/therefore) carry exactly the same logic as the original, just in two sentences?
:::
