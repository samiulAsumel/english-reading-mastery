## Introduction

[Lesson 52](/lessons/52/) taught you that a good definition is never just a
one-line label — it's what it is, what it is not, what it includes, and how
it differs from something people easily confuse it with. Writing one forces
you to actually understand a concept, not just recognize its name.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A weak definition just restates the word in slightly different language
("scalability means a system is scalable"). A strong definition does four
things: names the category the concept belongs to, states what it is not
(the boundary), names a related concept it's easily confused with and says
how they differ, and gives one concrete instance so the abstraction lands.
:::

::: example
Demurrage is not simply "a shipping fee." It is the charge a shipping line
applies when a customer keeps a container beyond its free time at the port —
it is not the same as detention, which is the fee for keeping the container
itself (rather than the space it occupies) too long after it leaves the
terminal. A shipper who returns a container to the port on time but leaves
it sitting there for two extra days pays demurrage; a shipper who takes the
container off-site and returns it two days late pays detention instead.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="120-160 words" time="20 min"
Choose one concept from your own field (technology, logistics, business, or
any domain you know well) that is often confused with a related term. Write
a paragraph that defines it precisely: state its category, say what it is
not, name the concept it's easily confused with and explain the difference,
and give one concrete example.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="boundary" pos="noun" meaning="the line that separates what a concept includes from what it excludes — সীমারেখা" example="Knowing a concept's boundary means knowing what does NOT count as an example of it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="circular" pos="adjective" meaning="(of a definition) explaining a term using a form of the same word, so nothing is actually explained — চক্রাকার" example="\"Efficiency means being efficient\" is a circular definition."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Latency is not the same as bandwidth, even though both describe network
performance. Latency is the delay before data starts moving — how long a
signal takes to travel from one point to another and back. Bandwidth is
something else entirely: how much data can move at once, once it starts
moving. A connection can have very high bandwidth and still feel sluggish if
its latency is high, the way a wide road with a long traffic light at the
start still makes every car wait before any of them move. Confusing the two
leads to the wrong fix — adding bandwidth does nothing for a latency
problem.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your definition name the concept's category rather than just restating the word?
- Did you state clearly what the concept is NOT?
- Did you name a related, easily confused concept and explain the difference?
- Is your definition free of circularity — does it actually explain, not just relabel?
:::
