## Introduction

A list of correct sentences isn't the same as writing that reasons.
"I was tired. I went to work." is grammatically fine, but it doesn't
tell the reader *how* those two facts relate. Four small words fix that
completely, and they're the single biggest upgrade available at this
stage: **and**, **but**, **because**, **so**.

## Four Connectors, Four Relationships

::: concept
**And** adds one idea to another: "I finished the report **and** I sent
it." **But** shows contrast: "I was tired, **but** I finished the
report." **Because** gives a reason, answering "why?": "I was tired
**because** I worked late." **So** shows a result, answering "what
happened next?": "I worked late, **so** I was tired." Each one answers a
different silent question the reader is asking — pick the word by
asking yourself which question your second sentence is actually
answering.
:::

::: warning
The most common mix-up: using *because* when you mean *so*, or the
reverse. Test it by asking which sentence is the **cause**. "I was tired
**because** I worked late" — the cause (worked late) comes *after*
*because*. "I worked late, **so** I was tired" — the cause comes
*before* *so*. If you can identify which half is the cause, you can
always pick the right word.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="10 min"
Write four sentences about a recent day at work or study, one using each
connector (and, but, because, so). Each sentence must genuinely show
that relationship — don't force a connector where it doesn't fit.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="contrast" pos="noun" meaning="a clear difference between two things — বৈপরীত্য" example="'But' signals a contrast between two ideas."
:::

::: vocabulary word="cause" pos="noun" meaning="the reason something happens — কারণ" example="Heavy traffic was the cause of the delay."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
I checked the schedule and confirmed the vessel's arrival time. The
weather was bad, but the ship still arrived on schedule. We finished the
report early because the team worked well together. The system went
down for an hour, so we had to record everything by hand.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use all four connectors correctly, one per sentence?
- For "because" and "so," can you point to which half of the sentence is the cause?
- Does each sentence still make sense as one connected thought, not two ideas awkwardly glued together?
:::
