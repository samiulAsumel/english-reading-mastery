## Introduction

[Lesson 5](/lessons/5/) taught you that Present Perfect exists
specifically to connect a finished past action to its present
relevance, and that Past Simple simply closes the door on it. Writing
both about the same topic, correctly, is the clearest way to make that
difference stick — instead of just being able to explain it about
someone else's sentence.

## Past Simple vs. Present Perfect, on Purpose

::: concept
**Past Simple** reports a finished event and stops: "I finished the
report yesterday." **Present Perfect** reports the same kind of event
but claims it still matters right now: "I have finished the report" —
implying it's ready for you to look at, today. Choosing between them is
not about which is more correct — it's about whether you want the
reader thinking about the past moment, or about its result, now.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="12 min"
Write four sentences about your work or studies: one Present Simple
stating a general habit or fact, one Past Simple about something that
happened and is now closed, one Present Perfect about something that
still matters right now, and one Future sentence making a prediction.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Present Simple: I usually... / Past Simple: Yesterday I... / Present Perfect: I have already... / Future: Next week I will..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="relevance" pos="noun" meaning="the quality of still mattering to the present situation — প্রাসঙ্গিকতা" example="Present Perfect signals a past event's relevance to now."
:::

::: vocabulary word="prediction" pos="noun" meaning="a statement about something expected to happen in the future — পূর্বাভাস" example="Will is the simplest way to write a prediction."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
I usually check the cargo manifest before the vessel docks.

Yesterday I checked the manifest twice because the numbers looked wrong.

I have already checked today's manifest, so the team can start loading.

Next month, the port will handle a much larger volume of containers.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your Present Simple sentence state a habit or general fact, not something happening right now?
- Can you explain, in one sentence, why your Present Perfect example still matters today while your Past Simple example is closed?
- Does your Future sentence make a genuine prediction rather than describing something already decided as fact?
:::
