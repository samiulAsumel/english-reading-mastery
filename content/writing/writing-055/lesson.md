## Introduction

[Lesson 55](/lessons/55/) taught you not to stop at "what number does this
show" but to ask what it measures, what it's compared with, how it changed,
how certain it is, and what conclusion it actually justifies. Writing about
data means doing that thinking yourself, in the sentence, before the reader
ever sees the raw number.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A weak data sentence just states a figure: "Sales were $50,000 in March." A
strong one gives the number context — what it's measuring, what it's being
compared with — and draws only the conclusion the comparison actually
supports, without inflating it into something bigger.
:::

::: example
Container throughput at the terminal rose from 1,200 to 1,350 TEU per week
over the quarter — an 12.5% increase compared with the same period last
year. That's enough to say demand grew; it isn't, on its own, enough to say
the new crane reduced turnaround time, since throughput and turnaround are
different measurements.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="90-140 words" time="20 min"
Take one number you know from your own work or studies (a metric, a score, a
statistic) and write a short passage that states what it measures, what it's
compared with (an earlier period, a target, another group), and one
conclusion the number genuinely supports — stopping short of a bigger claim
it doesn't actually prove.
:::

:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Average page load time fell from 3.2 seconds to 1.8 seconds after the new
caching system was deployed — a drop of nearly 44% compared with the month
before the change. That's enough evidence to say the caching system reduced
load time for this site during this period. It is not, on its own, enough
to claim the change increased sales, since load time and sales are two
different measurements that would need their own comparison to connect.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you state clearly what the number measures?
- Did you give a comparison point (an earlier period, a target, another group)?
- Does your conclusion stay within what the number actually supports?
- Did you avoid connecting the number to a bigger claim it doesn't prove?
:::
