## Introduction

[Lesson 18](/lessons/18/) taught you to look at a noun like
"implementation" and instantly recover the verb hiding inside it. This
task runs that process backward: you start with two plain sentences and
build the dense, formal version yourself — the version a report or a
professional email would actually use.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Turn your main verb into a noun (*reduce → reduction, analyze →
analysis*), attach *of + what the action applies to*, and connect it to
the result with a phrase like *resulted in* or *led to*. **The reduction
of loading time** compresses two separate facts — something got reduced,
and it was loading time — into one noun phrase built around a single
head noun.
:::

::: example
**Plain:** The port automated its gate system. This reduced truck
waiting times.
**Nominalized:** The automation of the port's gate system resulted in a
reduction in truck waiting times.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="1 sentence" time="10 min"
Here are two plain sentences: *The company trained its staff on the new
software. This reduced the number of data-entry errors.* Combine both
ideas into ONE formal sentence, using a nominalization (such as
"training" or "reduction") and a connector like "resulted in" or "led
to."
:::

::: draft placeholder="The [nominalization] of ... resulted in / led to the [nominalization] of ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="automate" pos="verb" meaning="to make a process work by machine, with little or no human control — স্বয়ংক্রিয় করা" example="The automation of the gate system reduced truck waiting times."
:::

::: vocabulary word="error" pos="noun" meaning="a mistake, especially one made carelessly — ভুল" example="Staff training led to a noticeable reduction in data-entry errors."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The training of staff on the new software resulted in a significant
reduction in data-entry errors. (Equally valid: "Staff training led to a
significant reduction in data-entry errors.")
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you build at least one nominalization around a head noun, rather than just joining the two sentences with "and"?
- Does your of-phrase correctly name what the nominalized action applied to?
- Does the sentence read as natural, formal English when read aloud, not like a translated pattern?
- Did every idea from the original two sentences survive the compression?
:::
