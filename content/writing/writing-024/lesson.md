## Introduction

[Lesson 24](/lessons/24/) taught you to read the exact strength of a
causal claim — *caused* is stronger than *contributed to*, which is
stronger than *is associated with*. Writing your own causal sentence
means making that same choice yourself, honestly, based on what your
evidence actually shows rather than what sounds more dramatic.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Match your verb to your evidence. If you only know two things happened
together, write *is associated with* — never *caused*. If you know one
thing was a factor among several, write *contributed to*. Reserve
*caused* for when you genuinely know the mechanism, not just the
pattern.
:::

::: example
**Weak evidence (a pattern only):** Higher humidity is associated with
more equipment failures in the warehouse.

**Stronger evidence (a known factor):** Poor ventilation likely
contributed to the recent equipment failures.

**Strongest evidence (a confirmed mechanism):** The power surge caused
the system to shut down.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="1-2 sentences" time="10 min"
Here's a scenario: *Sales at a shop dropped 15% the same month a
competitor opened nearby. No other information is available.* Write one
sentence describing this relationship using the correctly hedged causal
language for evidence this weak — do not write "caused."
:::

::: draft placeholder="The drop in sales is [associated with / may have been partly caused by] ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="contribute to" pos="phrasal verb" register="formal" meaning="to act as one of several factors that help cause something (অবদান রাখা)" collocation="contribute to a decline, contribute significantly, partly contribute to" example="Understaffing contributed to the backlog, though it was not the only cause."
:::

::: vocabulary word="associated with" pos="collocation" register="formal" meaning="occurring together in a pattern, without this alone proving one causes the other (সম্পর্কযুক্ত)" collocation="be associated with, strongly associated with, risks associated with" example="Late arrivals are associated with weekend shift changes."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The drop in sales is associated with the competitor's opening nearby,
though with only one month of data and no other information, it's not
possible to say the new competitor actually caused the decline.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you avoid the word "caused" given how weak this evidence is?
- Does your sentence use "associated with" or a similarly hedged phrase instead?
- Did you name what's missing (more data, other explanations) rather than just stating the weaker claim?
:::
