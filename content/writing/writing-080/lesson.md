## Introduction

A whole chapter, or a whole complex topic, can usually be read as a
structured answer to one central question. [Lesson 80](/lessons/80/)
taught you to find that question when reading. This task makes you
build the same structure yourself, from a topic you already know.

## Finding the Spine

::: concept
Pick a topic with several connected points you know well. Ask: what
single question are all of these points really answering together? Then
write that question, followed by a short, precise answer built from the
points — not a list of separate facts, but one coherent position.
:::

::: example
Points known: automation speeds up processing; automation requires
retraining; automation performance depends on data quality. Central
question: *Under what conditions does automation actually improve
performance?* Compressed answer: *Automation improves performance
mainly when paired with adequate staff retraining and reliable input
data — without both, its raw speed advantage often fails to translate
into better outcomes.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="1 question + 3-sentence answer" time="20 min"
Pick a topic you know well with at least three connected points (a
process, a technology, a policy). Write the single central question all
those points are really answering, then a three-sentence answer that
compresses them into one coherent position — not a separate list.
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Central question: What determines whether a new scheduling system
actually reduces missed shifts? Answer: A new scheduling system reduces
missed shifts mainly when staff receive proper training before rollout
and when the system integrates cleanly with existing payroll and
communication tools. Where either condition is missing, the system's
theoretical benefits rarely appear in practice, and missed shifts can
even increase temporarily during a poorly supported transition.
Ultimately, the technology itself matters less than the implementation
conditions around it.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your question genuinely capture what all your points are answering together, not just one of them?
- Does your three-sentence answer read as one coherent position, not a list of separate facts?
- Could someone who hadn't seen your original points still understand your compressed answer on its own?
:::
