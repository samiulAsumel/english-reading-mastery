## Introduction

[Lesson 72](/lessons/72/) taught you to take an argument apart into its
working pieces. This task reverses the direction: build a short argument
yourself, then label each piece so the structure is visible on the page,
not just in your head.

## The Four Parts

::: concept
**Premise** — a starting fact or assumption. **Evidence** — data or
observation supporting the argument. **Inference** — the step the
author takes from the evidence toward a claim. **Conclusion** — the
final position, which should follow from, and not overreach, the
evidence and inference above it.
:::

::: example
*Delayed shipments increase warehousing costs (premise). Data from the
last quarter shows a 12% rise in average delay time (evidence). This
suggests warehousing costs likely rose alongside it (inference).
Therefore, the terminal should prioritize reducing average delay time
(conclusion).* Each part does a distinct job — none of them repeats
another.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="15 min"
Write a four-sentence argument about a workplace or logistics topic of
your choice: one premise, one piece of evidence, one inference, one
conclusion — in that order. Then, on a new line, label each sentence:
"Premise:", "Evidence:", "Inference:", "Conclusion:".
:::

::: draft placeholder="Sentence 1 (premise) / Sentence 2 (evidence) / Sentence 3 (inference) / Sentence 4 (conclusion) / then labels"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="premise" pos="noun" meaning="a starting statement an argument is built on — যুক্তির ভিত্তি" example="The premise here is that delays increase costs."
:::

::: vocabulary word="infer" pos="verb" meaning="to draw a reasoned conclusion from evidence, without it being directly stated — অনুমান করা" example="From the rise in delays, we can infer that costs likely rose too."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Late deliveries damage a supplier's reputation with retail clients
(premise). Records show three of our five largest clients filed
complaints about late deliveries last quarter (evidence). This suggests
our reputation with major clients may already be affected (inference).
Therefore, the delivery process should be reviewed before client
contracts come up for renewal (conclusion). Premise: late deliveries
damage reputation. Evidence: three of five largest clients complained.
Inference: reputation with major clients may already be affected.
Conclusion: review the delivery process before contracts renew.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your evidence sentence contain an actual observation or data point, not just a restated premise?
- Does your inference sentence clearly move from the evidence toward a claim, using a word like "suggests" or "this indicates"?
- Does your conclusion follow from the inference without claiming something stronger than the evidence actually supports?
- Are all four labels correctly matched to the right sentence?
:::
