## Introduction

[Lesson 32](/lessons/32/) taught you to test a causal claim by imagining
the world where the supposed cause never happened. This task asks you to
build that test into your own writing: state a before/after result, then
write the exact counterfactual question that checks whether the cause
really did the work.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
State your before/after claim first, but avoid letting "after" silently
mean "because." Then write the counterfactual question directly: "What
would have happened to [outcome] if [the supposed cause] had never
happened?" If your starting point was unusually extreme (a very bad
month, a very low score), consider regression to the mean as an
honest alternative explanation — extreme results tend to drift back
toward normal on their own, cause or no cause.
:::

::: example
**Customer complaints fell after the call center introduced a new
training program.** **Counterfactual question:** would complaints have
fallen anyway, without the training, simply because the previous month's
complaint total was unusually high to begin with? **Honest note:**
if the "before" period was an unusual spike, regression to the mean
alone could explain part of the drop.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-110 words" time="20 min"
Write a paragraph describing a real or plausible before/after result you
know something about (a change at work, a personal habit, a team
outcome). State the result without letting "after" silently mean
"because." Then write the specific counterfactual question that would
test whether the supposed cause actually produced it, and note whether
regression to the mean could be part of the explanation.
:::

::: draft placeholder="After [change], [outcome] improved. / The real question is: would [outcome] have improved anyway if [change] had never happened, because .../ Since the 'before' period was unusually [bad/low], regression to the mean may also explain part of this."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="counterfactual" pos="adjective" register="technical" meaning="describing what would have happened in a world where the supposed cause did not occur (বাস্তবতা-বিরোধী)" collocation="counterfactual scenario, counterfactual question, counterfactual reasoning" example="The counterfactual question is what would have happened without the new system."
:::

::: vocabulary word="regression to the mean" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="the tendency for an unusually extreme result to drift back toward average on its own (গড়ের দিকে প্রত্যাবর্তন)" collocation="due to regression to the mean, explain by regression to the mean, regression to the mean effect" example="A record-bad month may improve simply through regression to the mean."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
After the terminal introduced a mandatory safety briefing before every
shift, the number of yard incidents fell for three straight months. The
real test is a counterfactual one: would incidents have fallen anyway,
without the briefings, simply because the month before the change had an
unusually high number of incidents to begin with? Since that earlier
month was a clear spike compared to the terminal's normal rate,
regression to the mean is a genuine alternative explanation the report
should not ignore before crediting the briefings alone.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you avoid letting "after" silently mean "because" in your first sentence?
- Did you write the specific counterfactual question, not just a vague doubt?
- Did you consider regression to the mean if your starting point was unusually extreme?
- Did you write this directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
