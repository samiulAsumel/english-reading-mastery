## Introduction

[Lesson 25](/lessons/25/) taught you to notice what a sentence puts
first, and why — the given information usually opens a sentence, and the
new, important information usually lands at the end or gets pushed into
the spotlight with a cleft structure. This task asks you to control that
spotlight yourself, on purpose, rather than just noticing it in someone
else's writing.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
The same fact can be written with different information in focus. A
cleft sentence — *It was X that...* — pulls one specific piece of
information into the spotlight. Passive voice — *X was done by Y* —
moves the receiver of an action to the front, pushing the doer toward
the end or out entirely.
:::

::: example
**Plain:** The new manager introduced the policy in March.

**Cleft, emphasizing who:** It was the new manager who introduced the
policy in March.

**Passive, emphasizing what:** The policy was introduced in March,
before most staff had been briefed on the change.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences" time="12 min"
Start from this plain sentence: *A software bug delayed the shipment by
three days.* Write it twice: once as a cleft sentence that puts the
spotlight on *the bug* as the cause, and once in passive voice that puts
the spotlight on *the shipment* as what was affected.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="emphasize" pos="verb" meaning="to give special importance to one part of a sentence or idea — গুরুত্ব দেওয়া" example="A cleft sentence lets a writer emphasize exactly which cause they mean."
:::

::: vocabulary word="spotlight" pos="noun" meaning="here, the part of a sentence the reader's attention is directed toward — মনোযোগের কেন্দ্র" example="Passive voice can put the spotlight on the result rather than on who caused it."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Cleft:** It was a software bug that delayed the shipment by three
days.

**Passive:** The shipment was delayed by three days after a software
bug was discovered in the tracking system.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your cleft sentence use "It was X that..." correctly, with X being the cause?
- Does your passive sentence put the shipment (the affected thing) at the front?
- Could you explain, in one sentence, what each version emphasizes differently?
:::
