## Introduction

[Lesson 34](/lessons/34/) taught you to spot the rhetorical structures
underneath an argument's surface — problem-solution, claim-support-
implication, concession-then-rebuttal — and to read an author's purpose
from their choices. This task asks you to build one of those structures
yourself, on purpose, and then explain the persuasive choices you made.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Build your passage as concession-then-rebuttal: open by fairly stating
the other side's real strength (the concession), then pivot with
"however" or "but" into your stronger, more confident point (the
rebuttal) — persuasive writing puts the stronger idea last, where it's
remembered. Choose your evaluative word choice deliberately: a word like
"struggles" reads more critically than "faces challenges," even when
describing the same fact. Then, separately, name your purpose (to
persuade, to recommend, to inform) and point to one specific word choice
that served it.
:::

::: example
**Passage:** "Critics fairly note that automated sorting systems cost
more upfront than hiring extra staff. However, the systems pay for
themselves within a year through fewer misrouted packages and lower
long-term labor costs." **Purpose note:** the purpose is to recommend
automated sorting; the word "fairly" in the concession signals respect
for the opposing view before the rebuttal claims the stronger ground.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-110 words" time="20 min"
Write a short passage using the concession-then-rebuttal structure on a
real or plausible topic (a tool, a policy, a method) you have an opinion
about. Fairly state the other side's real strength, then pivot with
"however" or "but" into your stronger point. Add a final sentence naming
your purpose (to persuade, to recommend, to inform) and pointing to one
specific word choice in your passage that served that purpose.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Critics fairly note that switching a whole terminal to electronic bills
of lading requires retraining every clerk who currently handles paper
documents by hand. However, the switch pays for itself within months,
since electronic records cut document-processing errors that once
delayed vessel departures by hours. My purpose here was to recommend the
switch, and the word "fairly" in my opening sentence was chosen
deliberately — it signals respect for the retraining concern before the
rebuttal claims the stronger ground.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your passage follow concession-then-rebuttal, with the stronger point placed last?
- Is your concession genuinely fair, not a weak point you immediately dismiss?
- Does your final sentence point to one specific word choice, not just a vague claim of persuasive intent?
- Did you write this directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
