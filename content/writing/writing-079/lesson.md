## Introduction

A paragraph rarely stands alone. [Lesson 79](/lessons/79/) taught you to
read what one paragraph receives from the one before it. This task
makes you build that connection yourself, across two short paragraphs
with two genuinely different jobs.

## What One Paragraph Gives the Next

::: concept
A well-connected second paragraph doesn't just continue the topic — it
explicitly picks something up: a term, a claim, an unanswered question,
a limitation. Common bridges: "This limitation, however, does not apply
to..."; "The same pattern appears in..."; "That question is answered
by..."
:::

::: example
Paragraph 1 ends: *...but this approach struggled with high-volume
periods.* Paragraph 2 opens: *This limitation prompted the terminal to
trial a hybrid scheduling model instead.* "This limitation" is a direct
reference back — paragraph 2 couldn't be understood on its own.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 short paragraphs (3-4 sentences each)" time="20 min"
Write two short paragraphs about a workplace process or change you know
well. Paragraph 1 should introduce a claim and end by naming a
limitation or open question. Paragraph 2 must open with an explicit
reference back to that limitation or question ("This limitation...",
"This raises the question of...") and then do a new job — resolving it,
extending it, or giving an example.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Paragraph 1 (claim, ends on a limitation) / Paragraph 2 (opens with an explicit reference back, then does a new job)"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="bridge" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a word or phrase that explicitly connects one paragraph to the idea just before it (সংযোগকারী শব্দ)" collocation="bridge the gap, a bridge between, bridge sentence" example="A bridge such as 'Cost is only half the picture' links the two paragraphs."
:::

::: vocabulary word="prompt" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to cause or lead to a following action or decision (উদ্বুদ্ধ করা)" collocation="prompt a response, prompt a review, prompted by" example="The complaint prompted a full review of the procedure."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Switching to self-checkout kiosks reduced average wait times during
regular hours by nearly a third. However, during peak evening hours,
the kiosks struggled to keep pace, and lines grew longer than under the
old staffed-counter system. This limitation prompted the store to keep
two staffed counters open during peak hours alongside the kiosks. The
combined approach has since kept wait times low across both quiet and
busy periods, without giving up the efficiency gains the kiosks provide
the rest of the day.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does paragraph 2 open with an explicit reference back to paragraph 1, not just a new, unrelated sentence?
- Does paragraph 2 do a genuinely different job (resolving, extending, exemplifying) rather than repeating paragraph 1?
- Could a reader tell, from the opening of paragraph 2 alone, exactly what it's responding to?
:::
