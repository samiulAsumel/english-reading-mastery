## Introduction

[Lesson 21](/lessons/21/) taught you to separate what a text explicitly
says from what it reasonably implies, and to hedge an inference with
words like *may*, *probably*, and *suggests* rather than stating it as
settled fact. Reading that boundary is one skill. Writing inside it —
producing your own hedged inference instead of just spotting someone
else's — is the harder, more useful twin skill, and it's what today's
task asks you to do.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
An explicit fact and an inference built on it belong in two separate
sentences, and the inference sentence needs a hedge word doing real
work: *may have*, *probably*, *this suggests*, *it's possible that*. If
your inference sentence reads exactly as confident as your fact
sentence, you've written the fact twice, not written an inference.
:::

::: example
**Fact:** The vessel's departure was delayed by six hours.
**Inference:** This delay may have been caused by congestion at the
berth — the sentence doesn't say so directly, but a six-hour delay is
consistent with a busy terminal waiting for space.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences" time="10 min"
Here is a short scenario: *A shipment scheduled for Tuesday arrived at
the warehouse on Thursday, and the delivery note listed "customs
inspection" as the reason.* Write exactly two sentences: one stating an
explicit fact directly taken from that scenario, and one drawing a
reasonable, properly hedged inference from it — about what the delay
might mean for the rest of the week's deliveries, for example. Do not
let your inference sentence claim more than the evidence supports.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="suggest" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="of evidence, to point toward a conclusion without proving it (ইঙ্গিত দেওয়া)" collocation="suggest that, strongly suggest, evidence suggests" example="The logs suggest that the outage began after the update."
:::

::: vocabulary word="presumably" pos="adverb" register="formal" meaning="probably true, based on reasonable expectation rather than direct proof (সম্ভবত)" collocation="presumably because, presumably not, and presumably" example="The clerk was absent, presumably because of the strike."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Fact:** The shipment arrived two days late, and the delivery note gave
"customs inspection" as the reason.

**Inference:** This suggests the shipment may have been held at customs
longer than usual, which could mean later deliveries this week face
similar delays — though the note doesn't say whether the inspection was
routine or exceptional.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Is your fact sentence stated with no hedge at all, exactly as given?
- Does your inference sentence contain a real hedge word (may, probably, suggests, could), not just a soft tone?
- Did you stop short of claiming something the scenario never mentioned — cost, blame, or a guarantee about future deliveries?
- Did you write both sentences directly in English, without composing them in Bangla first?
:::
