## Introduction

This lesson doesn't teach a new rule. It asks you to use every rule from
the last five lessons at the same time, in one short paragraph: correct
sentences, articles, time sequencing, description, and connectors. This
is exactly the skill that lets you eventually write something longer —
an email, a report, a message to a colleague — without stalling every
few words to translate the next sentence from Bangla.

## What Makes a Paragraph, Not Just Sentences

::: concept
A paragraph needs one more thing sentences alone don't have: a **topic
sentence** — usually the first sentence — that tells the reader what the
whole paragraph is about before the details arrive. Everything after it
should support that one idea. A paragraph about your morning routine
that suddenly mentions your favorite football team, with no connection
drawn, isn't one paragraph — it's two ideas sharing a page.
:::

::: framework
- Write one topic sentence that states what the paragraph is about
- Add 3-5 supporting sentences, connected with time words or and/but/because/so
- Use at least one description sentence (to be + adjective) and one article correctly
- Read it back once, checking only for a topic sentence and a clear connection between every pair of sentences
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="60-90 words" time="15 min"
Write one paragraph (4-6 sentences) describing a typical day at your
job or in your studies. Start with a topic sentence. Use at least one
time expression, one connector (and/but/because/so), and one description
sentence with "to be" plus an adjective.
:::

:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="topic sentence" pos="noun" meaning="the sentence, usually first, that states what a paragraph is about — মূল বাক্য" example="A clear topic sentence tells the reader what to expect."
:::

::: vocabulary word="support" pos="verb" meaning="to give more detail or evidence for a main idea — সমর্থন করা" example="Every sentence after the topic sentence should support it."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
My job as a port operations assistant involves several regular tasks
every day. First, I check the vessel arrival schedule as soon as I
arrive at the office. Then, I confirm the details with the shipping
agent, because the schedule sometimes changes overnight. The work is
demanding, but it is also genuinely interesting to me. By the end of the
day, I usually have a clear picture of every ship that arrived or
departed, so I can brief the next shift accurately.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does the first sentence work as a topic sentence — could a stranger guess what the paragraph is about from it alone?
- Is every following sentence connected to the one before it, using a time word or a connector?
- Did you include at least one correctly used article and one "to be + adjective" description?
- Read the whole paragraph aloud once — does it sound like one connected piece of writing, not five separate facts?
:::

This closes the Foundation writing module. The next level's writing
tasks build on exactly this paragraph — connecting several of them into
a short piece with more than one topic sentence.
