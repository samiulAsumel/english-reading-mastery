## Introduction

Bangla has no direct equivalent of *a*, *an*, and *the*, which is
exactly why articles are one of the most persistent small mistakes in
Bangla speakers' English writing — not because the rule is hard, but
because there's no habit in your first language to build on.
[Lesson 6](/lessons/6/) taught you to read the new-vs-known signal every
article carries; this task asks you to send that same signal yourself.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Use **a/an** the first time you introduce something the reader doesn't
know yet: "I saw **a** ship." Use **the** once it's already
identified — either because you mentioned it a moment ago, or because
context makes it obvious which one you mean: "**The** ship was already
loaded." *A* becomes *an* only when the next word starts with a vowel
**sound**, not a vowel letter: *an hour*, but *a university*.
:::

::: warning
Two traps worth watching for. First: **information**, **advice**, and
**research** are uncountable — never *informations* or *a research*; say
*some information* or *a piece of research* instead. Second: speaking
generally needs **no article** at all — "Ships carry cargo" (ships in
general), not "The ships carry cargo" (specific ships).
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="6 sentences" time="10 min"
Write six sentences describing a task at work, using at least one
article correctly in each. Include one sentence introducing something
new with *a/an*, a following sentence referring back to it with *the*,
and one sentence speaking generally with no article at all.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="specific" pos="adjective" meaning="one particular thing, not just any example of it — নির্দিষ্ট" example="'The report' means one specific report, not reports in general."
:::

::: vocabulary word="uncountable" pos="adjective" meaning="a noun with no plural form, treated as a mass rather than individual units — অগণনীয়" example="Information and advice are uncountable nouns."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
I received a report from the shipping agent this morning. The report
listed every container due to arrive this week. Ships carry cargo across
long distances every day. I needed some information before I could
confirm the numbers. The information turned out to be correct.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use "a/an" the first time something was mentioned, and "the" once it was already known?
- Did you leave out an article where the sentence speaks generally?
- Did you avoid treating information, advice, or research as countable nouns?
:::
