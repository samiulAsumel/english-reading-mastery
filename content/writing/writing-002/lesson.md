## Introduction

Bangla has no direct equivalent of *a*, *an*, and *the*. That's exactly
why articles are one of the most persistent small mistakes in Bangla
speakers' English writing — not because the rule is hard, but because
there's no habit in your first language to build on. The good news: the
rule really is small. Once it's automatic, it stays automatic.

## The Rule

::: concept
Use **a** or **an** the first time you mention something, when the
listener doesn't yet know which specific one you mean: "I saw **a**
dog." Use **the** once it's already been identified — either because you
mentioned it a moment ago, or because there's only one of it: "**The**
dog followed me home." *A* becomes *an* only when the next word starts
with a vowel **sound** (an hour, a university — listen to the sound, not
the spelling).
:::

There are also two common situations with **no article at all**:

- Plural or uncountable nouns speaking generally: "**Dogs** are loyal."
  Not "the dogs are loyal" (that would mean one specific group of dogs).
- Most countries, languages, and meals: "I speak **English**." Not "the
  English" (there are exceptions — *the* United States — but this is the
  general pattern).

::: warning
The most common mistake in this direction: dropping *the* where English
needs it because Bangla doesn't mark it at all — "I went to market"
instead of "I went to **the** market." Read your sentence back and ask:
*is there only one of this thing, or have I already mentioned it?* If
yes, it almost always needs *the*.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="specific" pos="adjective" meaning="one particular thing, not just any example of it — নির্দিষ্ট" example="'The car' means one specific car, not cars in general."
:::

::: vocabulary word="uncountable" pos="adjective" meaning="a noun with no plural form, treated as a mass, not individual units — অগণনীয়" example="Water, rice, and advice are uncountable nouns."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
I wake up early every day. I make a cup of tea before I do anything
else. The tea is usually still hot by the time I sit down to drink it.
Rice is part of almost every breakfast in my house. I catch the 8
o'clock bus to work. The bus is almost always on time.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you use "a/an" the first time something was mentioned, and "the" once it was already known?
- Did you leave out an article where the sentence speaks generally (plurals, most countries/languages)?
- Read one sentence aloud — does the article sound natural, or did you translate a Bangla sentence structure directly?
:::
