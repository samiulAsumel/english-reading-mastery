## Introduction

[Lesson 48](/lessons/48/) taught you to preview a chapter's title and
headings before reading its body, tracking the job each section does
toward one larger argument. A writer who plans the same way — headings
first, content second — produces a chapter that a previewing reader can
actually follow. This task has you build that outline from the writer's
side.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A chapter outline is not just a list of topics — it's a sequence of
jobs. One section might establish the problem, the next might present
evidence, and the last might argue for a solution. If you can't state, in
one sentence, what job a section does toward the chapter's larger point,
the section doesn't have a clear reason to exist yet.
:::

::: example
Title: "Why the Port Needs a Second Access Road." Section 1 — "The
Current Bottleneck": establishes the problem with data. Section 2 —
"What Other Ports Did": shows evidence that a second road solved a
similar problem elsewhere. Section 3 — "The Cost of Waiting": argues the
problem is getting worse, making the case urgent.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="1 title + 3 headings + 3 one-line jobs" time="12 min"
Pick a topic you could write a short chapter about — something from your
work, studies, or a subject you know well. Write a chapter title, then
three section headings. After each heading, write one sentence stating
the specific job that section does toward the chapter's overall
argument.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Title: ... / Section 1: [heading] — job: ... / Section 2: [heading] — job: ... / Section 3: [heading] — job: ..."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Title: "Why Our Onboarding Process Needs to Change." Section 1 — "New
Hires Are Leaving Too Fast": establishes the problem with turnover data.
Section 2 — "What the Exit Interviews Reveal": presents evidence pointing
to weak onboarding as the cause. Section 3 — "A Ninety-Day Plan":
proposes a specific solution built directly from that evidence.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each section have a genuinely different job, not three versions of the same point?
- Could a reader who only saw your title and headings guess your chapter's overall argument?
- Does the last section actually follow from the evidence in the earlier ones, rather than arriving out of nowhere?
:::
