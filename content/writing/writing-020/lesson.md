## Introduction

[Lesson 20](/lessons/20/) taught you to read a paragraph as one argument
— claim, reason, example, conclusion — held together by discourse markers
acting as signposts. Writing your own short paragraph with that same
architecture is the natural next step: instead of finding the structure,
you build it, sentence by sentence.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Open with a **claim** — one sentence stating your main point. Follow
with a **reason**, usually signposted by "because" or simply placed
right after the claim. Add a concrete **example**, signposted by "for
example" or "for instance." Close with a **conclusion**, signposted by
"therefore" or "as a result," that restates the claim's real-world
consequence. Four sentences, four clear jobs.
:::

::: example
**Claim:** Clear labeling reduces errors in a warehouse.
**Reason:** Workers can find and check items faster when labels are easy
to read.
**Example:** For example, color-coded shelf labels cut picking mistakes
noticeably at one distribution center.
**Conclusion:** Therefore, investing in clear labeling is a small change
with a real, measurable payoff.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="15 min"
Write a four-sentence paragraph about a habit that improves English
learning (for example, reading daily, or speaking without translating).
Sentence 1: a claim. Sentence 2: a reason, showing why the claim is
true. Sentence 3: a concrete example, signposted with "for example."
Sentence 4: a conclusion, signposted with "therefore" or "as a result."
:::

::: draft placeholder="Claim: ... / Reason (because...): ... / For example, ... / Therefore, ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="label" pos="noun" meaning="a piece of information attached to an item, identifying it — লেবেল" example="Clear labeling reduces errors in a warehouse."
:::

::: vocabulary word="payoff" pos="noun" meaning="a useful or beneficial result gained from an effort — লাভ/ফলাফল" example="Clear labeling is a small change with a real, measurable payoff."
:::

::: vocabulary word="distribution" pos="noun" meaning="the process of delivering goods to where they're needed — বিতরণ" example="Color-coded labels reduced picking mistakes at one distribution center."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Reading a little English every day builds vocabulary faster than
occasional long study sessions. Because the words appear in a natural,
meaningful context, they're easier to remember than a list learned by
heart. For example, a learner who reads one short news article daily
will meet the same useful words — "increase," "significant," "according
to" — again and again, in slightly different sentences each time.
Therefore, a short daily reading habit is a more reliable path to real
vocabulary growth than cramming.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your first sentence state one clear claim, with nothing else mixed into it?
- Does your reason sentence genuinely explain why the claim is true, not just restate it?
- Is your example concrete and specific, not another general statement?
- Does your conclusion sentence connect back to the original claim, signposted by "therefore" or "as a result"?
:::
