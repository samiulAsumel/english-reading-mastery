## Introduction

[Lesson 47](/lessons/47/) taught you to ask, for every sentence in a
paragraph, "why is the author telling me this here?" — context, claim,
evidence, qualification, or conclusion. This task asks you to build a
paragraph the same way an author does: write each sentence knowing
exactly what job it's doing, so a reader asking that same question gets a
clear answer every time.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A well-built paragraph rarely repeats a job. One sentence sets up
context. One states the claim. One gives evidence for it. One
qualifies or limits it. One concludes. Five sentences, five different
jobs — if two sentences are doing the same job, one of them is probably
unnecessary.
:::

::: example
"Container traffic at the port has grown every year since 2019.
[context] This growth is straining the current yard capacity. [claim]
Average dwell time for containers rose from 3 days to 5 days last year
alone. [evidence] The increase is smaller at the new automated terminal,
which suggests capacity, not demand, is the real bottleneck.
[qualification] Expanding yard space should therefore be the priority
over attracting more traffic. [conclusion]"
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="5 sentences" time="15 min"
Write a five-sentence paragraph about a topic you know well. Give each
sentence one job: context, claim, evidence, qualification, conclusion —
in that order. After each sentence, write its job in square brackets, the
way the example above does.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Context sentence [context] / Claim sentence [claim] / Evidence sentence [evidence] / Qualification sentence [qualification] / Conclusion sentence [conclusion]"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="qualification" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="a statement that limits or adjusts a claim so it isn't overstated (শর্তসাপেক্ষ মন্তব্য)" collocation="add a qualification, important qualification, qualification of" example="A short qualification limits the claim to peak season."
:::

::: vocabulary word="bottleneck" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the point in a process that limits the speed of the whole system (সংকীর্ণ বাধা)" collocation="a bottleneck in, cause a bottleneck, remove a bottleneck" example="The single scanner is the bottleneck limiting gate speed."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Employee turnover at the company has risen steadily since last year.
[context] This is starting to hurt the quality of customer service.
[claim] New hires now leave within six months on average, down from
eighteen months two years ago. [evidence] The drop is smallest in the
department with the strongest onboarding program, suggesting training
quality matters more than pay alone. [qualification] Improving onboarding
across every department should be the company's first priority.
[conclusion]
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each sentence genuinely do a different job, or do two sentences repeat the same one?
- Is your claim sentence a real claim (arguable), not just another fact?
- Does your qualification actually limit or complicate the claim, rather than just restating it?
- Does your conclusion follow logically from the evidence and qualification above it?
:::
