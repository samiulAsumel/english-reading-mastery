## Introduction

[Lesson 69](/lessons/69/) taught you that "this does not mean," "not
necessarily," and "does not imply" aren't there to say "no" — they're
there to block one specific, over-strong conclusion. This task asks you
to build that exact move yourself: claim, concede, block, refine.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
State an initial claim. Concede a real limiting point with "although"
or "while." Then use "this does not mean" to name, precisely, the
over-strong conclusion a reader might jump to — and block only that
one, not the original claim itself. Close with a refined, still-
positive position.
:::

::: example
*Automation has reduced processing errors significantly. This does not
mean human review is no longer necessary — unusual cases still require
judgment the system cannot yet replicate. The system has made human
review faster and more targeted, not obsolete.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-120 words" time="20 min"
Write a short paragraph about a real or realistic improvement or change
in your field. State the claim, concede one genuine limitation with
"although" or "while," then use "this does not mean" to block one
specific wrong conclusion a reader might draw — and close with a
refined position.
:::

::: draft placeholder="[Claim]. Although..., [limitation]. This does not mean... — [refined conclusion instead]."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="concede" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to admit a point is true, even while defending a larger claim (স্বীকার করা)" collocation="concede that, concede a point, willingly concede" example="The author concedes that the trial was small but argues the trend is clear."
:::

::: vocabulary word="over-generalise" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to draw a broader conclusion than the evidence actually supports (অতিসাধারণীকরণ করা)" collocation="tend to over-generalise, risk over-generalising, over-generalise from" example="Concluding that all ports are slow from one delay would over-generalise."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The new scheduling system has cut average delays by a third. Although
some routes still experience occasional bottlenecks during peak season,
the overall improvement has been consistent across most of the network.
This does not mean delays have been eliminated, or that the system
requires no further monitoring — it means the baseline has shifted, and
remaining delays now point to a smaller, more specific set of causes
worth investigating directly.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you concede a genuine limitation, not a token or throwaway one?
- Did "this does not mean" block one specific, named conclusion rather than vaguely softening the whole claim?
- Did you close with a refined position, not a flat reversal of your opening claim?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the paragraph in Bangla first?
:::
