## Introduction

[Lesson 39](/lessons/39/) taught you to resolve what "this," "such," and
"one" actually point to when reading. Writing that same toolkit is a
discipline in the opposite direction: deliberately choosing a pronoun,
demonstrative, or substitute word instead of repeating a noun — while
making sure every one of them still resolves clearly to exactly one
thing.

## The Toolkit, for Writing

::: concept
**Reference:** *it, this, that* point back to something already
mentioned. **Substitution:** *one/ones, do so* stand in for a repeated
noun or verb phrase. **Ellipsis:** drop a repeated word entirely when
it's recoverable from context, as in *some support the plan; others do
not [support the plan].*
:::

::: example
**Repetitive:** *The company introduced a new billing system. The new
billing system reduced errors. The new billing system also reduced
processing time.* **Cohesive:** *The company introduced a new billing
system. It reduced errors and processing time.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-120 words" time="20 min"
Write a short paragraph, 80-120 words, introducing one system, policy,
or idea in your first sentence, then writing 3-4 more sentences about it
without repeating its name. Use at least one pronoun/demonstrative
reference, one substitute word ("one" or "do so"), and one instance of
ellipsis (a dropped, recoverable word).
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
*A shipping line introduced an automated container-stacking system at
one terminal. It stacked containers faster on average than the manual
crews it replaced. This speed advantage, however, came with a cost: the
system struggled with damaged containers that the manual crews had
handled reliably. Some managers argued for expanding the automation
regardless; others did not, preferring to keep the older crews on
unusual cargo. The company eventually chose a hybrid approach — the
newer system for standard containers, the older one for anything
irregular.* Reference: *it, this speed advantage.* Substitution: *the
older one.* Ellipsis: *others did [not argue for expanding the
automation].*
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does the entity you introduced first stay clearly traceable through every later pronoun or substitute word?
- Did you use at least one substitute word ("one" or "do so") correctly, not just a pronoun?
- Does your ellipsis (dropped word) remain genuinely recoverable, not confusing?
- Would a reader ever need to ask "wait, what does 'it' refer to here"?
:::
