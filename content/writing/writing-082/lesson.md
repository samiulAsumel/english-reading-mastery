## Introduction

[Lesson 82](/lessons/82/) taught you to follow a single concept's
identity as its meaning expands, narrows, or gets reframed across a
book — and to notice the signal phrases ("more precisely," "not X but
Y") that mark the exact moment it happens. This task asks you to write
that tracking down cleanly, citing the evidence rather than just
asserting a change occurred.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A concept-tracking paragraph does three things: names the concept,
states what kind of change happened (expansion, narrowing, refinement,
or reframing), and quotes or closely paraphrases the exact words that
mark the shift. Skip the third part and the claim is just an assertion.
:::

::: example
"Efficiency first meant 'lower cost per container' in the opening
chapter, but by chapter three the author narrows it: 'efficiency, more
precisely, means lower cost per container without increasing turnaround
time' — the same word, a stricter definition." Notice the quoted phrase
is doing the proving.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="70-100 words" time="20 min"
A report first defines "risk" as "the chance a shipment is delayed."
Two paragraphs later it adds: "risk, more precisely, is the chance a
shipment is delayed *and* the delay causes a missed contractual
deadline — a delay with no financial consequence is not, in this
report's sense, a risk." Write a paragraph naming what type of change
this is (expansion, narrowing, refinement, or reframing) and quoting the
exact words that mark it.
:::

::: draft placeholder="The concept 'risk' undergoes.../ The signal phrase is.../ This means the report now excludes..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="narrow" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to make a concept's meaning stricter or more limited than before (সংকুচিত করা)" collocation="narrow the definition, narrow the meaning, narrow down" example="The author narrows 'delay' to mean lateness beyond two hours."
:::

::: vocabulary word="boundary" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="what a concept includes and excludes (সীমা)" collocation="conceptual boundary, clear boundary, boundary of" example="The boundary of 'risk' widens in the second chapter."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The concept "risk" is narrowed, not reframed — its core meaning (a
chance of delay) survives, but its boundary shrinks. The signal is "more
precisely," followed by an added condition: the delay must also cause a
missed contractual deadline. The report is explicit about what falls
outside the boundary now: "a delay with no financial consequence is
not... a risk." A reader who kept the chapter-one definition would
wrongly count harmless delays as risks the report no longer counts.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you correctly identify narrowing, not expansion or reframing?
- Did you quote or closely paraphrase the exact signal phrase?
- Did you explain what the narrower boundary now excludes?
- Did you avoid simply saying "the meaning changed" without evidence?
:::
