## Introduction

[Lesson 4](/lessons/4/) taught you to tell a reason clause from a time
clause, a condition clause, and a contrast clause by the small word that
introduces it. This task asks you to produce all four on purpose, so
that *because*, *when*, *if*, and *although* stop being labels you
recognize and become tools you reach for.

## Four Clauses, Four Jobs

::: concept
Each subordinating word commits you to a specific relationship: **because**
answers *why* — "I left early because the ferry was leaving." **when**
answers *at what point* — "I left when the ferry was leaving." **if**
states a condition — "I will leave if the ferry is on time." **although**
signals a surprising contrast — "Although the ferry was late, I still
made it." Swap one for another without checking the relationship, and
the sentence claims something you didn't mean.
:::

::: important
When the dependent clause comes first, it needs a comma before the main
clause: **Because the ferry was late, I missed my connection.** When the
main clause comes first, no comma is needed: **I missed my connection
because the ferry was late.**
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="12 min"
Write four sentences about a real event from your work or daily life:
one with *because*, one with *when*, one with *if*, and one with
*although*. Put the dependent clause first in at least two of them, and
punctuate the comma correctly.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Because..., I... / I... when... / If..., I will... / Although..., I still..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="dependent clause" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="a clause with its own subject and verb that cannot stand alone as a full sentence (অধীন খণ্ডবাক্য)" collocation="begin a dependent clause, dependent clause with because, attach a dependent clause" example="In 'Because the crane failed, the vessel waited', the first clause is a dependent clause."
:::

::: vocabulary word="condition" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="something that must be true before something else can happen (শর্ত)" collocation="meet a condition, on condition that, necessary condition" example="Payment is released on condition that the goods pass inspection."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Because the ferry was late, I missed my connection to the port office.

I usually check the schedule again when the weather turns bad.

If the delay continues, we will have to reroute the shipment.

Although the delay cost us an hour, the cargo still arrived undamaged.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each sentence use the connector that matches the relationship you actually meant, not just the closest-sounding one?
- Did you add a comma when the dependent clause came first, and leave it out when it came second?
- Could you point to the main clause and the dependent clause separately in each sentence?
:::
