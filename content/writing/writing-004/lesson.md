## Introduction

So far, every sentence in this module has described an *action* — what
someone does. Description is different: it describes what someone or
something *is*. English marks that difference clearly with the verb "to
be" (am/is/are), and mixing the two up is one of the fastest ways for a
sentence to sound foreign, even when every individual word is correct.

## "To Be" Plus Adjectives

::: concept
Use **am/is/are** to link a subject to a description: "She **is** tall."
"They **are** friendly." Never add another main verb after it for a
simple description — "She is have tall" is not a sentence English
recognizes, even though the individual words are all real English words.
The adjective (tall, friendly, busy) does the describing; "to be" just
connects it to the subject.
:::

When you use more than one adjective before a noun, English has a
loose but real order — opinion, then size, then age, then shape, then
color, then origin, then material, then purpose: "a **beautiful old
wooden** table," not "a wooden old beautiful table." You won't memorize
the whole list from one lesson — the useful habit for now is simpler:
put the adjective that shows your *opinion* first, and the adjective
closest to the noun's actual material or purpose last.

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="describe" pos="verb" meaning="to say what something or someone is like — বর্ণনা করা" example="Describe your workplace in three sentences."
:::

::: vocabulary word="reliable" pos="adjective" meaning="able to be trusted to do what is expected, consistently — নির্ভরযোগ্য" example="She is a reliable colleague; she is never late."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
My colleague is reliable and hardworking. Our office is small but busy
every morning. My laptop is old, but it is still fast enough for daily
work. The port is noisy during the day and quiet at night. My neighbor
is a friendly, talkative man. This tea cup is a simple white ceramic
cup, but I use it every single day.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did every description sentence use am/is/are, with no extra verb added after it?
- Did you put your opinion adjective first when you used two adjectives together?
- Is each description specific to the real person or thing, not a generic sentence?
:::
