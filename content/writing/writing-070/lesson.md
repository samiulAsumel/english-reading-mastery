## Introduction

[Lesson 70](/lessons/70/) taught you to look past a sentence's grammar
to find what an author actually wants you to notice — through cleft
sentences, "not only... but also," and explicit focus markers like
"what matters is." This task asks you to build that emphasis yourself,
deliberately.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
"Not only X but also Y" needs both sides grammatically parallel (same
structure on each side). A cleft sentence — "it is X that..." or "what
X does is..." — pulls one element into the spotlight that a plain
sentence would leave buried in the middle. Use "what matters is" to
name your real point directly.
:::

::: example
*The new system not only processes orders faster but also reduces
manual errors. It is the error reduction, not the speed, that has made
the biggest difference to customer satisfaction.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-120 words" time="20 min"
Write a short paragraph about a real or realistic improvement, change,
or comparison. Include one correctly parallel "not only... but also"
sentence and one cleft sentence ("it is X that..." or "what X does
is...") that spotlights the single most important point — placed
somewhere other than the paragraph's first sentence.
:::

::: draft placeholder="X not only [does A] but also [does B]. It is [the real point] that..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="parallel" pos="adjective" register="technical" meaning="matching in grammatical structure on both sides of a comparison (সমান্তরাল)" collocation="parallel structure, parallel form, in parallel" example="'Faster, cheaper, and safer' keeps the list parallel."
:::

::: vocabulary word="spotlight" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to deliberately draw attention to one specific element (আলোকপাত করা)" collocation="spotlight the issue, put a spotlight on, spotlight a point" example="The report spotlights safety by placing it in the first sentence."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The new inspection process not only catches more defects but also
completes each check in half the time. It is the speed, more than the
accuracy, that has changed how the floor operates day to day — workers
no longer wait on inspection results before moving to the next stage.
What matters most, for the team, is not the defect count on paper but
the fact that production no longer stalls waiting for a result.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Is your "not only... but also" sentence genuinely parallel on both sides?
- Does your cleft sentence spotlight one specific element, rather than restating the whole claim?
- Did you place your real point somewhere other than the very first sentence, on purpose?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the paragraph in Bangla first?
:::
