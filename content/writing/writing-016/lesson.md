## Introduction

[Lesson 16](/lessons/16/) taught you to split a claim into its source,
reporting verb, and content. Writing one yourself means making a real
decision the reading task never asked of you: exactly which reporting
verb fits how confident you actually are.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
Build the sentence in order: **Source + reporting verb + that + claim.**
Pick your reporting verb by strength — *show/demonstrate* for a strong,
well-supported claim; *argue/maintain* for a defended position;
*suggest/indicate* for a more cautious interpretation. Don't default to
"say" for everything — the verb you choose tells the reader how much
weight to give the claim.
:::

::: example
**Strong claim:** The inspection report shows that the crane passed
every safety check.
**Cautious claim:** The preliminary data suggest that the new schedule
reduces waiting time.
Same sentence shape, different reporting verb — and a different level of
confidence attached to each claim.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences" time="10 min"
Write two sentences about the same topic — the effect of regular
practice on English fluency. In the first, attribute a strong,
well-supported claim to a source using a strong reporting verb (show,
demonstrate, confirm). In the second, attribute a more cautious claim to
a different source using a cautious reporting verb (suggest, indicate).
:::

::: draft placeholder="Strong: [Source] shows/demonstrates that... / Cautious: [Source] suggests that..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="preliminary" pos="adjective" meaning="happening before the main or final part; not yet complete — প্রাথমিক" example="The preliminary data suggest that the new schedule reduces waiting time."
:::

::: vocabulary word="attribute" pos="verb" meaning="to say that something comes from a particular source or cause — আরোপ করা" example="The report attributes the delay to a shortage of staff."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Strong claim:** A recent study demonstrates that daily reading
significantly improves vocabulary retention.
**Cautious claim:** Some teachers suggest that shorter, more frequent
practice sessions may work better than one long session.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each sentence follow the order source + reporting verb + that + claim?
- Does your chosen reporting verb genuinely match how confident the claim is meant to sound?
- Is the "that" in each sentence introducing a complete claim, not describing a noun the way a relative clause would?
- Did you write both sentences directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
