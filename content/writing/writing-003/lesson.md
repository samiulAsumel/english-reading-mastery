## Introduction

[Lesson 3](/lessons/3/) taught you to find the head noun buried inside a
long noun phrase, and to read a helping verb plus a main verb as one
unit. Writing them is the reverse skill: build the phrase up around its
head, instead of stripping it back down to find one.

## Building Both Kinds of Phrase

::: concept
A noun phrase grows around its **head noun**: start with *strategies*,
then add a determiner and an adjective in front — *the effective
strategies* — and a prepositional phrase after — *the effective
strategies of the team*. The head noun (*strategies*) never changes; only
the material around it grows.

A verb phrase grows around its **main verb**: start with *developed*,
then add a helping verb in front — *has developed* — or a modal —
*should have developed*. The passive form adds *be* plus a past
participle: *the strategies were developed* — here the action is done
*to* the strategies, so no one performing the action needs to be named.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="12 min"
Write four sentences about a project or task you know well: one using a
noun phrase with at least one adjective before the head noun, one using
a verb phrase with a helping verb (*has/have + past participle*), one
using a modal + verb phrase (*can/should/must + verb*), and one using
the passive voice (*is/was + past participle*).
:::

::: draft placeholder="Noun phrase: The busy port handled... / Helping verb: The team has finished... / Modal: We should check... / Passive: The report was reviewed..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="head noun" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="the single core noun a whole noun phrase is built around (মূল বিশেষ্য)" collocation="identify the head noun, head noun of the phrase, single head noun" example="In 'the new cargo-handling equipment', 'equipment' is the head noun."
:::

::: vocabulary word="past participle" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="the verb form used after have or be, such as finished or written (অতীত কৃদন্ত)" collocation="form the past participle, past participle after have, regular past participle" example="'Approved' is the past participle in 'The plan has been approved'."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The busy port handled several large vessels this week.

The team has finished the monthly cargo report.

We should check the container numbers again before signing off.

The shipment was delayed by heavy weather offshore.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Can you point to the head noun in your noun-phrase sentence, and name every word attached to it?
- Does your helping-verb sentence use exactly have/has + past participle, not a mix of tenses?
- In your passive sentence, is the thing receiving the action the subject, with no need to name who did it?
:::
