## Introduction

[Lesson 29](/lessons/29/) taught you to read a causal claim critically —
checking for a mechanism, a confounding variable, and an alternative
explanation the author might be quietly skipping. This task asks you to
build that same honesty into your own writing: state your cause, explain
how it works, and admit what else could be going on.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A strong written causal claim has three parts: the cause and effect
themselves, the mechanism (how the cause actually produces the effect,
often signalled with "by + V-ing" or "through"), and at least one
alternative explanation or confounding variable you're willing to name
rather than hide. Prefer "largely responsible for" or "contributed to"
over an unqualified "caused," unless your evidence is genuinely that
strong.
:::

::: example
**Switching to LED lighting cut the warehouse's energy bill by a
quarter.** (bare causal claim) **The reduction happened by replacing
older bulbs that wasted most of their energy as heat.** (mechanism)
**However, the warehouse also reduced its operating hours that same
quarter, so shorter hours likely contributed to the savings as well.**
(confounding variable, named honestly)
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-110 words" time="20 min"
Write a paragraph describing a real or plausible cause-and-effect
situation you know something about (a change at work, in a routine, or
in a system you use). Include, in order: the causal claim, a sentence
naming the mechanism (how the cause produces the effect), and a final
sentence naming one honest alternative explanation or confounding
variable you cannot fully rule out. Use "largely responsible for" or
"contributed to" rather than an unqualified "caused."
:::

::: draft placeholder="[Change] was largely responsible for [effect]. / This worked by .../through .../ However, [other factor] also changed at the same time, so it may have contributed too."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="mechanism" pos="noun" meaning="the actual process by which a cause produces its effect, not just the fact that it does — প্রক্রিয়া/কার্যপদ্ধতি" example="The paragraph names the mechanism: fewer wasted-heat bulbs, not just 'LEDs help.'"
:::

::: vocabulary word="confounding variable" pos="noun phrase" meaning="a third factor changing alongside the claimed cause that could explain the effect instead — সহ-পরিবর্তনশীল কারণ" example="Shorter operating hours are a confounding variable the writer honestly admits."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Moving the port's container-booking system online was largely
responsible for the drop in queueing time at the gate. This worked by
letting trucking companies reserve arrival slots in advance, so fewer
drivers showed up during the same crowded morning window. However, the
terminal also added a second inspection lane that same month, so faster
inspections likely contributed to the shorter queues as well, and the
booking system alone cannot claim the full credit.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the mechanism — how the cause actually produces the effect — not just assert that it does?
- Did you honestly name one confounding variable or alternative explanation, rather than hiding it?
- Did you use "largely responsible for" or "contributed to" instead of an unqualified "caused"?
- Did you write this directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
