## Introduction

So far, most sentences in this module described an *action* — what
someone does. Description is different: it says what someone or
something *is*, and how strongly. [Lesson 9](/lessons/9/) showed you
that "extremely effective," "relatively effective," and "probably
effective" are three different claims, not three ways of saying the same
thing — so choosing the wrong degree or certainty word in your own
writing overstates or understates exactly what you mean.

## Degree, Then Certainty

::: concept
Use **am/is/are** to link a subject to a description: "The plan is
**effective**." Add a degree adverb to control its strength: "The plan
is **highly** effective" (strong) versus "The plan is **relatively**
effective" (effective *compared to* something else, not absolutely).
Add a certainty adverb to control how sure you sound: "This **probably**
caused the delay" claims far less than the flat "This caused the
delay."
:::

::: warning
Never add a second main verb after "to be" for a simple description —
"The plan is have effective" is not a sentence English recognizes, even
though every individual word is real English. The adjective does the
describing; "to be" just connects it to the subject.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="5 sentences" time="12 min"
Write five sentences about your work: two descriptions using "to be" +
adjective, with a degree adverb attached to at least one (*highly,
relatively, extremely*); two claims about a cause or result, each using
a certainty adverb (*probably, certainly, possibly*) that honestly
matches how sure you actually are; and one sentence stacking an adverb +
adjective + noun before a head noun.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="degree" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="how strong or weak a quality is (মাত্রা)" collocation="to a large degree, degree of certainty, degree of risk" example="The email states the degree of certainty clearly: 'almost certainly delayed'."
:::

::: vocabulary word="hedge" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to soften a claim on purpose, to avoid overstating certainty (সতর্কভাবে দাবি সীমিত করা)" collocation="hedge a claim, hedge carefully, hedge with 'may'" example="The analyst hedged the forecast by adding 'may' and 'in most cases'."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
My colleague is highly reliable and hardworking.

The delay was relatively short compared to last month's.

This probably caused the schedule change, though I haven't confirmed it
yet.

The system failure certainly affected today's shipment.

A highly experienced port manager reviewed the final report.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your degree adverb genuinely match how strong the quality really is, not just sound more impressive?
- Does your certainty adverb honestly reflect how sure you actually are, rather than overstating a guess as a fact?
- Did you avoid adding a second main verb after "to be" anywhere in your sentences?
:::
