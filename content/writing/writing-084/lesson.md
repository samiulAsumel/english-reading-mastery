## Introduction

[Lesson 84](/lessons/84/) taught you that evaluating an argument means
answering three separate questions — is the evidence reliable, does it
actually support the claim, and is the conclusion justified by it — not
one vague verdict of "good" or "bad." This task asks you to write that
three-level evaluation out, on a short made-up argument.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A real evaluation names all three levels even when the answer to one is
"yes" and another is "no." A conclusion can follow from reliable
evidence, or reliable evidence can still fail to support the specific
conclusion drawn from it — these are different failures, and a good
evaluation says which one occurred.
:::

::: example
"The survey of 40 port workers (reliable enough for a small pilot) shows
80% wanted flexible shifts. But the report concludes 'all port workers
prefer flexible shifts' — the evidence is reliable and does show a real
preference among the surveyed group, yet the conclusion overreaches:
40 workers at one port cannot justify a claim about all port workers
everywhere."
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-120 words" time="20 min"
A report surveys 25 dockworkers at one terminal and finds 70% support
switching to a four-day work week. It concludes: "the shipping industry
should adopt a four-day work week." Write an evaluation paragraph
addressing all three levels: is the evidence reliable (for what it is),
does it support the stated finding, and is the final conclusion
justified by it? Use calibrated language (demonstrates / suggests / does
not justify).
:::

::: draft placeholder="The evidence itself is.../ It does support the narrower claim that.../ But the conclusion that... is not justified because..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="justify" pos="verb" meaning="for evidence to be strong enough to reasonably support a specific conclusion — যুক্তিসঙ্গত করা" example="The small survey does not justify an industry-wide conclusion."
:::

::: vocabulary word="overreach" pos="verb" meaning="to claim more than the evidence actually supports — অতিরিক্ত দাবি করা" example="The report overreaches by generalizing from one terminal to the whole industry."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The survey evidence is reliable enough for what it is — a small,
specific sample at one terminal, honestly reported. It does support a
narrow finding: most surveyed dockworkers at that terminal favor a
four-day week. But the final conclusion — that the whole shipping
industry should adopt it — is not justified by this evidence. Twenty-five
workers at one terminal cannot represent an entire global industry with
different labor conditions, contracts, and operational needs. The report
overreaches: reliable evidence, real support for a narrow claim, but an
unjustified leap to a much broader one.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you address all three levels (evidence reliability, support, conclusion justification) separately?
- Did you avoid a flat "good/bad argument" verdict?
- Did you use calibrated language rather than absolute claims?
- Did you identify exactly where the argument overreaches?
:::
