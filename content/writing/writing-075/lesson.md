## Introduction

Not all evidence for the same claim is equally trustworthy. [Lesson
75](/lessons/75/) taught you to judge source quality, not just whether a
source exists. This task asks you to write two versions of the same
claim from different kinds of sources, then judge them yourself.

## Judging Source Quality

::: concept
Ask: who produced this evidence, and do they have a stake in the
result? Is it a direct measurement or someone's secondhand account? Is
it one isolated source, or does it converge with other, independent
evidence? A manufacturer's own performance claim and an independent
lab's test of the same product are not equally trustworthy, even if
they report the identical number.
:::

::: example
*The manufacturer's brochure states the new engine reduces fuel
consumption by 15%.* Versus: *An independent transport-agency test
measured a 9% reduction under real operating conditions.* The second is
more trustworthy — independent measurement, real conditions, no direct
stake in the product's success.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences + judgment" time="15 min"
Write the same claim about a product, policy, or process as it might be
reported by (1) the party that benefits from the claim being true, and
(2) an independent, disinterested source. Then write a judgment sentence
naming which is more trustworthy and the specific reason why.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="independent" pos="adjective" register="formal" meaning="not connected to, or benefiting from, the outcome being reported (স্বাধীন/নিরপেক্ষ)" collocation="independent source, independent review, independent of" example="An independent auditor confirmed the figures."
:::

::: vocabulary word="converge" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="when separate, independent pieces of evidence point to the same conclusion (একত্রিত হওয়া)" collocation="converge on, findings converge, evidence converges" example="Three independent studies converge on the same conclusion."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The software vendor's website claims the new system cuts processing
errors by 30%. An independent audit commissioned by three client
companies measured a 14% reduction over six months of real use.
Judgment: the independent audit is more trustworthy — the vendor
benefits directly from a higher number, while the audit was
commissioned by parties with no stake in inflating the result, and it
measured real use rather than a controlled demo.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did your two versions genuinely differ in who produced the evidence, not just in wording?
- Did your judgment name a concrete reason — independence, measurement conditions, stake in the outcome — not just "it seems more reliable"?
- Would a reader understand exactly what would make the evidence even stronger still?
:::
