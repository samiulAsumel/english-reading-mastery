## Introduction

[Lesson 65](/lessons/65/) taught you never to accept "better,"
"effective," or "successful" as complete on their own — always ask
better *at what*, effective *for what purpose*. This task asks you to
write an evaluation that names its criterion up front, rather than
leaving the reader to guess it.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
State the criterion first ("in terms of speed," "for reliability"),
then the comparative claim, then evidence that actually matches that
criterion — not evidence for a different dimension used to support an
unrelated claim. Close with one honest trade-off.
:::

::: example
*In terms of processing speed, the new system is clearly better: it
handles transactions in half the time. In terms of setup cost, however,
it is worse — the initial investment is nearly triple the old system's.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="80-120 words" time="20 min"
Compare two real or realistic options from your field (two tools, two
methods, two vendors). Name your criterion explicitly before judging,
support the claim with evidence matching that exact criterion, and
state one dimension where the "better" option is actually worse.
:::

::: draft placeholder="In terms of [criterion], X is better because... / However, in terms of [different criterion], X is worse because..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="criterion" pos="noun" register="formal" meaning="the specific standard used to judge something (মাপকাঠি)" collocation="evaluation criterion, meet the criterion, main criterion" example="Cost was the main criterion used to compare the bids."
:::

::: vocabulary word="trade-off" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="a disadvantage accepted in exchange for an advantage (বিনিময়ে ত্যাগ)" collocation="trade-off between, accept a trade-off, difficult trade-off" example="The cheaper option involves a trade-off in reliability."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
In terms of accuracy, the automated inspection system is clearly
better than manual checks: it catches nearly 98% of defects, compared
with roughly 85% for a trained human inspector. In terms of upfront
cost, however, it is worse — the equipment and calibration cost far
exceed a year of manual inspection wages. For a facility processing
high volumes daily, the accuracy gain likely justifies the cost; for a
smaller operation, it may not.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the criterion before making the comparative claim?
- Did your supporting evidence actually match the stated criterion, not a different one?
- Did you state one dimension where the "better" option is actually worse?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the paragraph in Bangla first?
:::
