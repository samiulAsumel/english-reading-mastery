## Introduction

[Lesson 1](/lessons/1/) taught you to find the Subject, the Verb, the
Object, and the Complement inside a sentence someone else wrote. This
task flips the direction: you build all three shapes yourself, from
nothing. If you can produce a Subject + Verb + Complement sentence on
purpose, and explain why the word after the verb is a Complement and not
an Object, you have actually learned the distinction — not just
recognised it on the page.

## The Three Shapes, for Writing

::: concept
**Subject + Verb** is already a complete sentence on its own: *Birds
fly.* Nothing is missing. **Subject + Verb + Object** adds a third piece
that receives the action: *Birds build nests* — *nests* is what gets
built. **Subject + Verb + Complement** looks similar but does a
different job: the word after the verb describes or renames the
Subject instead of receiving an action — *Birds are intelligent* — the
bird isn't doing anything *to* "intelligent"; "intelligent" is telling
you what the bird is like.
:::

::: example
**She works.** — Subject + Verb, complete on its own.

**She manages a team.** — Subject + Verb + Object: *a team* receives
the managing.

**She is confident.** — Subject + Verb + Complement: *confident*
describes *she*; it isn't something she's acting on.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="6 sentences" time="10 min"
Write six sentences about your own work or studies: two using only
Subject + Verb, two using Subject + Verb + Object, and two using Subject
+ Verb + Complement (with *am/is/are* or a similar linking verb like
*seem* or *become*). Label each one afterward with its shape.
:::

::: draft placeholder="1. I work. (S+V) / 2. I check the schedule. (S+V+O) / 3. I am careful. (S+V+C)"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="complement" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="a word that describes or renames the subject rather than receiving an action (পরিপূরক)" collocation="subject complement, adjective complement, complement of the verb" example="In 'The delay was unavoidable', the word 'unavoidable' is the complement that describes the subject."
:::

::: vocabulary word="linking verb" pos="noun" register="technical" meaning="a verb like is, seem, or become that connects a subject to a description of it (সংযোজক ক্রিয়া)" collocation="use a linking verb, linking verb such as, linking verb and complement" example="In 'The berth seems available', 'seems' is a linking verb joining 'berth' to its description."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
1. I work at a shipping office. (S+V)
2. Vessels arrive early in the morning. (S+V)
3. I check the arrival schedule. (S+V+O)
4. The team reviews every report. (S+V+O)
5. I am careful with the details. (S+V+C)
6. The schedule seems accurate today. (S+V+C)
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each Subject + Verb sentence stand on its own, with nothing missing?
- In each Object sentence, is the last word genuinely receiving the action, not just sitting near the verb?
- In each Complement sentence, can you explain why that word describes the Subject rather than being acted upon?
:::
