## Introduction

Before you can write a paragraph, you need to be able to write one
sentence correctly, without stopping to think about it. That sounds
small. It isn't — it's the whole foundation. A learner who translates
each sentence from Bangla before writing it in English will always write
slowly and often write something that sounds foreign, even when the
grammar is technically right. A learner who has *practiced* writing plain
English sentences about real things — their own life, their own day —
builds the habit of thinking directly in English sentence shapes.

That's what this task does. No essay, no paragraph yet. Just one
sentence at a time, about you.

## The Shape of a Simple Sentence

::: concept
Every simple English sentence needs three things, in this order: a
**subject** (who or what), a **verb** (the action), and often an
**object** (what receives the action). "I read books." Subject: *I*.
Verb: *read*. Object: *books*. That's a complete, correct sentence — and
almost every English sentence you'll ever write is built on this same
skeleton, no matter how long it eventually gets.
:::

The part that trips up most learners isn't the shape — it's the verb
form. English changes the verb slightly depending on who's doing the
action:

| Subject | Verb form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | base form | I **work** in an office. |
| he / she / it | base form + **-s** | She **works** in an office. |

That's the entire rule for present-simple sentences about facts and
habits. Miss the "-s" on *he/she/it* and the sentence is still
understandable, but it reads as a mistake a careful writer wouldn't make
— exactly the kind of small error that separates "understandable
English" from "accurate English."

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="5 sentences" time="8 min"
Write five simple sentences about yourself, one per line. Cover: your
job or your studies, one thing you do every day, one thing you like,
where you live, and one thing about your family. Use only present-simple
sentences — no translating a Bangla sentence first, just write directly
in English, even if it feels rough.
:::

::: draft placeholder="1. I work as a... / 2. Every morning I... / 3. I like... / 4. I live in... / 5. My family..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="habit" pos="noun" meaning="a thing you do regularly, almost without thinking — অভ্যাস" example="Reading before bed is a good habit."
:::

::: vocabulary word="routine" pos="noun" meaning="the usual order of things you do — নিয়মিত কাজের ধারা" example="Coffee is part of my morning routine."
:::

::: vocabulary word="fact" pos="noun" meaning="something true and provable, not an opinion — সত্য / তথ্য" example="Present-simple tense is usually used for facts and habits."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
1. I work as a port operations assistant in Chattogram.
2. Every morning I check the vessel schedule before I do anything else.
3. I like solving problems that other people find confusing.
4. I live with my family in a quiet part of the city.
5. My family always eats dinner together, no matter how busy the day was.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does every sentence have a subject, a verb, and (where needed) an object?
- Did you add "-s" to the verb wherever the subject was he/she/it (including "everyone," "my family")?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the sentence in Bangla first?
- Is each sentence about something specifically true for you, not a generic textbook sentence?
:::
