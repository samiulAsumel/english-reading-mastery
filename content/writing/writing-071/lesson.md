## Introduction

[Lesson 71](/lessons/71/) taught you to read an argument as it moves —
an author's claim rarely stays where it started. This task asks you to
build that same movement yourself: open broad, complicate it, and close
narrower and more precise than where you began.

## The Shape to Build

::: concept
Four moves, in order: (1) a broad opening claim, (2) "however" plus a
piece of evidence or a limitation, (3) a word like "therefore" or "in
particular" that narrows the claim, (4) a final position more precise
and conditional than the opening one. The reader should be able to
compare your first and last sentences and see exactly what changed.
:::

::: example
*Remote work can increase productivity. However, the effect varies
across occupations. Therefore, remote work is most effective where
tasks can be performed independently, with less benefit for roles that
depend on constant, real-time collaboration.* Opening claim: broad
benefit. Closing claim: benefit, but only under a stated condition.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4 sentences" time="15 min"
Write a four-sentence paragraph about container terminal automation.
Sentence 1: a broad, unqualified claim about what automation does.
Sentence 2: start with "However," and introduce a limitation or a case
where the claim doesn't fully hold. Sentence 3: start with "Therefore,"
or "In particular," and narrow the claim. Sentence 4: a final,
conditional position — more precise than sentence 1, not a repeat of it.
:::

::: draft placeholder="1. Broad claim about automation / 2. However, ... / 3. Therefore, ... / 4. Final refined position"
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="qualify" pos="verb" meaning="to limit or add a condition to a claim so it becomes more precise — শর্তযুক্ত করা" example="The second sentence qualifies the first claim rather than repeating it."
:::

::: vocabulary word="refine" pos="verb" meaning="to make a claim more precise and accurate as evidence accumulates — পরিমার্জন করা" example="By the final sentence, the claim has been refined into a conditional statement."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Automation can significantly reduce container handling times at busy
terminals. However, the improvement depends heavily on yard layout,
since poorly designed traffic flows can bottleneck even a fully
automated crane system. Therefore, automation delivers its full benefit
mainly at terminals that have already redesigned their yard flow to
support it. In terminals still using older layouts, the gain is real
but considerably smaller than the technology's full potential suggests.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your final sentence say something more precise than your opening sentence, not just repeat it in different words?
- Did "however" mark a genuine limitation, not just a restatement?
- Did "therefore" or "in particular" actually narrow the claim, rather than introduce an unrelated new idea?
- Could a reader compare sentence 1 and sentence 4 and name exactly what changed?
:::
