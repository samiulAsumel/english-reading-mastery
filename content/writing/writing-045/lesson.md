## Introduction

[Lesson 45](/lessons/45/) taught you to recognise fixed academic word
groups on sight — *contribute to*, *be consistent with*, *on the basis
of* — instead of decoding them one word at a time. Writing with these
chunks is a different discipline: you have to choose the exact
collocation whose strength matches what you actually mean, not just any
combination that sounds roughly academic.

## Choosing the Right Chunk, Not Just Any Chunk

::: concept
The causal-family verbs are not interchangeable: **contribute to**
signals partial causation, **result in** and **lead to** point directly
from cause to effect, and **be attributed to** credits a cause, often
softened with *partly*. Stance bundles work the same way: **be
consistent with** means *compatible with*, not *proof of* — swapping it
for **demonstrate** silently makes the claim far stronger than the
evidence supports.
:::

::: example
**Correct strength:** *Better staff training contributed to the
improvement, and the results are consistent with findings from earlier
studies.* **Overstated:** *Better staff training caused the improvement,
proving the earlier studies right.* The second version uses stronger
words than the evidence actually earns.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="100-150 words" time="20 min"
Write one paragraph (100-150 words) about an operational or business
topic. Use, correctly, at least one causal-family verb (contribute to,
result in, result from, give rise to, or lead to), one stance bundle (be
consistent with, be attributed to, or be associated with), and one
formal lexical bundle (on the basis of, with respect to, or the extent
to which). **Bold** each of the three chunks so their exact function is
checkable.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="give rise to" pos="phrase" meaning="a formal way of saying 'create' or 'cause' — সৃষ্টি করা" example="Rapid growth in cargo volume gave rise to new congestion problems at the yard."
:::

::: vocabulary word="be associated with" pos="phrase" meaning="to occur together with something, signalling correlation rather than proven causation — সম্পর্কযুক্ত হওয়া" example="Higher automation levels are associated with shorter processing times, though the link is not necessarily causal."
:::

::: vocabulary word="with respect to" pos="phrase" meaning="restricting a claim to one named dimension, equivalent to 'regarding' — সম্পর্কে" example="With respect to cost, the new vendor was clearly preferable."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
*The terminal adopted a new yard-management platform to improve
container retrieval. Faster retrieval times **contributed to** a
noticeable drop in truck queuing during peak hours. These results **are
consistent with** findings from similar terminals that made comparable
upgrades. **On the basis of** data collected over six months, managers
concluded that the platform, alongside recent staffing changes, was
responsible for most of the improvement.* Each bolded chunk carries a
specific, checkable strength: *contributed to* claims only partial
causation, *are consistent with* claims compatibility rather than proof,
and *on the basis of* names the evidence the conclusion actually rests
on.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your causal-family verb match the actual strength of the cause you mean (partial vs. direct)?
- Does your stance bundle avoid overstating the evidence (compatible with, not proven by)?
- Does your formal lexical bundle do real logical work, rather than sitting there for decoration?
- Could each bolded chunk be replaced by its plain-English equivalent without changing your paragraph's actual claim?
:::
