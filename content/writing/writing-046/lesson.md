## Introduction

[Lesson 46](/lessons/46/) taught you to read at two speeds: skim the
sentences that set the scene, and slow right down for the sentence that
actually carries a claim, a piece of evidence, or a qualification. Good
writers build that difference in on purpose — a reader who can't tell
which sentence matters most is a reader who has to slow down for
everything, which is exhausting and unclear. This task asks you to write
that difference in deliberately.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
A paragraph should contain some sentences that exist purely to set up
context (who, when, where — read fast, low information density) and
exactly one sentence that carries the actual point (the claim, the
number, the decision — read slow, high information density). If every
sentence in your paragraph carries equal weight, the reader has no way to
know what mattered.
:::

::: example
"The port had been expanding its container yard for two years, adding
new cranes and widening the access road. **Even with all of that
investment, average vessel turnaround time increased by 12 percent.**"
The first sentence is fast background. The bolded sentence is the one a
reader must slow down for — it's the actual claim, and it's surprising
given the context just given.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="4-5 sentences" time="12 min"
Write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about something you know well —
your job, your studies, or a daily routine. Three or four sentences
should be plain context, written fast and simply. Exactly one sentence
should carry the real claim — a result, a problem, or a decision — and it
should be noticeably more information-dense than the rest. After the
paragraph, add one line: "Slow-down sentence: [copy it here]."
:::

::: draft placeholder="Context sentence 1 / Context sentence 2 / THE CLAIM sentence / Slow-down sentence: ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="density" pos="noun" meaning="how much information is packed into a small space — ঘনত্ব" example="A claim sentence usually has higher information density than a context sentence."
:::

::: vocabulary word="surprising" pos="adjective" meaning="unexpected given what came before — অপ্রত্যাশিত" example="The result was surprising because the port had just invested in new equipment."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
The port had been expanding its container yard for two years, adding new
cranes and widening the access road. Traffic through the gate had also
grown steadily over the same period. Management expected turnaround
times to improve as a result. **Instead, average vessel turnaround time
increased by 12 percent, driven mainly by congestion at the new access
road during peak hours.** Slow-down sentence: "Instead, average vessel
turnaround time increased by 12 percent, driven mainly by congestion at
the new access road during peak hours."
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Are three or four of your sentences genuinely fast, plain context — no hidden claims buried in them?
- Does exactly one sentence carry the real point, and is it noticeably denser than the rest?
- Did you correctly identify your own slow-down sentence, or did you accidentally bury the claim somewhere else?
- Is the claim sentence surprising or important given the context you set up — not just more of the same?
:::
