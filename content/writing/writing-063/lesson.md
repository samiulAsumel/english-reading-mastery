## Introduction

[Lesson 63](/lessons/63/) taught you never to stop at "what is the
solution?" — but to trace the full chain: problem, root cause, solution,
outcome, and trade-off. This task asks you to build that chain yourself,
choosing your solution-strength verb carefully rather than defaulting
to "solved."

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
State the symptom, then the root cause behind it (not just the
symptom restated). Name the solution and pick your verb honestly:
"eliminated" only if the problem is fully gone, "reduced" or
"mitigated" if it's smaller but still present. Then state one real
trade-off — cost, speed, or complexity the solution introduced.
:::

::: example
*Late deliveries were traced not to traffic, but to an outdated route-
planning system. A new routing algorithm reduced average delivery delay
by 30%, though it also increased fuel costs, since routes now
prioritised time over distance.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="100-150 words" time="20 min"
Describe a real or realistic problem in your field, its root cause (not
just its symptom), the solution applied, and one honest trade-off. Use
a solution-strength verb (reduced, mitigated, eliminated, prevented)
that matches what actually happened — don't default to "solved" unless
the problem is genuinely gone.
:::

::: draft placeholder="The problem was... / The root cause was... / The solution... reduced/mitigated/eliminated... / However, this introduced..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="root cause" pos="noun phrase" register="technical" meaning="the underlying reason behind a problem, as opposed to its visible symptom (মূল কারণ)" collocation="root cause analysis, identify the root cause, underlying root cause" example="The root cause of the delays was a shortage of trained operators."
:::

::: vocabulary word="mitigate" pos="verb" register="formal" meaning="to make a problem less severe, without necessarily removing it (প্রশমিত করা)" collocation="mitigate the risk, mitigate the impact, partly mitigate" example="A backup generator mitigates the risk of a power failure."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Frequent stockouts at the warehouse were traced not to demand
forecasting, but to a slow manual reorder process that left gaps
between running low and placing a new order. An automated reorder
system reduced stockouts by roughly 40%, since orders now triggered the
moment stock crossed a set threshold. This did not eliminate the
problem entirely — unusually large, unpredictable orders still slip
through occasionally — and it introduced a new trade-off: the warehouse
now carries slightly higher average stock levels, which increases
storage cost.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name the root cause separately from the visible symptom?
- Did your solution-strength verb (reduced/mitigated/eliminated) match what actually happened, not just what was intended?
- Did you state one honest trade-off rather than presenting the solution as free?
- Did you write directly in English, without composing the paragraph in Bangla first?
:::
