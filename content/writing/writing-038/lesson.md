## Introduction

[Lesson 38](/lessons/38/) taught you to read a connector as a precise
logical signal, not a decoration — *however* is not *nevertheless*, and
*because* is not *therefore*. Writing is where that precision actually
gets tested: a reader can't ask you which relationship you meant, so the
connector you choose has to carry the exact weight on its own.

## Choosing, Not Reaching

::: concept
Before writing a connector, name the relationship first: is the next
sentence adding, contrasting, qualifying, or concluding? Only then pick
the word from that family that fits — and run the **substitution test**:
if a same-family synonym keeps the meaning intact, you chose correctly.
:::

::: example
**Vague choice:** *Automation reduces processing time. But it increases
setup costs.* **Precise choice:** *Automation reduces processing time.
Nevertheless, the company adopted it, judging the setup costs
worthwhile.* — *nevertheless* signals concession-plus-persistence,
which *but* alone doesn't carry.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="100-150 words" time="20 min"
Write a short paragraph, 100-150 words, that makes a claim, then moves
through a contrast, a qualification, and a conclusion — using one
connector for each of those three moves (three connectors total,
minimum). Before submitting, underline each connector and, next to it in
your draft, name the exact relationship it's carrying (contrast,
qualification, conclusion).
:::

::: draft placeholder="Claim: ... / Contrast connector + sentence: ... / Qualification connector + sentence: ... / Conclusion connector + sentence: ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="qualify" pos="verb" meaning="to limit or add a condition to a claim, making it more precise — সীমিত/শর্তসাপেক্ষ করা" example="'To some extent' qualifies an otherwise broad claim."
:::

::: vocabulary word="concession" pos="noun" meaning="acknowledging a point against your position before continuing to hold it — ছাড়/স্বীকৃতি" example="'Nevertheless' signals concession plus persistence, not agreement."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
*Automated gate systems can substantially reduce truck processing time
at a container terminal. However, faster processing does not
automatically mean lower overall congestion, since arrivals remain
unevenly distributed across each shift. In many cases, the technology's
real benefit depends on whether it is paired with staggered arrival
scheduling rather than deployed on its own. Overall, therefore, a
terminal considering automation should evaluate it alongside scheduling
reform, not as a replacement for it.* Connectors: *however* (contrast),
*in many cases* (qualification), *overall, therefore* (conclusion).
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Did you name, in your draft, the exact relationship each connector is carrying?
- Does the removal test confirm each connector is doing real logical work (does the relationship go unclear without it)?
- Did you avoid reaching for "but" or "and" when a more precise connector was available?
- Is the paragraph's overall claim → contrast → qualification → conclusion structure clear on one read?
:::
