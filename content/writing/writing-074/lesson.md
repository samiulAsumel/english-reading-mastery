## Introduction

[Lesson 74](/lessons/74/) taught you that the real question is never
just "is there evidence?" but "is this evidence enough?" This task
makes you write both a claim and an honest verdict on how well its
evidence actually holds up.

## Judging Sufficiency

::: concept
Ask: how relevant is the evidence to this exact conclusion? How large or
representative is it? How reliable is the source? Does it rule out
obvious alternative explanations? A conclusion should only be as
confident as its evidence earns — "some evidence exists" is not the same
as "enough evidence exists."
:::

::: example
*Three customers complained about slow delivery this month. Therefore,
our delivery service has gotten worse.* Judgment: weak. Three complaints
out of an unknown total number of deliveries is a small, unrepresentative
sample — it shows a problem exists for some customers, not that the
whole service declined.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="3 sentences" time="15 min"
Write one sentence of evidence and one "therefore" conclusion about a
workplace topic. Then write a third sentence judging the evidence:
sufficient, weak, or mixed — and name one specific thing (a larger
sample, a comparison, a longer time period) that would make it stronger.
:::

::: draft placeholder="1. Evidence / 2. Therefore, conclusion / 3. Judgment: sufficient/weak/mixed, because... What would strengthen it: ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="sufficient" pos="adjective" meaning="enough to properly support a conclusion — পর্যাপ্ত" example="Three complaints alone are not sufficient evidence of a system-wide decline."
:::

::: vocabulary word="representative" pos="adjective" meaning="accurately reflecting the larger group it's drawn from — প্রতিনিধিত্বমূলক" example="A sample of three customers may not be representative of all customers."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Two of our twelve regional warehouses reported stock shortages last
month. Therefore, our inventory system needs a full redesign. Judgment:
weak — two out of twelve warehouses is a small fraction, and nothing
here shows whether those two share a specific cause, like a local
supplier issue, rather than a flaw in the inventory system itself. What
would strengthen it: data showing shortages are spreading across more
warehouses, or ruling out a local, warehouse-specific cause for those two.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your judgment go beyond "yes" or "no" and give a real reason?
- Did you name a specific way the evidence could be strengthened, not just "more evidence would help"?
- Is your conclusion's confidence actually matched to how strong the evidence is?
:::
