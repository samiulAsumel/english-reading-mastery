## Introduction

[Lesson 77](/lessons/77/) taught you to separate a text's reasoning
layers — what's observed, what's inferred, and what's merely judged.
This task makes you build all three on purpose, about the same
situation, so the difference becomes something you can produce, not
just recognize.

## The Three Layers

::: concept
**Fact** — something directly observable or verifiable, with no
judgment attached. **Interpretation** — a reasoned reading of what the
fact might mean, still open to reasonable disagreement. **Opinion** — a
value judgment about whether something is good, bad, or desirable, which
another reasonable person could hold differently.
:::

::: example
Fact: *The warehouse processed 200 fewer orders this week than last
week.* Interpretation: *This drop is likely connected to the two staff
members who were on leave.* Opinion: *Management should have arranged
temporary cover, because customer service matters more than short-term
staffing costs.*
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="3 sentences" time="15 min"
Pick one real situation from your work or studies. Write one sentence
that is purely a fact (no judgment, nothing inferred), one sentence that
is a reasonable interpretation of that fact, and one sentence that is a
genuine opinion about it. Label each sentence.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Fact: ... / Interpretation: ... / Opinion: ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="verifiable" pos="adjective" meaning="able to be checked and confirmed as true or false — যাচাইযোগ্য" example="The number of orders processed is verifiable from the system logs."
:::

::: vocabulary word="value judgment" pos="noun phrase" meaning="a statement about whether something is good, bad, right, or wrong — মূল্যবোধভিত্তিক রায়" example="Whether management 'should have' acted differently is a value judgment, not a fact."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Fact: The delivery app's average rating dropped from 4.6 to 4.1 over
three months. Interpretation: The drop likely reflects the slower
delivery times introduced when the courier partnership changed.
Opinion: The company was wrong to switch courier partners without
first testing the new provider's reliability, since customer trust is
harder to win back than it is to lose.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Is the fact sentence truly free of any inference or judgment?
- Does the interpretation sentence stay reasonable and connected to the fact, rather than jumping to a value judgment?
- Does the opinion sentence clearly state a position another reasonable person could disagree with?
:::
