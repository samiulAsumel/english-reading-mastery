## Introduction

[Lesson 36](/lessons/36/) taught you to resolve a word's meaning from the
context around it — the same word, *pressure* or *issue* or *model*,
meaning something different depending on its neighbors. Writing is the
reverse skill: instead of decoding context someone else built, you build
it yourself, precisely enough that a reader can't land on the wrong
meaning.

## Building Context on Purpose

::: concept
To make a word's meaning unmistakable, give it a **collocation** (a
natural word partner) or a **context clue** — a definition, a contrast,
or an example — rather than leaving the word to stand alone.
:::

::: example
**Weak (ambiguous):** *The report raised an issue.* (problem? topic?
edition?) **Strong (resolved):** *The report raised a billing issue that
the finance team had not previously flagged* — *billing* and *flagged*
now make "problem" the only possible reading.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="two short passages, 2-3 sentences each" time="20 min"
Choose one word with at least two genuinely different meanings — for
example *bank*, *model*, *issue*, *capacity*, or *bias*. Write two short
passages, 2-3 sentences each, using the word once in each passage with a
clearly different meaning. In each passage, include at least one context
clue (definition, contrast, or example) that makes your intended meaning
unmistakable — don't just rely on the reader guessing correctly.
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="collocation" pos="noun" meaning="a natural, frequent word pairing whose meaning is learned as a whole chunk — স্বাভাবিক শব্দ-সংযোগ" example="'Strong evidence' is a collocation, not just 'strong' plus 'evidence.'"
:::

::: vocabulary word="unambiguous" pos="adjective" meaning="having only one possible, clearly intended meaning — দ্ব্যর্থহীন" example="Adding 'financial institution' makes 'bank' unambiguous in that sentence."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Passage 1 (financial):** *The bank raised its interest rate on
savings accounts last month. Customers with fixed deposits noticed the
change first.* **Passage 2 (riverside):** *The fishermen moored their
boats along the bank of the river before sunset. Years of flooding had
worn the bank down to a steep, muddy slope.* "Interest rate" and
"savings accounts" fix the first meaning; "moored their boats" and
"river" fix the second — neither passage needs the reader to guess.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does each passage make its intended meaning of the word clear from context alone, without stating "I mean the financial sense"?
- Did you use a genuine context clue (definition, contrast, or example), not just hope the reader infers correctly?
- Would a reader who only saw one passage, not both, still land on the correct meaning?
- Did you write both passages directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
