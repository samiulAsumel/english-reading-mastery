## Introduction

[Lesson 19](/lessons/19/) taught you to spot matching grammatical forms
as a signal that several ideas are meant to be read as equal and
related. Writing your own parallel list means building that matching
shape on purpose — and getting a comparison's exact meaning right is
just as much a writing skill as a reading one.

## The Rule, for Writing

::: concept
When you list three items joined by "and," give all three the same
grammatical form — three gerunds, three base verbs, or three adjectives,
never a mix. For comparisons, "more X than" and "as X as" each make one
precise claim; adding "not" in front of "as...as" reverses the whole
comparison, so check that word placement carefully before you finish the
sentence.
:::

::: example
**Parallel list:** A good supervisor plans the shift, checks the
equipment, and briefs the team.
**Comparison:** The new route is not as expensive as the old route.
(Meaning: new route costs less — not the same as "as expensive as,"
which would mean equal cost.)
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences" time="10 min"
Write one sentence listing three duties or activities in matching
grammatical form (all gerunds or all base verbs). Then write one
comparison sentence about two things you know well (two jobs, two
routes, two methods), using either "more...than" or "not as...as" — make
sure the meaning is exactly what you intend.
:::

::: draft placeholder="Parallel list: ...ing, ...ing, and ...ing / Comparison: ... is (not) as ... as ..."
:::

## Vocabulary in Context

::: vocabulary word="brief" pos="verb" register="neutral" meaning="to give someone essential information before a task begins (সংক্ষিপ্ত নির্দেশনা দেওয়া)" collocation="brief the team, brief on, thoroughly brief" example="The supervisor briefs the crew on safety rules before every shift."
:::

::: vocabulary word="equipment" pos="noun" register="neutral" meaning="the tools or machines needed for a particular task (সরঞ্জাম)" collocation="safety equipment, heavy equipment, piece of equipment" example="All protective equipment must be inspected before use."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
**Parallel list:** Learning a language well requires reading regularly,
listening carefully, and speaking without fear of mistakes.
**Comparison:** My current job is not as stressful as my previous one.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Do all three items in your list share exactly the same grammatical form?
- Does your comparison sentence claim precisely what you intended — check "as...as" against "not as...as" carefully?
- Did you avoid slipping in an extra meaning (like "better") that your comparison sentence doesn't actually state?
- Did you write both sentences directly in English, without translating from Bangla first?
:::
