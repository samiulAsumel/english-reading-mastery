## Introduction

Every argument leans on something it never says out loud. [Lesson
73](/lessons/73/) taught you to find that hidden assumption when reading.
This task asks you to build one on purpose, then expose it yourself —
proof you understand exactly where the gap sits.

## The Gap and the Bridge

::: concept
Evidence alone rarely reaches a conclusion — an unstated assumption
usually does the bridging. *Sales rose after the new packaging launched.
Therefore, the new packaging caused the increase* only holds if you
assume nothing else changed at the same time — no price cut, no
seasonal demand shift, no competitor closure. That assumption is the
gap, even though the sentence never mentions it.
:::

::: example
*Employee satisfaction scores rose after the new office layout was
installed. Therefore, the new layout improved satisfaction.* Hidden
assumption: nothing else affecting satisfaction changed during the same
period. Is it reasonable? Only if the timing rules out other likely
causes, like a recent pay increase.
:::

## Your Turn

::: prompt words="2 sentences + 1 assumption statement" time="15 min"
Write a two-sentence argument (evidence, then a "therefore" conclusion)
about a workplace change you're familiar with — a new process, tool, or
policy — where the conclusion assumes something the evidence never
actually proves. Then write one more sentence starting "Hidden
assumption:" naming exactly what's being assumed, and one sentence
judging whether it's reasonable.
:::

::: draft placeholder="1. Evidence sentence / 2. Therefore, conclusion / 3. Hidden assumption: ... / 4. Is it reasonable? Because..."
:::

## Model Answer

::: model-answer
Customer complaints dropped after the support team switched to a new
ticketing system. Therefore, the new ticketing system reduced
complaints. Hidden assumption: no other change — such as extra staff
being hired, or a seasonal drop in order volume — happened at the same
time. Is it reasonable? Only partly; the timing is suggestive, but
without checking staffing levels and order volume for that period, the
new system can't be credited with the drop on its own.
:::

## Self-Check

::: rubric
- Does your conclusion genuinely go beyond what the evidence alone proves?
- Did you name a specific, concrete hidden assumption — not a vague "other factors might exist"?
- Did you actually judge whether the assumption is reasonable, rather than just naming it?
:::
